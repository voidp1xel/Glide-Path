## 1. Atmosphere

### Composition and characteristics (p.1–3)
- Atmosphere = envelope of a **homogeneous mixture of gases called air**, held to the earth by **gravitation**; it moves with the earth at the same speed and direction.
- About **480 km thick**, but **~80% of it lies within 16 km**. There is no exact top: it thins out and merges with space.
- Characteristics: has **weight → exerts pressure**; **compressible and expandable**; occupies space, **no definite shape**; mobile (transfers heat and moisture); **poor conductor of heat and electricity**.
- Constituents by volume:

| Gas | % by volume |
|---|---|
| Nitrogen (N₂) | **78.09%** |
| Oxygen (O₂) | **20.95%** |
| Argon | **0.93%** |
| Carbon dioxide (CO₂) | **0.035%** (≈0.03%) |
| Traces | neon, helium, methane, krypton, xenon, nitrous oxide, iodine, H₂, ozone (0.000007%), ammonia, CO, SO₂, NO₂ |

- Plus **water vapour** and **solid particles**.
- N₂ + O₂ ≈ **99%** of air. Ratio **N₂ : O₂ = 4 : 1 by volume**, **3 : 1 by weight** (so O₂ : N₂ = 1 : 4 by volume, 1 : 3 by weight).
- Gases reduce rapidly with height: **supplementary oxygen needed above 10,000 ft**.
- Composition nearly uniform (except ozone) up to **80 km** = **Homosphere** (book attributes this to earth's gravitation); above 80 km = **Heterosphere**.

### Variable gases and greenhouse gases (p.2)
- **Variable gases**: water vapour, CO, SO₂, NO₂, methane — more over industrial areas, cities, water bodies; tiny in amount but very significant for weather and life.
- **Greenhouse gases**: WV, CO₂, O₃, methane — **transparent to solar (short-wave)** radiation but **partly absorb terrestrial (long-wave)** radiation and re-radiate it → keep the earth **warmer**. Excess → global warming.

### Water vapour, dry and saturated air (p.2)
- Water exists as solid (snow, ice, hail), liquid (drizzle, rain, shower), gas (WV).
- WV concentration **falls rapidly with height**; depends mainly on **temperature** (warmer air holds more).
- Tropics (23½°N–23½°S): air can hold up to **4% WV by volume**. WV negligible at the **poles** and **above 30,000 ft**.
- With 4% WV air is **saturated, RH 100%**. RH < 100% = unsaturated = "**dry air**".

### CO₂, ozone and particles (p.3)
- **CO₂**: from burning fuel/wood; high in industrial areas, negligible over poles and at height; large amounts dissolved in oceans; plants absorb CO₂, release O₂.
- **Ozone (O₃)**: forms in the upper atmosphere, **absorbs UV** and raises temperature; protects from skin cancer, sunburn, cataracts.
- Appreciable O₃ between **10 and 50 km**, **maximum concentration at 20–25 km** (ozone layer ≈ 25 km).
- **Ozone hole**: thinning, mainly over polar regions, caused by pollution and halocarbons/CFCs (refrigerants, solvents) that release halogens in the stratosphere by photodissociation.
- **Particles** (sea salt, dust, industrial): obscure sunlight, reduce visibility, affect temperature, act as **condensation nuclei** → premature condensation; frequent fog/**smog** (fog + smoke) in cities.

### Vertical distribution of air mass (p.3)

| Below | Fraction of the atmosphere's mass |
|---|---|
| 6 km (~18,000–20,000 ft) | **½** |
| 10 km | **¾** |
| 35 km | **99%** |

### How the atmosphere is heated (p.4)
- Sun heats the **earth**, earth heats the **atmosphere** (conduction, convection, radiation, latent heat of condensation) → **atmosphere is heated from below**, so temperature falls with height (in the troposphere).
- Heat flow surface → atmosphere: **sensible heat 23%** (conduction, convection, radiation); **latent heat 77%** (evaporation, condensation, sublimation) → **latent heat is the biggest heat transfer**.

### Layers (thermal structure) (p.4–7)

| Layer | Height | Temperature trend | Key facts |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Troposphere** | Surface to **16–18 km at equator**, **8–10 km at poles** | Falls ~**6.5°C/km** | 75% of mass, 99% of WV and aerosols; all weather; generally **unstable**; most flying |
| **Tropopause** | Top of troposphere | LR drops to **1–2°C/km** / stops falling | Max cloud tops; jets, max winds and CAT just below it |
| **Stratosphere** | Tropopause to **50 km** | Isothermal for first 8–10 km, then rises (slowly, then sharply) | Inversion from **ozone absorbing UV**; ozone layer ~25 km; very **dry, stable**, no weather; steady strong horizontal winds; **nacreous (mother-of-pearl) clouds** in upper stratosphere, high latitudes, winter |
| Stratopause | ~50 km | — | Upper boundary of stratosphere |
| **Mesosphere** | **50–80 km** | Falls | Weak solar absorption, weak mixing; **noctilucent clouds** (upper mesosphere, polar) |
| **Mesopause** | **80 km** | Stops falling | **Coldest** part of atmosphere, **≈ −100°C (173.15 K)**; **meteors burn up** here |
| **Thermosphere** | Mesopause to top | Rises with height | Above 60 km = **ionosphere** (radio propagation; disturbed by solar activity) |
| **Exosphere** | Up to ~10,000 km above thermosphere | — | Molecules/atoms escape to space; very hot particles but very little heat (far apart) |

- Troposphere sub-divisions: **lower** surface–2.1 km; **mid** 2.1–7.6 km; **upper** 7.6 km–tropopause.
- Tropopause is higher at the equator because of **rising hot convection currents**: **warmer surface → higher tropopause**.

### Tropopause details (p.5–6)
- Height controlled by: **surface temperature, latitude, season, land–sea distribution, synoptic situation**.
- Tropopause **temperature**: equator **−70 to −75°C** (colder), poles **−40 to −45°C**.
- Tropopause **lower in winter** than in summer.
- **Reversal above 8 km**: because the equatorial troposphere is deeper, above ~8 km the **poles are warmer than the equator** (reversal of temperature and density).
- **Breaks / folds** in the tropopause at about **40° and 60° latitude**; the **40° break is more prominent**; **jet streams occur at these breaks**. They divide the tropopause into three sections:

| Section | Pressure level | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Polar tropopause** | ~**300 hPa** | Polewards of 45°–60° | Occasionally over **Srinagar** in winter (polar air incursion); **Polar Front Jet** at its southern end |
| **Middle tropopause** | ~**200 hPa** | Between polar and tropical | Over India ~**11.5 km, −45°C**; in winter may be at 23°N; **Subtropical Jet (STJ)** at its southern end |
| **Tropical tropopause** | ~**100 hPa** | Equator to 35°–40° | Over India **16–16.5 km** |

- Importance to pilots: **maximum height clouds reach**; **jets, maximum winds and CAT occur just below it**; weather mainly confined below it.

### International Standard Atmosphere (ICAO ISA) (p.7)
- Used for aircraft design and testing, performance evaluation, altimeter calibration.

| ISA item | Value |
|---|---|
| Air | **Dry** |
| MSL temperature | **15°C (288.15 K)** |
| MSL pressure | **1013.25 hPa** |
| MSL density | **1225 g/m³** (1.225 kg/m³) |
| Gravity | **980.665 cm/s²** |
| Lapse rate to 11 km (36,090 ft) | **6.5°C/km (1.98°C/1000 ft)** |
| 11 km to 20 km (65,617 ft) | **Constant −56.5°C** (isothermal) |
| 20 km to 32 km | Temperature **rises 1°C/km** (0.3°C/1000 ft), reaching **−44.5°C at 32 km** (104,987 ft) |
| ISA tropopause | **11 km** |

- **ISA deviation = Actual − ISA**. Example: 8°C warmer than ISA = ISA +8.
- ISA temperature at height h (km) up to 11 km: **15 − 6.5h**. Per 1000 ft: **15 − 2h** (h in thousands of ft).

### Jet Standard Atmosphere (JSA) (p.7)
- ISA is fine below 30,000 ft but not above; JSA introduced for very high flying, space flight and engine manufacture.
- MSL temperature **15°C (288.15 K)**; lapse rate **2°C/1000 ft**; **no tropopause**.

### Exam traps
- "Atmosphere is heated by" → **heat from the earth's surface** (not directly by the sun).
- "Above 8 km, lower temperatures are over" → the **equator**.
- Mother-of-pearl = **nacreous** = **stratosphere**; **noctilucent** = **mesosphere**.
- Negative lapse rate = temperature **rising** with height (inversion).
- Maximum heat transfer = **latent heat**.

## 2. Atmospheric Pressure

### Static and dynamic pressure (p.12)
- **Static (barometric) pressure**: air at rest; molecules in random motion push **equally in all directions**.
- **Dynamic (wind) pressure**: extra pressure when air moves, exerted in the direction **opposite to the flow**.

### Pressure as the weight of air above (p.12)
- Pressure at any level = **weight of the column of air of unit cross-section** above it, up to the top of the atmosphere → so pressure **falls with height**.
- ICAO unit: **hectopascal (hPa)**. 1 hPa = 1000 dynes/cm² (= 1 millibar). Dyne = force giving 1 cm/s² to 1 g.
- 1 bar = 100,000 N/m² (≈ sea-level pressure); 1 mb = 1/1000 bar = **1 hPa** ("hecto" = 100).
- **1013.25 hPa = 760 mm Hg = 29.92 in Hg**.
- hPa → inches: **× 0.02953** (1013.25 × 0.02953 = 29.92 in).
- Pressure changes make ears "pop" over varying terrain and in rapid descent.

### Vertical variation of pressure (p.13)
- Falls with height **at a decreasing rate**: ~4% per 600 m near sea level, ~3% up to 1.5 km, ~2.5% up to 3 km.
- At **6 km pressure is half** the sea-level value; at **100 km negligible** (vacuum).
- Height change for 1 hPa: **Δh = 96 T / p feet** (T in K, p in hPa). Example: 1000 hPa, 300 K → 96 × 300/1000 = **28.8 ft**.

| Level (ISA) | Height per 1 hPa |
|---|---|
| MSL | **27 ft** |
| 2,000 ft | 30 ft |
| 20,000 ft | 50 ft |
| 40,000 ft | 100 ft |

- Air **warmer than ISA** → more feet per hPa; **colder** → fewer feet per hPa.

### Warm and cold columns (p.14)
- Cold air is denser → pressure **falls faster with height over a cold column**.
- So a given pressure level (e.g. 850 hPa) is **higher over warm air, lower over cold air**.
- Therefore at any given height **pressure is higher over warm air** than over cold air → aloft, **low over the cold column, high over the warm column**.
- Isobars **dip** going from high to low, **rise** going from low to high. **Isobar** = line joining places of equal pressure.

### Semi-diurnal variation (p.14–15)
- Pressure has a **bimodal daily curve**: **maxima ~1000 h (primary) and ~2200 h**, **minima ~0400 h and ~1600 h** local time.
- Very small at the poles; **largest at the equator, about 3–5 hPa** (solar influence).
- Not in phase with temperature (≈3 h lag). Probably a natural ~12-hour oscillation of the atmosphere: a high on one side of the globe, a low on the other, rotating with the earth → two maxima, two minima in 24 h.

### Measurement (p.15)
- **Mercury barometer**: most accurate; pressure balanced by a mercury column.
- **Aneroid barometer**: less accurate but convenient, widely used.
- **Altimeter** = an **aneroid barometer** graduated in altitude, with a **sub-scale** for the pressure setting.
- **Barograph** = continuous record (daily or weekly).

### Altimeter correction (Alticor / D value) (p.15–16)
- **Alticor = indicated altitude − true altitude**; **D value = true − indicated**. D value **decreases flying from high to low**.
- Rough Alticor: **pressure** — 30 ft per hPa (add when MSL pressure > 1013.2, subtract when lower); **temperature** — **1% of indicated altitude per 3°C** off ISA (add if warmer, subtract if colder); add algebraically.

### Contours and thickness (p.16)
- On a constant-pressure chart, the height of the pressure surface is lower over cold places, higher over warm.
- **Contour (isohypse)** = line of equal height of a pressure surface; acts like an isobar.
- Contour interval: **40 gpm on 700 and 500 hPa charts**; **80 gpm on 300 and 200 hPa charts**. Labelled in geopotential **decametres** (5280 gpm → "528"). 1 gpm = 9.8 J/kg.
- **Thickness** = height difference between two pressure levels: **low where the layer is cold, high where warm**; thickness lines = **isotherms of mean layer temperature**.

### Pressure gradient, tendency, isallobars (p.16–17)
- **Pressure gradient**: horizontal rate of change of pressure **perpendicular to isobars, from high to low**. Close isobars = **steep** gradient (strong wind); far apart = weak/flat.
- **Pressure tendency** = change of pressure with time: **24 h in India**, **3 h in higher latitudes**.
- **Isallobars** = lines of **equal pressure tendency**. Greatest fall = **isallobaric low**; greatest rise = **isallobaric high**.
- Surface lows tend to **move towards the isallobaric low and intensify**; an isallobaric high indicates **weakening**.

### Altimetry definitions (p.17)
- **MSL**: average of high and low tides.
- **Altitude**: vertical distance from **MSL**. **Height**: from a **specified datum** (e.g. ground). **Elevation**: of a point on the earth's surface, from MSL.
- **Transition altitude (TA)**: altitude **at or below which** you fly on **QNH** (vertical position as altitude).
- **Transition level (TL)**: **lowest flight level** available above TA; above it you fly on **1013.2 hPa**. Expressed in hundreds of feet.
- **Transition layer**: airspace **between TA and TL**.
- **Flight levels**: surfaces of constant pressure referenced to **1013.25 hPa**, separated by 500 ft intervals (FL50 = 5,000 ft; FL300 = 30,000 ft).
- **Pressure altitude**: altimeter reading with **1013.2 hPa** set (written 3500, 19000; as FL35, FL190).

### Pressure settings (p.18)
- Barometer reading is corrected for **index error, gravity (latitude) and temperature**, then reduced to MSL.

| Setting | What it is | Altimeter shows |
|---|---|---|
| **QFE** | Pressure at **aerodrome level** (ARP / highest point of runway) | **Zero** on the ground ("zero setting"): height above aerodrome |
| **QFF** | Aerodrome pressure reduced to MSL using the **actual temperature** of the column | Used for **synoptic charts / isobars** |
| **QNH** | Station pressure reduced to MSL **assuming ISA** | **Aerodrome elevation** on the ground; altitude above MSL; used for terrain and vertical separation |
| **Regional QNH** | Forecast **lowest** QNH expected in an **Altimeter Setting Region** | Issued **hourly**, valid **1 h**; ensures terrain clearance |
| **QNE** | Altitude shown on the ground with **1013.25 hPa (29.92 in)** set | Pressure altitude of the aerodrome; used at **high-altitude airfields** |

- Rules: **hotter than ISA → QFF < QNH**; **at MSL QFF = QNH**.
- Set pressure **lower than QNH → indicated < true**; set **higher than QNH → indicated > true**.

### Worked examples (p.18–19), using 1 hPa = 30 ft (or 27 ft)
- QFE 950, QNH 1000 → elevation = (1000 − 950) × 30 = **1500 ft**; with 1013 set, airfield shows (1013 − 950) × 30 = **1890 ft**.
- QFE 970, QNH 1020 → elevation **1500 ft**; with 1013 set → (1013 − 970) × 30 = **1290 ft**.
- QFE 1000, elevation 270 ft, 27 ft/hPa → 10 hPa → **QNH 1010**.
- Depart A (elev 400 ft, QNH 1010), land at B (elev 600 ft) without resetting; altimeter reads 700 ft → 300 ft ÷ 27 = 11 hPa → **QNH at B = 999 hPa** (high-to-low: over-read).

### Standard isobaric levels (Table 2.1)

| hPa | 850 | 700 | 500 | 400 | 300 | 200 | 100 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pressure altitude (ft) | **5,000** | **10,000** | **18,000** | **24,000** | **30,000** | **38,000** | **53,000** |
| Flight level | 50 | 100 | 180 | 240 | 300 | 380 | 530 |

### Over-reading and under-reading (p.19–20)
- Altimeter **over-reads** if pressure **falls** en route and in air **colder than ISA**; **under-reads** if pressure rises and in **warmer** air.
- **H → L → H**: **High to Low, look out below** = over-read. **Warm to cold** = over-read.
- **L → H → L**: Low to High = under-read; cold to warm = under-read.
- If QNH is high and the altimeter setting is low → true altitude high, indicated low.
- **Wind from port** (N hemisphere) = flying towards low pressure → altimeter **over-reads** (actual lower); **wind from starboard** → **under-reads**. Error grows with wind strength and route length. **Opposite in the S hemisphere**.

### Pressure patterns (p.21–22) (isobars at 2 hPa)
- **Low (L)**: closed isobar(s), lowest at centre. 2+ closed isobars = **depression**; stronger = **cyclone / severe cyclone**. N hemisphere winds **anticlockwise and converging** → **upward motion → bad weather, but visibility better than in a high**. Winds **back**. Surface wind < 17 kt (for a "low").
- **Trough**: tongue/**V-shaped** extension of a low; pressure lower than either side; wind **changes abruptly and backs**; bad weather. Example: **monsoon trough over the Indo-Gangetic plains**.
- **High (H) / anticyclone**: highest at centre; **fair weather but poor visibility (subsidence)**; N hemisphere winds **clockwise, veer, diverge**.
- **Blocking anticyclone**: near-stationary high that blocks migratory cyclones.
- **Cold anticyclone**: forms over cold ground behind a departing cyclone; subsidence later warms it.
- **Ridge**: wedge from a high; **rounded** (not V) isobars; fair weather; winds **veer**.
- **Col**: between **two highs and two lows**; uniform pressure, **light variable winds**, mixed weather.

### Exam traps
- Altimeter **always measures height above the datum set on its sub-scale**.
- Altimeter on ground reads **aerodrome elevation** → **QNH**; reads **zero** → **QFE**.
- Isallobars = equal **pressure tendency** (not equal pressure).
- **Col** = two lows and two highs.
- Semi-diurnal variation biggest in the **tropics**.
- Steep gradient = **isobars close together, strong wind**.
- Inversion in a high = **subsidence inversion**.

## 3. Temperature

### Scales (p.27)
- Temperature = **measure of heat**, measured by a thermometer in °C or °F, fixed by the melting point of ice and boiling point of pure water at normal pressure.
- **Celsius**: ice 0°C, boiling 100°C (international, aviation, science). **Fahrenheit**: 32°F and 212°F (a few English-speaking countries).
- **Absolute zero**: the lowest possible temperature, same for all substances; molecular motion almost stops. **0 K = −273.16°C** (book wording "1K = −273.16°C").
- Conversions: **F = 9C/5 + 32**; **C = 5/9 (F − 32)**; **K = C + 273**.
- **−40°C = −40°F**.
- Freezing point of water = 0°C = **32°F = 273 K**; boiling = 100°C = **212°F = 373 K**. Example: 68°F = 20°C = **293 K**.

### Instruments (p.28)
- **Dry bulb**, **wet bulb** (bulb wrapped in moist muslin), **maximum** thermometer (like a doctor's thermometer), **minimum** thermometer, **thermograph**.
- Mercury in thermometers, **except the minimum thermometer, which uses alcohol**.
- **Bi-metallic strips** in thermographs and upper-air temperature measurement. Thermograph = continuous record.
- Minimum thermometer: a **dumbbell-shaped index** in alcohol; alcohol's concave meniscus drags the index down as temperature falls.

### Definitions (p.28)
- **Surface temperature**: measured **1.25 m (4 ft) above the ground in shade**, inside a **Stevenson screen** (door opens **away from the sun**).
- **Ambient temperature**: temperature of the surroundings.
- **Virtual temperature**: the temperature at which **dry air** would have the same pressure and density as the moist air parcel (lets the dry-air equation of state be used for moist air).
- **Heat** = **sum total** of the kinetic energy of all molecules; **temperature** = **average** KE. A bathtub at 60°C holds more heat than a cup of boiling water. The thermosphere is very hot but holds little heat (few, far-apart particles) → no effect on spacecraft.
- **Specific heat**: heat needed to raise unit mass by 1°C. **Water 1 (highest), ice 0.5, soil 0.2** → **land heats and cools much faster than water**. Temperature rise is **inversely proportional** to specific heat.
- **Latent heat**: heat absorbed or released in a **change of phase**; **absorbed** solid→liquid→gas, **released** in the reverse. Turning boiling water into vapour needs **> 5×** the heat needed to bring ice-cold water to the boil. Released again on condensation.

### Heat transfer (p.29–30)
- **Conduction**: molecule-to-molecule by contact; important **very close to the ground**. (Air is a poor conductor.)
- **Convection**: hot fluid bodily moves to the colder part. **Free convection** = intense solar heating; **forced convection** = topography/mechanical lifting. >70% of earth is water → convection important.
- **Radiation**: needs no medium and does not heat the medium; solar radiation heats the earth directly. **Radiation plays the most significant role** in atmospheric heat transfer.
- Others: **advection** (horizontal transport by wind), **latent heat** release, **turbulence** (eddies redistribute heat), up/down air motion.
- Troposphere: all processes act. **Stratosphere**: no convection or latent heat; heated by **ozone absorbing short-wave (UV)**.

### Insolation and radiation laws (p.30)
- **Insolation** = total solar radiation received over an area; depends on the **obliquity of the sun's rays**: max when the sun is overhead, least at sunrise/sunset; max over the **tropics**, least at the poles.
- **Black body**: a body that emits radiation (at its temperature) over a wide range of wavelengths.
- **Stefan–Boltzmann law**: total energy radiated ∝ **T⁴** (absolute temperature) → hot bodies (sun) emit intense radiation.
- **Wien's law**: wavelength of maximum radiation ∝ **1/T** → hot sun emits **short waves**, cool earth emits **long waves**.
- **Planck's law**: distribution of radiated energy with wavelength (right-skewed curve).

### Solar, terrestrial and nocturnal radiation (p.31)
- Sun's surface ≈ **6000°C** → mainly **short waves**. Visible light = VIBGYOR (red longest, violet shortest); **UV** beyond violet, **IR** beyond red.
- Solar radiation ≈ **IR 46%, visible 45%, UV 9%**.
- **Terrestrial radiation**: long-wave, invisible, mostly IR. Earth gains heat as short wave and loses it as long wave.
- **Nocturnal radiation**: radiation from the earth at night (no incoming short wave). **Warmer earth → more intense** nocturnal radiation.

### Heat budget and albedo (p.31–32)
- Earth's mean temperature is steady, so incoming short wave = outgoing terrestrial radiation.
- Of incoming solar radiation: **30% reflected/scattered** back to space; **19% absorbed by the atmosphere** (ozone, WV, dust 16% + clouds 3%); **51% absorbed by the earth's surface**.

| Absorbed | % | Scattered back | % |
|---|---|---|---|
| Earth's surface | 51 | Air | 6 |
| Water vapour, dust, ozone | 16 | Clouds | 20 |
| Clouds | 3 | Earth's surface | 4 |
| **Total absorbed** | **70** | **Total reflected (albedo)** | **30** |

- **Albedo** = reflected ÷ incident radiation = **reflecting power of the earth (~30%)**.
- In clear weather about **5/6** of solar radiation reaches the surface; the surface reflects ~10% of that; **snow reflects ~80%**.

### Diurnal variation of surface temperature (p.32–33)
- **Sea < 1°C** day-to-night; **land averages ~20°C**. Coastal stations: depends on **wind direction** (off-land wind → large like inland; off-sea wind → small; sea breeze cools).
- **Largest with calm wind**; strong wind mixes heat through the **friction layer (~1 km)** → small variation.
- Ground keeps cooling after sunrise until incoming = outgoing radiation → **minimum temperature ½–1 h after sunrise/dawn**.
- Ground is hottest about **noon**; screen **maximum 2–3 h later** (early afternoon).
- **Clouds**: by day they reflect sunlight (thin cirrostratus cuts some; thick low cloud/fog may cut all) → cooler days; by night they absorb outgoing radiation and their bases radiate back → **cloudy nights are warmer**.
- **Low cloud cover → small diurnal range**; the lower the cloud, the more it reduces night cooling. Least diurnal variation on an **overcast** day; most over **dry desert**, calm, clear.

### Exam traps
- Minimum thermometer liquid = **alcohol**.
- Surface temperature height = **1.25 m**.
- Heat = **sum total**; temperature = **average** KE.
- Black-body energy ∝ **T⁴**; peak wavelength ∝ **1/T**.
- Isothermal layer = temperature **constant with height**.

## 4. Air Density

### Why it matters (p.37)
- Density affects **lift, thrust, drag, climb rate and airspeed**.
- **Low density** → slower climb-out, **higher take-off and landing speeds**, **less engine thrust**, **longer runways**; allow for it in runway length and all-up weight.

### Definition and density altitude (p.37)
- **Density = mass of air per unit volume** (g/m³ or kg/m³).
- **Density altitude** = the altitude **in ISA** at which the **prevailing density** occurs.
- In ISA, **pressure altitude = density altitude**.
- **1°C deviation from ISA ≈ 120 ft** of density altitude.
- Temperature **above ISA → density altitude > pressure altitude** (air behaves as if higher). High density altitude = like operating from a higher airfield.

### Gas law (p.38)
- **PV = RT** → **ρ = P / RT** (P in hPa, T in K, R for dry air = 2.87 × 10⁶ in cgs units).
- ISA (1013.25 hPa, 288 K) gives **1225 g/m³**.
- Moist air: gas constant for WV = 8/5 × dry air; ρ(moist) = **(p − 3e/8) / RT** → **moist air is less dense** than dry air at the same pressure and temperature (effect small; usually ignored for aviation).

### Factors affecting density (p.38)
- **Altitude**: higher → less dense. **Temperature**: warmer → less dense. **Humidity**: humid air is lighter (minor factor).
- High humidity: **add 10% to computed take-off distance**, expect reduced climb rate.

### Surface variations (p.39)
- At constant pressure, **density ∝ 1/T (absolute)**.
- Daily: **lowest density in the afternoon**, **highest just after sunrise**. Seasonal changes from temperature and pressure.
- **≈1% density decrease** from any of: **pressure fall of 10 hPa**, **temperature rise of 3°C**, **height increase of 300 ft**.

### Variation with height (p.39)
- In ISA density decreases at all levels: about **3% per 1000 ft** (good to ~20,000 ft).
- If density were uniform the atmosphere would be only **8 km** deep.
- Density **halves every 5–6 km**: **½ at 6 km, ¼ at 11 km, ⅛ at 17 km**. Still measurable drag at 240 km → atmosphere believed to extend to ~**800 km**.

### Latitudinal variation (p.39)
- At sea level density is **lowest at the equator, highest at the poles** (to ~8 km).
- **Above 8 km reversal**: density greater over the equator.
- So **low-level (piston) aircraft** are more efficient in **high latitudes**; **high-level (jet) aircraft** more efficient in the **tropics**.

### Exam traps
- Higher density altitude = **lower** density.
- Pressure up (temperature constant) → density up → density altitude **down**.
- Less dense in high altitude, warm air **and** high humidity: "**all these**".

## 5. Humidity

### Water in the air (p.41)
- WV is always present in the troposphere; it drives all weather. Water cycle: evaporation (oceans, lakes, rivers, vegetation) → cloud → precipitation.
- Three phases: gas (WV), liquid (rain, drizzle, shower), solid (snow, hail).
- Capacity of air to hold WV depends **mainly on temperature** (some on pressure): **warmer air holds more**.

### Terms (p.41–42)
- **Dry air**: no WV at all (upper troposphere, stratosphere).
- **Moist air**: normal air; also called "unsaturated" or "dry" air at its temperature and pressure.
- **Saturated air**: holds the maximum WV (like a full sponge).
- **Vapour pressure (e)**: partial pressure of WV; pressure of dry air = p − e.
- **Saturation vapour pressure (SVP)**: vapour pressure when saturated.
- **Absolute humidity**: actual mass of WV **per volume** of air (**g/m³**).
- **Humidity mixing ratio (HMR)**: mass of WV per mass of dry air (**g/kg**). **Constant when air is lifted adiabatically** (no WV added or removed).
- **Saturation HMR**: maximum HMR at that temperature and pressure; **rises with temperature**.
- **Relative humidity (RH)** = actual WV ÷ maximum possible at the same temperature and pressure × 100 = **HMR/HMRsat × 100 = e/SVP × 100**.

### Measurement (p.42)
- **Psychrometer** and **hygrometer** measure; **hygrograph** records.

### Wet bulb, dew point, frost point (p.42)
- **Wet-bulb temperature**: lowest temperature air can reach by **evaporating water into it** until saturated (desert-cooler principle; drier air cools more).
- **Dew point (DP)**: temperature to which air must be **cooled at constant pressure** to saturate it (with respect to water). Cooling below DP → condensation.
- DP changes **only with water content**; **RH changes with water content and temperature**. Warming/cooling changes RH but **not DP**. More WV → higher DP.
- **Frost point**: saturation with respect to **ice**; cooling below it → **hoar frost**.
- **Cloud base (ft) ≈ (T − DP) × 400** (surface values in °C).

### Key facts (p.43)
- Warmer air needs **more WV** to saturate.
- Below 0°C, **SVP over water > SVP over ice** → in mixed clouds water drops evaporate and deposit on ice crystals (explains rain from clouds above the 0°C level).
- Supercooled droplets exist down to **−40°C** (to **−45°C in CB**).
- **Saturated** (fog, rain): **T = Tw = Td**. **Unsaturated**: **T > Tw > Td**.
- Rainy/humid day → **longer runway** needed.

### Exam traps
- Spread T − Td is **least (zero)** when saturated.
- "Actual amount of WV in a given volume" = **absolute humidity**.
- "Lowest temperature by evaporating water into it" = **wet bulb**, not dew point.

## 6. Winds

### Basics (p.45)
- **Wind** = horizontal movement of air; a succession of **gusts** (strong) and **lulls** (weak). Two components: **direction and speed**.
- **Direction** = where the wind blows **from** (a southerly blows S → N). Given on 16 compass points or in degrees (10° steps), measured from **True North** (in met reports).
- **Speed** in **knots**. 1 km/h = 0.278 m/s = 0.540 kt; roughly **1 m/s ≈ 2 kt ≈ 4 km/h**.
- Reported in 5 kt steps: **0–2 kt = calm**; 3–7 → 05 kt; 8–12 → 10 kt; … 48–52 → 50 kt.

### Crosswind and instruments (p.46)
- Runways face the **prevailing wind** (from climatological records). Wind at 90° to the runway = **crosswind component**; each type has a **critical crosswind** limit; crosswind swings aircraft (lighter ones more).
- Surface speed: **anemometer**; direction: **wind vane**. Both at **10 m** height, clear of obstructions.
- Averaging: **10 minutes** for weather reports; **2 minutes** for take-off and landing.
- Upper winds: **RAWIN** (tracked by **radar**) and **pilot balloon** (tracked by **optical theodolite**); hydrogen-filled balloons.

### Gust, lull, squall, gale (p.46–47)
- **Gust**: rapid, irregular, transient increase lasting **seconds** (friction turbulence, uneven heating, hot afternoons). Negative fluctuation = **lull**.
- **Squall**: sudden increase of **≥ 16 kt (32 km/h, 8 m/s)**, reaching **≥ 22 kt (44 km/h, 11 m/s)**, lasting **≥ 1 minute**. With **CB / violent convection**; spreads several km and thousands of feet; direction and speed differ widely from the prevailing wind; often a sharp temperature drop and precipitation.
- Violent squalls: **Nor'westers, TS and duststorms, March–June**. **Line squalls ahead of cold fronts** (and sometimes with Nor'westers).
- Gust vs squall = **duration** (seconds vs minutes).
- **Gale**: **persistent mean wind ≥ 34 kt**, with depressions and cyclonic storms. A parked aircraft can survive a gale but not necessarily a sudden 60 kt squall.

### Backing and veering, Buys Ballot (p.47)
- **Backing** = direction changes **anticlockwise** (090 → 060). **Veering** = **clockwise** (060 → 090). Example: 310° → 020° veers; 270° → 250° backs.
- NH: wind blows anticlockwise around a low (backs), clockwise around a high (veers).
- **Buys Ballot's law**: in the **NH, stand with your back to the wind — low pressure is on your left**, high on the right. Opposite in SH.

### Coriolis (geostrophic) force (p.48)
- Earth's rotation deflects moving air **to the right in the NH, left in the SH**; an **apparent** force acting **perpendicular** to the wind.
- **Coriolis force = 2 Ω ρ V sin φ** (Ω earth's angular velocity, V wind speed, φ latitude).
- **Maximum at the poles** (sin 90° = 1), **zero at the equator**. Proportional to wind speed and air density.
- **Pressure gradient force (PGF)**: from high to low, = pressure difference ÷ distance between isobars.

### Geostrophic wind (p.49–50)
- Frictionless wind **parallel to straight isobars**, from a **balance of PGF and Coriolis**, low on the left (NH). Closer isobars → stronger. A **calculated**, not real, wind.
- **Vg = PGF / (2 Ω ρ sin φ)** → for the same gradient, **stronger at lower latitudes**.
- **Breaks down at the equator** (sin 0 = 0 → infinite); good approximation **beyond ~30°** and in **mid-latitudes**.
- Limits: needs balance, straight parallel isobars, steady pressure; gusts, squalls and local winds don't follow it.

### Cyclostrophic, gradient, isallobaric, inertial winds (p.50–51)
- **Cyclostrophic**: strongly curved flow where **Coriolis is negligible**; **PGF balances centripetal (ρV²/r)**. Good near the **centre of a tropical revolving storm or a tornado** (e.g. 2000 ft wind in an intense TRS).
- **Gradient wind**: parallel to **curved** isobars; balance of **PGF, Coriolis and centripetal**; better than geostrophic in mid-latitudes (friction ignored).
  - **Cyclone** (book: P + C = f): part of the PGF supplies the centripetal force → wind **less than Vg = sub-geostrophic**.
  - **Anticyclone** (book: P = C + f): → wind **greater than Vg = super-geostrophic**.
  - Straight isobars → gradient = geostrophic. Coriolis negligible → gradient = cyclostrophic.
- **Isallobaric wind**: when pressure changes rapidly, an isallobaric force acts from higher to lower isallobar → deflects wind **towards falling pressure**.
- **Inertial wind**: **no PGF**; balance of centrifugal and Coriolis; Vi = fR; **anticyclonic in both hemispheres**.

### Friction (p.52)
- Friction reaches about **1 km** (the **friction layer**; depends on speed, lapse rate, roughness). Slower wind → weaker Coriolis → wind turns **across isobars towards low pressure**.

| Surface | Angle to isobars | Speed vs geostrophic |
|---|---|---|
| **Sea** | **≈ 15°** | **≈ 2/3** |
| **Land** | **≈ 30°** | **1/3 to 1/2** |

### Turbulence (p.52–53)
- Width of gust–lull swings = **gustiness**. Two types: **frictional (mechanical)** — flow over rough ground becomes unstable above a critical speed; **thermal** — convection from surface heating or cool air over a warm surface.
- Increased by buildings, trees, rugged terrain and **steep lapse rates**; least over the open sea and in stable air; **more by day**, least on clear nights with an inversion.
- **Thermal eddies** are bigger and give stronger gusts than frictional ones; can reach great heights.
- **Factors**: turbulence and the friction layer are increased by flow over **buildings, trees and rugged country** and by a **steep lapse rate**; less over open sea; **most by day**, **least on a clear night with an inversion** (p.52–53).

### Diurnal variation of surface wind (p.53–54)
- **Day**: thermals deepen the friction layer and bring stronger upper wind down → surface wind **stronger, gustier and veered** towards the gradient wind (NH).
- **Night**: friction layer shrinks; upper wind cannot reach down → surface wind **weak and backed** (crosses isobars more); marked **wind shear near 500 m** (strong above, weak below) — a hazard.
- Little diurnal change over the **sea** (~1°C range) and under continuous overcast; most marked in fine weather.

### Wind shear (p.54–55)
- **Wind shear**: variation of wind vector along the aircraft's path that can displace it.
- **Low-level WS**: along final approach, runway, take-off and initial climb. **Vertical WS**: change of horizontal wind with height. **Horizontal WS**: with distance. **Up/downdraughts**: change of the vertical component with distance.
- Causes:
  - **TS gust front**: cold outflow meets warm air; reaches **~30 km** ahead and **~6,000 ft** up.
  - **Microburst**: concentrated downdraught **~4 km across**, lasts **1–5 min**; most hazardous; surface wind difference up to **90 kt**, even opposite directions. **Wet** (heavy rain, evaporative cooling) or **dry** (high-based TS, virga evaporating under the anvil/AC).
  - **Low-level inversion** on clear nights (up to ~1 km): weak surface wind, strong wind above.
  - Gusty surface winds; intense solar heating; **topography** (mountains, buildings, valleys); **fronts** (abrupt change; strong winds ahead of cold fronts); **jet streams** aloft.
- Effects (book): if shear **reduces the headwind** → in **level flight** IAS falls and the aircraft **loses height**; in a **climb** IAS falls and it loses height; in **descent** the book lists IAS increasing and the aircraft gaining height. A **tailwind** shear reverses these. Critical at take-off and landing.

### Local winds (p.55–58)
- **Anabatic**: **daytime upslope** wind (heated slope); weak, masked by convection; stronger when funnelled = **valley wind**; helped by sea breeze on coasts.
- **Katabatic**: **night downslope** cold wind (slope cools by radiation); few knots but pools cold air in hollows → **frost, mist, fog**. Common in **NE India** (morning fog/TS). Over **snow-covered** slopes can blow **by day too**. **Bora** (Adriatic, gusts > 100 kt), Greenland, Black Sea.
- **Föhn**: moist air forced over a range; cools at **SALR** while cloud forms and rain falls on the windward side; descends dry on the lee and warms at **DALR** → **warm, dry** lee wind (Alps); **10°C or more warmer**. **Chinook** (Rockies). Needs (a) a substantial mountain range, (b) wind blowing **within 30°** of the range's perpendicular (book: "within 30° of the range"), (c) **high moisture content**. Dry air may not give a Föhn.
- Föhn = warm and dry; katabatic = cold. Both are downslope.
- **Ravine winds**: pressure difference across hills drives strong flow through narrow valleys, continuing beyond the mouth.

### Sea and land breeze (p.57–58)
- **Sea breeze (day)**: land heats → air rises → pressure aloft over land rises → upper air drifts seaward → surface pressure rises over sea, falls over land → sea-to-land wind.
- Sets in **a few hours after sunrise** (delayed to afternoon by an offshore gradient wind); brings a **fall in temperature and rise in humidity**; can be gusty; extends **15–25 km** inland (to **Pune, ~170 km** from Mumbai). Routine in the tropics.
- Starts perpendicular to the coast; Coriolis later turns it along the coast with **land on the left (NH)**. Dies after sunset; land breeze follows a few hours later.
- Depth: sea breeze can reach **3,000–5,000 ft** with a line of small cumulus at its front (useful to gliders). **Land breeze is weaker and shallower** (a few hundred feet), less regular.

### Thermal wind (p.58–59)
- **Thermal wind** = the vector that must be **added to the lower-level geostrophic wind to give the upper-level geostrophic wind** = **upper − lower** (vertical wind shear).
- Blows **parallel to thickness lines / isotherms of mean temperature**, **cold (low thickness) on the left in the NH**; speed ∝ temperature gradient.
- Vector subtraction: reverse the direction of the vector being subtracted, then add.
- Examples: 270/10 + 270/15 → 270/25. 270/10 + 090/15 → 090/05. Upper 240/25, lower 060/15 → thermal **240/40**. Lower 050/10, upper 230/05 → thermal **230/15**.
- South warm, north cold → **westerly** thermal wind, increasing with height (the **subtropical westerly jet** is an example).

### Contours and upper winds (p.59–60)
- Geostrophic wind blows **along contours** with **lower contours on the left (NH)**, right in SH.
- Troposphere: temperature falls equator → pole → **westerly thermal wind**, westerlies increase with height; upper troposphere mainly westerly.
- Westerly thermal wind → **northerly winds back with height, southerly winds veer**.
- Outside tropics low-level westerlies increase with height; easterlies weaken and turn westerly (easterlies are shallow).
- **India, monsoon (Jun–Sep)**: **easterlies above 500 hPa**, strengthening with height, stronger over S India; **Tropical Easterly Jet ~13°N at ~15 km**.
- Strongest tropospheric westerlies **~40,000 ft between 25°N and 40°N** in winter; shift to **40–45°N** in summer.
- Winter stratosphere: westerlies increase with height (cold pole); summer stratosphere: **easterly** thermal wind (warm pole).

### Vorticity (p.61)
- Measure of rotation (cyclonic/anticyclonic); changes cause convergence/divergence.
- Causes: **horizontal wind shear**, **curvature** (trough = cyclonic, ridge = anticyclonic), **earth's rotation** (always **cyclonic** in both hemispheres). **Absolute vorticity is always cyclonic**.

### Named winds (p.61–62)
- **Bora**: cold katabatic, Yugoslavia/NE Italy → Adriatic. **Mistral**: katabatic down the Rhône valley (France) to the Gulf of Lyons.
- **Buran (Purga with snow)**: strong NE wind, Russia/central Asia, winter. **Chinook**: warm dry westerly, east of the Rockies.
- **Haboob**: sand-raising wind, Sudan. **Harmattan**: hot dusty NE wind (book: central Asia). **Khamsin**: hot dry sandy southerly over **Egypt, Apr–Jun**.
- **Monsoon**: any markedly seasonal wind (E and SE Asia).
- **Roaring Forties / Furious Fifties / Crying Sixties**: westerlies 35°–60° (stormy beyond 40°S).
- **Trades**: steady winds **10°–30°**, **NE in NH, SE in SH**.
- **Doldrums**: calm belt ~**5°N–5°S** between the trades; low pressure, cloud and rain = **ITCZ**.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Calm | < 1 | 7 | Near gale | 28–33 |
| 1 | Light air | 1–3 | **8** | **Gale** | **34–40** |

### Exam traps
- Facing the wind in the SH, low is on your **left** (back to wind → right).
- Port drift (NH) → flying towards **high** → altimeter **under-reads**.
- Isotachs = equal wind speed.
- Sea breeze → temperature **falls**, RH **rises**.
- Thermal wind is parallel to **isotherms** (thickness lines), not isobars.
- Inertial flow: **anticyclonic in both hemispheres**.

## 7. Visibility and Fog

### Definition and reporting (p.67)
- Visibility for aeronautical purposes is the **greater** of: (a) the greatest distance at which a **black object** of suitable size near the ground can be seen and recognised against a **bright background**; (b) the greatest distance at which **lights of ~1,000 candela** can be seen and identified against an **unlit background**.
- Met office reports the **lowest horizontal visibility** at the airfield (it can differ with direction, and from vertical/slant visibility).
- Estimated by day from runway markings and objects at known distances, by night from known lights = **visibility landmarks**.

### RVR (p.67–68)
- **Runway Visual Range**: range over which a pilot on the **runway centre line** can see the runway markings or the lights delineating the runway / its centre line.
- Measured close to touchdown and take-off areas. **Reported when visibility or RVR is below 1500 m**. RVR 2000 and 1500 may appear in METAR as supplementary information but are not disseminated internationally.
- Instruments: **transmissometer / scopograph** (light source + photoelectric receiver; displays in Met office and ATC tower); **AVRA** (Automatic Visual Range Assessor) at some Indian stations.

### Obscurations (p.68–69)

| Phenomenon | What | Visibility |
|---|---|---|
| **Haze** | Very small dry particles (smoke, dust, salt); milky look | **≤ 5000 m** |
| **Dust haze** | Dust raised by strong winds (desert, semi-arid); thicker by day; **summer** hazard; widespread (Rajasthan → Punjab, Haryana, UP, Bihar); up to **6–8 km** deep; diversion airfields also affected | poor |
| **Smoke haze** | Industrial/domestic smoke under **calm wind + ground inversion** | poor |
| **Mist** | Water droplets, **RH ≈ 100%** | **1000–5000 m** |
| **Fog** | Water droplets or ice crystals; **cloud on the ground** (stratus); RH ≈ 100%; thick / moderate / light | **< 1000 m** |
| **Smog** | Fog + smoke haze; health hazard | very poor |
| **Dust storm** | — ; improves fast if followed by rain | **≤ 5000 m** |
| **Sea spray** | Salt from wave crests, encourages condensation; salt film can blind a low-flying windscreen | reduced |
| **Volcanic ash** | Solid matter carried far; engine damage | near nil |

- Fog forms by **cooling air to its dew point** or by **adding moisture** until saturated.
- Indian volcanoes: **Barren Island** (12.16°N 93.51°E; first eruption 1787; latest 2020) and **Baratang mud volcano** (12°N 93°E, last 2005; 150 km N of Port Blair). Active elsewhere: Japan, Philippines, Indonesia, America, Mexico, Iceland, Russia.
- Precipitation: moderate rain **1–3 km**; heavy rain/showers **< 1000 m**; drizzle **≤ 3 km**; snow **< 1000 m**.

### Vertical and slant visibility (p.70)
- Fog/haze layers are shallow (a few thousand ft). Overhead, a pilot sees the airfield through a thin layer (good **vertical** visibility) but on approach looks through a long slant path and **loses the runway** (poor **slant** visibility).
- Transparency improves with height (except in cloud/precipitation). Visibility is lower looking **towards the sun**; better looking towards the moon.

### Radiation fog (p.70–71)
- Formed by **nocturnal radiative cooling** of the ground and the air next to it, below the dew point. **Land only; a night phenomenon.**
- Wind balance: **calm → dew** (cooling not mixed upward); **too strong → mixing over too deep a layer → low stratus** instead; **light wind 3–7 kt** → fog. That's why one place fogs and a nearby one doesn't.
- Favourable: **high RH**, **clear sky**, **light wind (3–7 kt)**, **stability** (cooling confined to a shallow layer). Found in **anticyclones, ridges, cols, ground inversions** (light winds, subsidence, clear skies).
- Can form below 100% RH on **hygroscopic nuclei**.
- **Most frequent / thickest around sunrise** (minimum temperature + slight wind increase). Clears **2–3 h after sunrise**; deep fog may last till midday; usually **lifts into low stratus** before dispersing.
- **India**: a **winter** hazard, typically **after a Western Disturbance** (rain, then clear skies). Almost daily in winter in the **Brahmaputra valley** (moisture + katabatic flow). Most susceptible: **NW India, UP, Bihar, Jharkhand, Chhattisgarh, Bengal, Assam** (southern banks of the Brahmaputra).
- Depth: a few hundred ft to **1500 ft**, max ~**1 km**; sharp top with clear air above.
- Pressure: **High or col with weak gradient**. No fog when the gradient is steep and low-level winds strong.

### Other fogs (p.72)
- **Advection fog**: **warm moist air moves over a colder surface** (land or sea) whose temperature is below the air's dew point. **Land and sea; any time of day or night.** Coastal Bengal and Orissa winter fogs = advection + radiation; Brahmaputra valley, Sundarbans, coastal belts and peninsular hills.
- **Steaming fog (sea smoke)**: **cold air over a warm sea**; vapour from the warm water condenses in the cold air. High latitudes, frozen land, Iceland, Norway.
- **Frontal fog**: in the continuous rain **ahead of a (warm) front**, saturating the surface air; or cloud lowering to the surface as the front passes.
- **Upslope fog**: moist air lifted up a slope, cooling adiabatically.
- **Inversion fog**: in an anticyclone, subsiding cold air contacts a warm water body.

### Fog dispersal (p.72)
- **Thermal**: heat the air above its dew point (WW2 FIDO burners; costly, discontinued).
- **Hygroscopic nuclei** seeded into fog → drops form, RH falls → fog clears. Dry ice / silver iodide / propane → freezing and precipitation (supercooled fog).

### Exam traps
- Fog **< 1000 m**; mist **1000–5000 m** (RH ~100%); haze **≤ 5000 m** (dry).
- Warm moist air over a cold surface = **advection fog** (fog and stratus).
- Radiation fog: **land, night, clear sky, light wind, high RH, inversion, highs and cols**.
- Fog is **stratus** on the ground. Measured by a **transmissometer**.

## 8. Vertical Motion and Clouds

### Vertical motion (p.75–76)
- Much smaller than horizontal wind but causes weather, turbulence, convergence/divergence, wind shear, fog and cloud.
- **Local** vertical motion (a few km, except in TS): friction, terrain, convection → sea/land breeze, katabatic, anabatic, valley winds.
- **Large-scale**: pressure systems, fronts, upper troughs/ridges, mountains → hundreds of km; mountain waves may reach the **upper stratosphere**.
- Causes:
  - **Frictional eddies**: horizontal, vertical or slant; grow in unstable air up to ~**1 km**.
  - **Terrain**: rises on the windward side, sinks on the lee; strong winds → **mountain (lee) waves**; can carry WV into the stratosphere → **nacreous clouds**.
  - **Convection (thermals)**: from ground heating; used by gliders; bumpiness; CU/CB with strong up- and downdraughts.
  - **Pressure systems**: lows/cyclones (ascent), anticyclones (descent).
  - **Fronts**: **warm front = gradual ascent over a large area**; **cold front = abrupt sharp ascent → convection, TS, squalls**.
  - **Wind shear**: vertical shear → eddies, turbulence; **CAT near jet streams**; amplified over mountains.

### Convergence and divergence (p.76–78)
- **Convergence** = net horizontal **inflow** → **ascent** near the ground (aloft, can go up or down below the tropopause). **Lows, cyclones, depressions, troughs** → bad weather, **better visibility** (except in precipitation).
- **Divergence** = net **outflow** (e.g. W wind 10 kt to the west of a station, 20 kt to the east) → **subsidence**. **Anticyclones, ridges** → fine weather, **poor visibility**.
- Surface convergence pairs with upper divergence over a cyclone; surface divergence with upper convergence over an anticyclone.

### Clouds: formation and classification (p.77)
- **Cloud** = visible aggregate of water droplets or ice particles, formed by **adiabatic lifting and cooling** until WV condenses (or **deposits** directly as ice). Height of condensation = **lifting condensation level (LCL)**.
- **10 genera**. By **form**: stratiform, cumuliform, cirriform. By **height** (tropics): **high 6–18 km** (ice crystals; give advance warning of weather), **medium 2–8 km** (water + ice; rain/snow), **low below 2 km**.

| Level | Polar | Temperate | Tropical |
|---|---|---|---|
| High | 10,000–25,000 ft (3–8 km) | 16,000–45,000 ft (5–13 km) | **20,000–60,000 ft (6–18 km)** |
| Medium | 6,500–13,000 ft (2–4 km) | 6,500–23,000 ft (2–7 km) | **6,500–25,000 ft (2–8 km)** |
| Low | up to 6,500 ft (2 km) | up to 6,500 ft | **up to 6,500 ft** |

### The ten genera (p.79–80)

| Genus | Level | Look | Weather / hazard |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cirrus (CI)** | High | White, fibrous, delicate filaments ("feathery"), ice | No precipitation, no icing |
| **Cirrostratus (CS)** | High | Thin whitish **veil** covering the sky, ice | **Halo**; thick → snow aloft; hardly any icing |
| **Cirrocumulus (CC)** | High | Small white ripples/puffs, ice, regular | No precipitation, hardly any icing |
| **Altocumulus (AC)** | Medium | White-grey patches, rolls in rows; **lenticularis** at lee-wave crests | No precipitation; **may give icing** |
| **Altostratus (AS)** | Medium | Grey/whitish fibrous sheet; sun as through **ground glass** | **Corona**; thick AS → continuous rain/snow; icing below 0°C |
| **Stratus (ST)** | Low | Grey uniform layer; **fog when on the ground**; ragged = fractostratus | **Drizzle**, ice prisms, snow grains; poor slant visibility |
| **Stratocumulus (SC)** | Low | Grey/white large puffs or rolls, regular | Bumpy |
| **Nimbostratus (NS)** | Low–medium | **Dark grey thick layer**, blots out the sun | **Continuous rain/snow**; icing; **ice pellets (< 5 mm) at warm fronts** |
| **Cumulus (CU)** | Vertical | Detached, sharp outlines, flat dark base, cauliflower top; towering = **TCU** | Fair-weather CU: daytime only, no rain |
| **Cumulonimbus (CB)** | Vertical | Huge tower, **anvil** top, very dark base with fractus | **All precipitation (RA, SH, GR, SN)**; most hazardous — avoid |

- **Fair-weather cumulus** forms in the forenoon, peaks in the afternoon, dissipates in the evening.
- **Virga** = precipitation falling from a cloud that evaporates before reaching the ground.
- **Nacreous (mother-of-pearl)**: upper stratosphere; like CI or AC lenticularis with strong **irisation**; ice or supercooled drops; seen mainly in Scotland and Scandinavia.
- **Noctilucent**: upper mesosphere (**80–85 km**); bluish/silvery; visible just after sunset/before sunrise, **45°–72°N (mostly ~55°N), late May to mid-August**; ice particles.

### Reporting (p.80–82)
- Cloud base is **AGL** in METAR, SPECI, TAF, local forecasts; **AMSL only in area forecast charts**.
- **Cloud of operational significance**: base below **1500 m (5000 ft)** or below the **highest minimum sector altitude**, whichever is greater (or any CB/TCU).
- **Ceiling**: height of the **lowest layer below 6000 m (20,000 ft)** covering **more than half the sky (≥ 5/8)**.
- Amount in **oktas** (eighths):

| Oktas | 0 | 1–2 | 3–4 | 5–7 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Code | SKC | **FEW** | **SCT** | **BKN** | **OVC** |

### Flying conditions (p.81–82)
- **Stratiform** (AS, CS): smooth, even in thick monsoon AS/NS; NS cuts visibility (rain); **ST** spoils slant visibility on approach; icing in AS and NS above the freezing level.
- **Cumuliform**: CC, AC some turbulence; fair-weather CU and SC bumpy; **CU, TCU, CB serious**: severe turbulence, hail, up/downdraughts, gusts, icing, lightning. Turbulence up to **10–20 km** around an active CB. Avoid landing/take-off in TS squalls. **Hail can fall in clear air under the anvil**.

### Species (p.83)
- **Fibratus**: filaments without hooks. **Lenticularis**: lens-shaped CC/AC/SC (orographic, or lee of CB/TCU).
- **Castellanus**: CI/CC/AC with **turret-like cumuliform tops** in lines → **mid-level instability**.
- **Fractus**: ragged ST/CU. **Congestus**: CU with strong sprouting, cauliflower (TCU).
- **Calvus**: CB without cirrus top yet. **Capillatus**: **CB with a distinct anvil** → SH, TS, squalls, hail, virga.

### Condensation trails (p.83–84)
- **Wing-tip trails**: short-lived; pressure drop at wing tips/propellers → adiabatic cooling.
- **Exhaust trails**: moisture from engine exhaust condensing, at about **9 km and above**; long, persistent, reveal position and track.
- **Distrails**: clear lanes cut through cloud by exhaust heat.
- **Mintra level**: **below it no contrails form**; for jets ≈ the level where temperature is **about −45°C** (at 100% RH).
- **Drytra level**: so cold that contrails form **even at 0% RH**; about **2 km above** the mintra level.
- **Maxtra level**: in the stratosphere; **above it no contrails form**.

### Exam traps
- Drizzle → **ST**. Showers → **CB**. Continuous rain → **NS**. Heaviest icing → **CB**.
- Halo → **CS** (ice); corona → **AS** (book key).
- Feathery ice cloud → **CI**. Lenticular → **mountain waves**.
- Ceiling = **≥ 5/8** below 20,000 ft.

## 9. Stability and Instability of the Atmosphere

### Processes (p.87)
- Air is a poor conductor → a small rising/sinking parcel is treated as **insulated** from its surroundings.
- **Isothermal process**: heat flows in/out so temperature stays constant.
- **Adiabatic process**: **no heat exchange**. Rising parcel → lower pressure → **expands → cools** (work done from internal energy); sinking parcel → compressed → **warms**. Adiabatic processes largely determine the **vertical temperature distribution**.
- **Lapse rate**: decrease of temperature with height (positive when temperature falls). Troposphere average **6.5°C/km**.

### The three lapse rates (p.88)

| Lapse rate | Value | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **DALR** (dry adiabatic) | **9.8°C/km ≈ 3°C/1000 ft** | Unsaturated parcel lifted adiabatically; constant |
| **SALR** (saturated adiabatic) | **≈ 5°C/km** near MSL (≈1.5°C/1000 ft) | Latent heat of condensation offsets cooling; **not constant**; **approaches DALR below −40°C** (little WV aloft) |
| **ELR** (environmental) | Actual; ISA 6.5°C/km | Usually between DALR and SALR (closer to SALR); can be **super-adiabatic (> DALR)**, **zero (isothermal)** or **negative (inversion)** |

### Inversion (p.88)
- **Temperature increasing with height** = **negative lapse rate** → **stability**, no turbulence.
- Low-level inversion: **winter**, nocturnal cooling; also **ridges, highs, anticyclones** (subsidence). Traps smoke, haze, mist, dust, fog → **poor visibility**.
- Often at the **top of a stratiform cloud layer or fog** (especially radiation fog).
- Inversion layer: surface ones on clear cool nights; higher ones with anticyclones and sometimes fronts. Vertical motion through it is **inhibited**.

### Stability ideas (p.88–89)
- **Stable**: a displaced parcel returns (ball in a bowl). **Unstable**: keeps going (ball on an upturned bowl). **Neutral**: stays where put.
- Stable layers: **stratosphere, thermosphere**. Unstable (temperature falls with height): **troposphere, mesosphere**.
- Tested on an **aerological diagram (tephigram)**: parcel KE decreases (stable), constant (neutral), increases (unstable).
- A lifted parcel **warmer than its environment rises (unstable)**; **colder sinks (stable)**.

### Stability criteria (p.89)

| Condition | Result |
|---|---|
| **DALR > ELR** | Dry air **stable** |
| **SALR > ELR** (ELR < SALR) | Saturated air stable → **absolutely stable** (dry and saturated) |
| **ELR > DALR** | **Absolutely unstable** (dry and saturated) |
| **DALR > ELR > SALR** | **Conditional instability**: stable if dry, unstable if saturated |
| **ELR = SALR = DALR** | **Neutral (indifferent)** |
| ELR > SALR | Saturated air unstable |
| Isothermal layer | Stable |

- **Latent instability**: initially stable air becomes unstable after forced ascent. **Real latent**: energy needed to lift it ≪ energy released later. **Pseudo latent**: lifting energy > energy released.
- **Potential (convective) instability**: a whole layer lifted bodily becomes unstable because latent heat released at its moist bottom steepens its lapse rate; needs **high RH low down, low RH higher up**.

### Tephigram (p.90–91)
- Plots radiosonde/RAWIN temperature, dew point and winds; gives stability, temperatures/winds at levels, altimeter correction, **tropopause**, **mintra level**, **LCL**, cloud base/top, latent and potential instability, wet-bulb temperature.
- Indian tephigram lines: **horizontal = potential temperature (DALR / dry adiabats)**; **vertical = isotherms**; **slanting ~45° = isobars**; curved lines slanting left becoming parallel to DALR aloft = **SALR**; dashed slightly-left lines = **saturation mixing ratio (isohygric)**, separate over water and ice.
- **Normand's theorem**: draw DALR up from the **dry bulb**, SALR from the **wet bulb** and the isohygric from the **dew point** — the three meet at the **Normand point**. The first one from surface values = **LCL** (likely cloud base).

### Exam traps
- DALR ≈ **10°C/km** (9.8) = 3°C/1000 ft; SALR ≈ **5°C/km** at MSL.
- ELR < SALR → **absolutely stable**; ELR > DALR → **absolutely unstable**.
- Inversion = **negative** lapse rate = **stability**; common in **winter**.
- The process mainly fixing the vertical temperature distribution = **adiabatic**.

## 10. Optical Phenomena

*They help identify cloud type and composition, turbulence, lapse rate and icing risk.*

### Rainbow (p.95–96)
- Concentric coloured arcs (violet → red) on a background of raindrops, drizzle or fog, lit by the sun or moon; seen **opposite** the sun/moon, centred on the line through the luminary and observer; a full ring from a hill, tower or aircraft.
- Cause: **two refractions + one total internal reflection** inside each drop. Colours VIBGYOR.
- **Primary bow**: radius **42°**; **red outside, violet inside**.
- **Secondary bow**: radius about **52°** (red inside at 50°, violet outside at 54°); fainter, about twice as broad.

### Halo (p.96)
- Ring round the sun/moon by **refraction through ice crystals** in **cirrostratus (CS)** — a **high** cloud.
- **Small halo: 22° radius** (most common); mostly white, **red on the outside** when well developed; sky inside the ring is darker.
- **Large halo: 46° radius**, fainter.
- Halo → ice crystals predominate → **negligible icing**.

### Corona (p.96–97)
- One to three (rarely more) coloured rings of **small radius (≤ 5°)** round the sun/moon; **red outside, violet/blue inside**; dull colours.
- Formed by **diffraction** (bending of light at the edge of particles) through **mist, fog or thin cloud of very small water drops or ice** — typically **altostratus (AS)**.
- Corona above the freezing level → **moderate icing** possible. Distorted coronas from uneven particle sizes (or a crescent moon).

### Mirage (p.97)
- Light bends towards denser air; density falls with height so rays curve (the rising sun/moon appears slightly higher).
- Occurs when the surface temperature differs markedly from the air above.
- **Inferior (lower) mirage**: over intensely **heated** road, sand, beach, water → **steep lapse rate**.
- **Superior (upper) mirage**: over **snow fields, cold sea** → **inversion**.

### Other phenomena (p.97–98)
- **Bishop's ring**: faint whitish ring, bluish inside, reddish-brown outside, **radius ~22°**; **diffraction by fine dust** high in the atmosphere (often volcanic).
- **Twilight colours**: refraction, scattering, selective absorption at sunrise/sunset.
- **Irisation**: colours/bands parallel to cloud margins (pink predominates), by diffraction.
- **Glory**: coloured rings around **your own shadow** (often the aircraft's shadow) on cloud/fog of small droplets; diffraction (arranged like a corona).
- **Crepuscular rays**: dark bluish streaks radiating from the sun = shadows of clouds at/below the horizon.

### Aurora (p.98–99)
- Light emitted by the high atmosphere, **70–1000 km, peak ~100 km**; streamers, arcs, curtains; greenish-white, red or yellow; shimmering.
- Most common at **high latitudes around the magnetic poles**.
- Cause: **charged particles from magnetic (solar) storms** exciting rarefied gases (like a neon sign). **Impairs radio communication**.
- **Aurora Borealis = Northern Lights** (N hemisphere, generally over NW Greenland); **Aurora Australis = Southern Lights**.

### Atmospheric electricity and lightning (p.99–100)
- Fair weather: **earth negatively charged**; potential gradient ~**100 V/m** directed towards the earth. In a TS it reverses locally (earth to air). Precipitation carries net **positive** charge down; cloud-to-ground flashes carry mainly **negative** charge to ground.
- **Ground discharge (thunderbolt)**: cloud to ground, branching (streak/ribbon lightning). **Ball lightning**: luminous ball **10–20 cm**, moves slowly, ends with an explosion.
- **Cloud discharge (sheet lightning)**: within the cloud. **Air discharge**: cloud to air.
- **St Elmo's fire**: bluish/greenish glow with crackling on an aircraft in **CU/CB** from a strong electric field; weak to moderate.

### Exam traps
- Halo = **refraction**, **ice**, **CS**, **22°**, **red outside**, **negligible icing**, **high** cloud.
- Corona = **diffraction**, **AS**, small radius, red outside.
- Superior mirage = **inversion**; inferior = **steep lapse**.
- Bishop's ring = **dust**, ~22°.

## 11. Precipitation

### Types and sizes (p.102)

| Type | Code | Size / description |
|---|---|---|
| **Drizzle** | DZ | Very small drops, **0.2–0.5 mm** |
| **Rain** | RA | **0.5–5 mm** |
| **Shower** | SH | Liquid or solid drops **> 5 mm** (from convective cloud) |
| **Hail** | GR | Balls/lumps of ice **5–50 mm or more** (a 1 kg hailstone has been reported) |
| **Snow** | SN | Assembly of ice crystals (flakes) |
| **Sleet** | — | **Mixture of rain and snow** (wet snow) |
| **Ice pellets** | PL | Small transparent ice particles, spherical/irregular (< 5 mm) |
| Snow grains / ice crystals | SG / IC | Also precipitation |

- To fall, drops/crystals must grow large enough to overcome the cloud's **vertical currents**.

### How precipitation forms (p.102–103)
- **Bergeron ice-crystal theory** (**cold clouds**, tops well above the freezing level): supercooled drops and ice crystals coexist; **SVP over water > over ice**, so drops evaporate and the vapour deposits on the crystals; crystals also collect supercooled drops that freeze on them (riming) → fall as snow or melt into rain.
- **Coalescence theory** (**warm clouds**, tops below the freezing level — tropical showers): small droplets collide and merge (helped by vertical currents and the wake behind falling drops) → large drops.
- **Giant nucleus theory** (**maritime areas**): large **salt** nuclei from sea spray absorb vapour, form big drops and start coalescence, so even shallow clouds rain.

### Cloud → precipitation (Table 11.1)

| Cloud | Precipitation |
|---|---|
| ST | **Drizzle** |
| AS | Rain and snow |
| NS | **Continuous** precipitation |
| TCU, CB | Rain, **showers, hail**, snow |
| Fair-weather CU, AC, CI, CC | None |
| Thick CS | Snow at high/medium levels |

### Snow, sleet and terms (p.104)
- Snow or sleet when the surface temperature is **below ~4°C** and the mean temperature between cloud base and the surface is **below 0°C**.
- **Cloud burst**: very heavy rain/showers over an area in a short time. **Flash flood**: sudden rise of rivers/streams.
- **Rainy day**: **≥ 2.5 mm** in a day.

### Diurnal and seasonal variation (p.104)
- **Tropics**: convective → rain mostly in the **afternoon**; **summer > winter**.
- **Coasts** (land/sea breeze) and **valleys / NE India** (katabatic/anabatic): maximum at **night and early morning**.
- **Temperate latitudes**: depressions and fronts → maximum in **winter**. **NW India / J&K / western Himalaya** follow this because of **Western Disturbances** (occluded fronts, frequent in winter).
- **Rain shadow** = lee side of a range, e.g. areas **east of the Western Ghats**.

### Cloud seeding (p.104–105)
- Injecting nucleating agents (**silver iodide, common salt, dry ice (solid CO₂), potassium chloride**) → ice nuclei start the Bergeron process; small clouds grow or rain within **15–20 minutes**.
- May rob another area of its natural rain. Uses: firefighting, ending drought, harvests, draining clouds, fog dispersal (heating or seeding clears fog briefly for take-off/landing).

### Classification of rainfall (24 h)

| Category | Amount |
|---|---|
| Light | < 7.5 mm |
| Moderate | 7.5–35.5 mm |
| Rather heavy | 35.6–64 mm |
| Heavy | 65–84.9 mm |
| Very heavy | ≥ 85 mm |
| Exceptionally heavy | ≥ 250 mm |

## 12. Ice Accretion

### Types of icing (p.107–108)
- Icing alters aerodynamics, can cause loss of control; de-icing gives only partial protection.
- **Airframe icing** and **engine icing**.

| Ice | How | Look / behaviour | Hazard |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hoar frost** | **Sublimation (deposition)** of WV onto an airframe **below the frost point**, in **clear air**; parked aircraft on cold nights or rapid descent from cold altitude into warm moist air | Feathery ice crystals | Not serious; clears as the aircraft warms / vibrates (but must be removed before take-off) |
| **Opaque rime** | **Small supercooled droplets** freeze instantly on impact in cloud above the freezing level | White, **light, porous**, air trapped; on leading edges | Light, but spoils the wing profile; easily removed |
| **Clear ice / glaze** (translucent rime) | **Large supercooled drops** freeze slowly, spread back before freezing | Glassy, smooth, uneven when built up; **sticky**, heavy; breaks off in dangerous lumps | **Most serious**: weight, aerodynamics, vibration (unequal loading) |
| Mixed | Wide range of drop sizes, 0 to −40°C | Rime + clear | Varies |

### Temperature ranges (except CB) (Table 12.1)

| Severity | Temperature |
|---|---|
| **Severe** | **0 to −7°C** |
| Moderate | −7 to −12°C |
| Light | −12 to −20°C |
| Very light | −20 to −40°C |

- Probability falls below −20°C as supercooled water decreases; maximum liquid water (and ice formation) around **−15°C** (book key).

### Icing by cloud (p.108)
- **CI, CS, CC**: ice crystals → **negligible** icing.
- **AS, NS**: supercooled drops + ice → **light to moderate**.
- **AC**: light to moderate; **severe possible over mountains**.
- **TCU**: light to severe up to at least the **−20°C level**.
- **CB**: light to severe up to the **−20°C level**; below that severe icing not significant.
- Book question ranges: **rime** in ST, SC, AC, CU, NS at −10 to −40°C (CB −20 to −40°C); **glaze** in AS, NS, AC, TCU/CB between 0 and −20°C, in **freezing rain below a warm front**, especially after a rapid descent from colder air.

### Effects of airframe icing (p.109)
- Alters aerodynamics, **adds weight, reduces lift**, **raises stalling speed**, **increases drag**.
- Uneven ice on **propellers** → loss of efficiency, vibration, power loss.
- **Control hinges / trim tabs** may jam.
- **Pitot icing → false ASI**; **aerial icing → poor communication**.

### Engine icing (p.109)
- **Impact icing**: supercooled drops hit the air intake → restricts airflow → power loss.
- **Carburettor icing**: pressure drop through the **venturi (choke)** and past the throttle butterfly → adiabatic cooling, plus **fuel evaporation** cooling → ice even in **clear air at +30°C**. Serious range **+30°C to −10°C**. Below −10°C negligible unless liquid water present; **unlikely when RH < 60%**.

### Freezing level over India (pressure levels, Table 12.2)

| Season | North | Central | South |
|---|---|---|---|
| NE monsoon (winter) | 700 hPa | 600 hPa | 600 hPa |
| Pre-monsoon | 650 hPa | 600 hPa | 550 hPa |
| SW monsoon | 550 hPa | 500 hPa | 500 hPa |
| Post-monsoon | 550 hPa | 600 hPa | 650 hPa |

### Exam traps
- Small drops → **rime**; large drops → **glaze**; clear-air deposition → **hoar frost**.
- Most dangerous = **glaze (clear) ice**. Severe band **0 to −7°C**.
- Carburettor icing possible at **+30°C**; unlikely below **60% RH**.
- Icing **increases** stall speed.

## 13. Thunderstorm

### What it is (p.112)
- A **thunderstorm (TS)** = weather system from strong convection in a **CB**, with **lightning and thunder**; often sudden cool strong squalls, dark sky, sharp showers, sometimes hail/snow/sleet, sometimes no precipitation, occasionally warm winds. Can cause floods, forest fires, tornadoes, waterspouts, microbursts. One of the **most hazardous** phenomena for aviation.
- **Reported when thunder is heard**. Also called electrical storm, lightning storm, thundershower. In lines → **squall line / rain band**.
- CB cells **1–10 km** across; clusters separated by narrow cloud-filled lanes.
- **Severe TS**: locally damaging winds, frequent lightning, or large hail.

### Types (p.112–113)
- **Air-mass (heat) TS**: intense surface heating + convergence in a low; also cold air over a warm sea. Random, relatively weak; **afternoons over plains**; **night/morning in valleys and hills**.
- **Frontal TS**: mainly at **cold fronts** (occasionally warm fronts); **more violent** than air-mass TS; any time of day or night. A line ahead of a cold front = **squall line**.
- **Steady-state TS**: fronts, converging winds, troughs aloft force ascent; precipitation falls outside the updraught; lasts **several hours**.
- **Mesoscale Convective Complex (MCC)**: near-circular cluster of interacting TS over a huge area (~1000× an air-mass TS); new cells keep forming → life **6–24 h**; severe weather, tornadoes, flash floods; near fronts and in the **ITCZ**.
- **Mesoscale weather**: local phenomena such as TS, DS, land/sea breeze, katabatic wind.

### Classification by structure (p.113)
- **Single cell**: one updraught; rarely severe; summer heating (or winter cold fronts).
- **Multi-cell cluster**: cells last ~20 min each, the cluster for hours; near mountains, cold fronts, troughs.
- **Multi-cell line = squall line**: along/ahead of cold fronts; hundreds of km; heavy rain, hail, lightning, very strong winds, isolated tornadoes. A very powerful one = **derecho**. Also in outer rain bands of tropical cyclones.
- **Supercell**: separate up/downdraughts (vertical wind shear) and a **rotating updraught (mesocyclone)**; can reach the lower stratosphere; **hail up to 10 cm**, winds > 130 km/h, flash floods; **most tornadoes**; up to **24 km** wide.

### Conditions and triggers (p.114)
- (a) **Steep lapse rate** — steeper than SALR through a layer **at least 5–6 km deep**, cloud reaching well above 0°C level. (b) **High humidity** low down and aloft (dry air aloft evaporates the rising cloud). (c) **Trigger** to lift air to saturation.
- Triggers: **insolation (convection)**, **frontal lifting**, **convergence**, **orographic lifting**, **radiational/katabatic cooling** (night TS in valleys).

### Life cycle (p.114–116)

| Stage | Draughts | What happens | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cumulus** | **Updraughts throughout** (up to **30 m/s ≈ 60 kt**) | Lifting → condensation → latent heat → CU grows into CB; entrainment from sides and base; low pressure under the cloud; drops grow, held up | — |
| **Mature** | **Up- and downdraughts side by side** | Top hits the tropopause → **anvil** (false cirrus); precipitation drags air down; evaporation cools it further → cold **downdraught** hits the ground as **squall**; leading edge = **first gust / gust front** (like a mini cold front) lifting warm air for new cells; **lightning, turbulence, hail** | **20–40 min** (longer if shear makes a supercell) |
| **Dissipating** | **Downdraughts only** | Updraught stops, growth stops, precipitation dwindles, cloud dissipates | — |

- Most violent stage = **mature**.
- **Roll cloud**: cylindrical, appears to roll; **behind the first gust**, **detached** from the CB; seldom severe.
- **Shelf cloud**: wedge-shaped with flat base, at the gust front, **attached** to the CB; associated with **severe** weather.
- **Severe TS are tilted** by vertical wind shear, so precipitation/downdraught falls away from the updraught → bigger, longer-lived storms. **Upper jet streams** help (shear, tilt).
- Movement steered by the **700–500 hPa winds**.

### Intensity (p.117)
- **Light**: faint thunder, lightning at long intervals.
- **Moderate**: loud thunder, frequent lightning, moderate/heavy showers, light hail; max wind **15–40 kt**.
- **Severe**: almost continuous thunder and lightning, heavy rain/showers, maybe hail; wind **> 40 kt**.

### Diurnal and seasonal variation (p.117)
- **Plains: afternoon** (die out in the evening). **Valleys and foothills: night and early morning**. **Sea: night**.
- Mid-latitudes: land TS most in **summer**; frontal TS more in **winter**.
- First gust → **sudden pressure rise** (pressure jump).

### Hazards (p.117–120)
- **Turbulence** (with icing, the most serious): inside (between up- and downdraughts), between cells, **10–20 km around the CB** in clear air, **25–30 km downwind**, thousands of feet **above the top**, and below the base to the ground. Gust loads can exceed structural limits; over-correcting causes structural failure.
- Turbulence felt in the cabin: **light** (slight strain on belts, walking difficult), **moderate** (definite strain, objects dislodged, walking very difficult), **severe** (thrown violently against belts, walking/serving impossible).
- **Wind shear**: outflow up to ~2,000 ft; landing aircraft gets a **headwind then a tailwind**; wind can change **90° or more** in a few hundred feet. Strong **vertical** wind shear is needed for a **severe** TS.
- **Squall**: usually up to 40 kt, can be **100 kt**; strongest **ahead** of the storm.
- **Draughts**: updraughts stronger and deeper (can suck up gliders); downdraughts strongest from mid-cloud down; height changes **3,000–5,000 ft**; > 1 km wide.
- **Gusts**: worst near the up/downdraught boundary; abrupt attitude changes.
- **Icing**: possible **0 to −30°C**; **clear ice worst in the first 10,000 ft above the freezing level** (large supercooled drops); rime higher up; turbine flame-out, carburettor icing.
- **Hail**: starts as an ice crystal, grows by **collision with supercooled drops** while tossed up and down; **onion layers** of clear (near freezing level) and rime ice. Worst from the freezing level to ~**25,000 ft**; damaging hail up to **45,000 ft** and **under the anvil** (even in clear air). Max ~5.5 in; 4.5 kg (China), 3.4 kg (India) recorded. Large hail dents airframes, breaks windscreens, rips de-icer boots.
- **Heavy showers**: very low visibility, **hydroplaning**, engine power loss from water ingestion, noise; fractostratus below 1,000 ft.
- **Lightning**: needs ~3×10⁶ V/m in clear air (~1×10⁶ V/m in cloud); usually not catastrophic (aircraft bonding) but burns/holes, **compass errors**, damaged electronics, radio noise, **temporary blinding**. Most likely between **+10°C and −10°C**. Thunder = shock wave from the heated air.
- **Noise**, **darkness and disorientation** (watch the attitude indicator).
- **Instrument errors**: pressure falls under a growing/mature CB → **altimeter over-reads**; pitot blocked by water → wrong ASI.

### Radar detection (p.120)
- Weather radars (ground or airborne) track TS. Precipitation detection: wavelength **3–20 cm**; measurement typically **10 cm**.

| Band | K | X | C | S | L |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wavelength (cm) | 0.86 | **3** | 4 (book) | **10** | 20 |

### Air pockets, downbursts, microbursts (p.121–123)
- **Air pocket**: local up- or downdraught from warm/cool pockets or high/low pressure.
- **Downburst**: intense damaging downdraught **> 4 km** across.
- **Microburst < 4 km**; **macroburst ≥ 4 km**. Spreads into rings 2–4 km across, over 600 m deep. **Dry** (under CB or anvil, virga) or **wet** (in heavy rain).
- Life **1–5 min** (a series can last 15–20 min). Cause: evaporative cooling of falling rain in dry air.
- Downdraught **> 75 km/h**; horizontal winds commonly **90 km/h** (can exceed 200 km/h).
- Produced by: CB (~**5% of TS**), heavy rain (high probability), virga (medium).
- Three stages: **contact**, **outburst**, **cushion**. Low down, an aircraft meets **headwind → downdraught → tailwind**; recognition time only **5–15 s** → avoid flying under CB, through TS/DS, in heavy rain or under virga; **no take-off or landing during TS/DS**.
- Effects: increasing headwind → above the glide path/overshoot; increasing tailwind → undershoot; vertical shear → AoA fluctuations, stick shaker; crosswind shear → roll/yaw; turbulence masks airspeed changes; heavy rain.

### Dust storms (p.123–124)
- Pre-monsoon N India: surface ≥ 35°C, very unstable desert air with a little moisture → CB that can't build high (dry aloft); rain evaporates before reaching ground; violent updraughts lift dust/sand **above 10,000 ft**; visibility **50 m or less**; approaches like a wall. Called **Andhi** in N India. If humid aloft, light showers follow and visibility improves. Shorter life than TS; mostly summer afternoons; weaker radar echoes.

| Dust storm | Wind | Visibility |
|---|---|---|
| Light | up to 21 kt | 500 to < 1000 m |
| Moderate | 22–40 kt | 200 to < 500 m |
| Severe | > 40 kt | < 200 m |

### Nor'westers (p.124)
- Violent **pre-monsoon (March–May)** TS over **West Bengal, Bihar, Jharkhand (Chota Nagpur), Chhattisgarh, Odisha, NE states, Bangladesh**; usually approach from the **north-west**; locally **Kalbaishakhi**. Strong squalls, hail, rarely tornadoes; often in lines. **Afternoon/evening**. Originate over the **Chota Nagpur** plateau. Avoid them.

### Tornado, waterspout, dust devil (p.124–125)
- **Tornado**: rotating funnel sucked up into a CB base; winds **> 150 kt**; core pressure drop of hundreds of hPa (condensation makes it visible); diameter metres to **~200 m**; life minutes to over an hour; narrow path of devastation; **hook-shaped radar echo**; rare in India (**New Delhi, April 1978**). Fujita scale (Appendix B).
- **Waterspout**: tornado over the sea (can cause "rain of fish").
- **Dust devil**: whirl over a hot spot; up to **~2 km** high, **~10 m** across; short-lived (willy-willy).

### Exam traps
- Mature stage = **up- AND downdraughts**; cumulus = updraughts only; dissipating = downdraughts only.
- Norwesters: **Mar–May, afternoons, Bengal/Bihar/Odisha/Assam, from Chota Nagpur**.
- Microburst **< 4 km, 1–5 min**; macroburst **≥ 4 km**.
- Lightning most likely **+10 to −10°C**. Hail grows by **collision with supercooled drops**.
- Altimeter **over-reads** under a CB.

## 14. Air Masses, Fronts and Western Disturbances

### Air masses (p.129–130)
- **Air mass** = large body of air (thousands of km²) with nearly **uniform horizontal and vertical distribution of temperature (density) and moisture**. Moves with the wind and is **modified** by the surfaces it crosses.
- **Source regions**: where air stagnates over a uniform surface — **high-pressure areas with slack gradients and light winds**: the **poles** and the **subtropical highs**.
- Classified by **temperature** (from latitude: **Polar / Tropical**) and **moisture** (from surface: **continental / maritime**).

| Air mass | Symbol | Source | Properties |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arctic | A | Polar regions | Extremely cold, very dry in winter (more humid in summer) |
| Polar continental | **Pc** | Sub-polar land | Cold, dry; warms moving south |
| Polar maritime | **Pm** | Sub-polar/Arctic oceans | Cold, **moist** |
| Tropical continental | **Tc** | Subtropical high over land | **Hot, dry** |
| Tropical maritime | **Tm** | Oceanic subtropical highs | Warm, **moist** |
| Equatorial (maritime) | **Em** | Equatorial/tropical seas | Hot, very humid (**SW monsoon = Em**) |

### Air masses over India (p.130–131)
- **Tm**: from the N Pacific subtropical high via SE Asia in the monsoon; hot, humid, high dew point; excellent visibility; fair diurnal range; CU with showers/TS. Over **NE India and along the Himalaya** in summer.
- **Tc**: **most common over India, especially winter**; from the **Siberian high**; cold, dry, fair weather, **poor visibility**. Over **NW India** in summer.
- **Em**: from the Indian Ocean (crosses the equator); south of **25°N** in the monsoon; very humid, sultry, small diurnal range, good visibility except in showers.
- WDs draw **Tm air from the Arabian Sea and Bay of Bengal**. After a WD in winter, **Pc** incursion → **cold wave**.
- Summer: equatorial low moves north, sits over the Gangetic plains as the **monsoon trough** (Em to the south; Tc NW, Tm NE to the north).

### Fronts (p.131–132)
- **Front** = narrow **transition zone between two air masses of contrasting density** (temperature/humidity); change of temperature, wind, cloud and precipitation across it.
- **Frontal surface** = the sloping boundary; **front** = where it meets the ground.
- **Frontogenesis** = formation/strengthening; **frontolysis** = weakening/dissipation. Weather only with significant density contrast.

### Cold front (p.132–133)
- **Cold air overtaking warm air**; warm air forced up the cold wedge; **CU/CB**; average slope **steep ~1:50**.
- **Kata cold front**: slope **~1:120**, ~**20 kt**; warm air **sinks** aloft → limited vertical growth; ST, NS, AS + CU/CB; widespread rain; showers ahead and just behind; fast ones can produce a **line squall 100–300 km ahead**.
- **Ana cold front**: slope **~1:50**, **> 30 kt**; warm air **rises**, unstable; **CU/CB**, violent TS, hail, short showers in a **100–150 km** belt; **sky clears rapidly after passage**; isolated showers behind.
- Passage: wind **veers sharply, squally**; temperature/dew point **fall**; pressure **rises**; visibility **improves** (good after passage).

### Warm front (p.133–134)
- **Warm air overtaking cold air**; slope **shallow 1:150 to 1:180**; speed **10–15 kt**.
- **Kata (stable) warm front**: **CI, CS ~1000 km ahead** → AS, NS **400–500 km** ahead → steady, increasing, prolonged precipitation → just ahead: **drizzle, low ST, frontal fog**; **freezing rain/fog (FZRA/FZFG) ahead** in cold air. After passage: fog clears, partly clear, **warmer and humid**.
- **Ana (unstable) warm front**: CU/CB embedded in the layer cloud → bursts of heavy rain.
- Cloud sequence: **CI → CS → AS → NS → ST**. Visibility **poor ahead and during**. On approach: pressure falls steadily, **wind backs and freshens**, temperature **rises** after passage, wind **veers** at the front.

### Occlusions and others (p.134–135)
- Cold fronts move about **twice** as fast as warm fronts → catch up → **occluded front**.
- **Cold occlusion**: air behind the cold front **colder** than the cool air ahead → undercuts and lifts both; warm-front weather then cold-front showers; **common in summer**.
- **Warm occlusion**: air behind **less cold** than the air ahead → rides over the warm front; warm-front-type weather; **winter**, less common.
- **Stationary front**: barely moves; winds parallel to it in opposite directions on either side (e.g. polar front).
- **Polar front**: between polar easterlies and mid-latitude westerlies (~60°), active in segments. **Arctic front**: between arctic and polar air. **Mediterranean front**: Pc Europe vs Tc N Africa (winter).
- Fronts belong to **extra-tropical cyclones**.

### Fronts in India and Western Disturbances (p.136–137)
- India has no fronts except extreme NW; **WDs are of frontal origin but arrive occluded**.
- **WD** = low or trough moving **W → E, north of 20°N**, causing weather. Two or more closed isobars (2 hPa) = **Western Depression**. Upper troughs above 500 hPa = **troughs in westerlies**.
- Originate over the **Mediterranean, Caspian and Black Seas**; **extra-tropical**; intensify over NW India (orography, Arabian Sea moisture).
- **Induced low / induced cyclonic circulation** may form to the **south** of a WD.
- Frequency: **5–7 per month in winter**, nil in the monsoon, reappear from **October**.
- Movement: from Pakistan/Rajasthan **NE towards the W Himalaya**, some along the foothills east, a few east across UP, MP, Bihar to NE India; **250–300 km/day**.
- Weather: ~50% confined to **J&K, HP, Punjab, Haryana, Uttarakhand, Sikkim, Bhutan, Arunachal**; otherwise also UP, MP, Bihar, Odisha, WB, NE. Maximum cloud/precipitation along the **foothills**. **Widespread fog and cold waves after passage** (fog lifts by forenoon or lasts days).
- Hazards: low cloud, poor visibility, rain, TS, hail, icing; afterwards widespread fog; pre-monsoon: dust haze and DS over NW India, TS over northern plains.
- **Troughs in westerlies**: upper-air waves moving W → E with divergence ahead; when behind a surface system, they enhance weather: severe TS/DS, Nor'westers, heavy rain.

### Exam traps
- Polar maritime = sea at high latitude; Tc = hot and dry; Em = hot and wet (SW monsoon).
- Line squalls form **ahead** of cold fronts; cold front moves at **double** a warm front's speed.
- FZRA/FZFG **ahead** of a warm front. Precipitation ceases after a **warm** front passes.
- WD = **occluded front**; maximum in **winter**; induced lows to the **south**.

## 15. Jet Streams

### Definition (p.140)
- **Jet stream** = strong, narrow current of wind along a quasi-horizontal axis in the **upper troposphere**; in **both hemispheres all year**; wavy, serpentine path.
- Size: **thousands of km long, hundreds of km wide, a few km (often < 5 km) deep**.
- Strong **vertical and horizontal wind shear**; vertical shear ≈ **5 m/s per km** (vertical shear is **stronger** than horizontal).
- WMO lower limit of core speed: **30 m/s = 60 kt**.
- **Axis** = line of maximum speed; **core** = tube around it. Speed along the axis is **not uniform**: centres of maximum speed = **jet streaks** (located near **ridges** in a wavy jet). Where speed drops below 60 kt = **breaks**. Jets can split or merge.

### Types (p.141)

| Jet | Direction | Height | Where / when | Speeds |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Arctic jet** | W | 7–8 km | Arctic, near polar tropopause | — |
| **Polar Front Jet (PFJ)** | W (NW/SW at times) | ~250 hPa, **9–12 km** (30,000–39,000 ft) | Above the surface polar front, at the mid-latitude tropopause break; **30°N in winter, 60°N in summer**; circles Antarctica in SH | **80–100 kt** in winter (stronger in winter) |
| **Sub-Tropical Jet (STJ)** | **W** | **10–16 km** (≈12 km over India, ~200 hPa) | Above the subtropical high belt; **~30°N winter (strong)**, **north of 35°N in summer (weak)**; southernmost **22°N in February** | **100 to > 215 kt** |
| **Tropical (Easterly) Jet (TJ/TEJ)** | **E** | **15–16 km**, ~150 hPa (100 hPa) | Monsoon; axis **~13°N (Chennai)** in July–Aug; only over **Asia and Africa** | **60–80 kt**, max ~150 kt |
| Stratospheric jet | W in winter, E in summer | > 20 km | Arctic and Antarctic | — |

### STJ over India (p.141–143)
- Appears over N India in **October** after monsoon withdrawal, moves south through winter; mean **~27°N at ~12 km**; southernmost **22°N in February**; over India **Oct–May (~8 months)**; leaves after May.
- Mean **100 kt**, max ~200 kt; lowest **60–70 kt in Oct and May**, highest **100–120 kt in January** (Jan–Feb > Dec).
- Level of max wind a few km below the tropopause (**3–4 km below**, in the tropical/middle tropopause break at 300–200 hPa).
- May **split around the Himalaya** and rejoin over China. **Layered structure**: often two layers of max wind **to the south** of the core. **Strengthens downstream** by 10–15 kt (Jodhpur → Guwahati).
- South of mean position in deep westerly troughs, north of it in ridges.
- Caused by a strong N–S temperature gradient below the jet (surface–300 hPa, warm south) which reverses above; **at and near the core the temperature gradient is very small**.
- **Vertical shear greater above the core** than below. **Horizontal shear greater to the north (cold/left)**: winds fall ~35 kt/100 NM to the south, ~100 kt/100 NM to the north.
- With a WD the STJ strengthens (130–150 kt) and moves south; divergence at the **left entrance and right exit** plus a surface low → severe TS.

### Tropical Easterly Jet (p.143)
- Monsoon easterlies south of 25°N at 100 hPa; strongest at ~16 km (~80 kt, max 150 kt). Seen **June–August**, gone by September. Reversed N→S temperature gradient (sun north of 20°N, Tibetan plateau heating) turns low-level westerlies into easterlies above 500 hPa. **Much weaker shear than the STJ**.
- **Low-level jet (LLJ)**: strong winds at **1–3 km** in the SW monsoon over the Peninsula; continuation of the **Somali jet** off East Africa; enhances monsoon rain.

### Jet stream weather and CAT (p.144)
- Cause: large **horizontal temperature gradient** (bigger contrast → stronger jet).
- Clouds: extensive sheets of high cloud with sharp edges parallel to the axis **on the warm side** (CS, CC); **CI more on the cold side**; streaks/rolls of CI along its length; haze on the warm side if dry. Recognised by **streaks of cirrus**.
- **CAT** around jet boundaries from strong shear; worse in developing, fast-moving jets. **Strongest just below the axis on the cold (low-pressure) side** (secondary maximum above) = **left looking downstream in the NH**. Worst with strong winds, **sharply curved** jets, and **over/lee of mountains**.

## 16. Clear Air Turbulence and Mountain Waves

*(Book chapters 16 and 17.)*

### Clear Air Turbulence (p.147–148)
- **CAT** = bumpiness at high altitude **in cloud-free air** (or in cirrus), where air moving at very different speeds meets.
- Most susceptible: **upper troposphere ~23,000–39,000 ft, near the tropopause**; mostly near **jet streams**; enhanced by instability; also near mountains lower down.
- Needs **marked horizontal or vertical wind shear** and a **steep lapse rate** (just below the tropopause near a jet core). In the lower stratosphere a sharp decrease of wind with height can give temporary CAT.
- Most CAT on the **fringes** of a jet (not in the core) and near **upper frontal zones** with strong temperature contrast.
- Strong winds across mountains amplify gravity waves into the stratosphere → CAT; **mountain wave + jet = severe CAT**; more frequent on the **lee** of high ground. Also downstream of **CB tops** (lenticular trains behind CB).
- **India**: highest frequency **Oct–May over N, central and NE India (STJ)**; over **S India in July–August (TEJ)**. Mostly weak–moderate; **severe Dec–Feb** (STJ peak). Patchy: ~**120 km N–S × 250 km E–W**, ~**2,000 ft** deep (can be deeper).
- Hazards: discomfort, control difficulty, sudden onset, amplified by mountain waves.
- Avoidance: keep away from the jet; **climb/descend or turn N/S** out of it; it is often clear about **1 km above the tropopause**.

### Mountain waves — definition and conditions (p.149)
- **Mountain (lee) wave** = oscillations on the **lee side** of high ground due to disturbance of horizontal flow; reach great heights and far downwind.
- Wind: flow across the ridge **within 30° of perpendicular**; at least **7 m/s (small mountains)** or **15 m/s (large mountains)**; deep steady flow, **increasing with height** in a constant direction.
- Stability: **stable layer below/at ridge level, with less stable air above** (a stable layer sandwiched).

### Vertical currents and clouds (p.150–151)
- Alternate up and down currents; **sinking immediately downwind of the ridge**; **10–25 m/s** over big mountains. Flying parallel to a ridge in a down current → continuous height loss. Keep big terrain clearance.
- Clouds (appear **stationary**, forming upwind and dissolving downwind): **cap cloud** (on the summit; swept down as a **Föhn wall**), **lenticular** (lens-shaped, at successive wave crests, a few thousand ft above crest; ragged edges = turbulence), **rotor/roll cloud** (under crests, **strongest in the first crest**, level with or slightly above the ridge), also ST, SC, AS, CI, and **nacreous** in the upper stratosphere.
- **Rotor streaming**: strong low-level wind that weakens/reverses aloft → violent rotors on the lee slope.

### Turbulence and altimetry (p.151)
- Not always turbulent; changes from smooth to violent **abruptly, without warning**. Worst: **rotor clouds** (like a violent TS; **2–4 g common, 7 g exceeded**), also cap cloud and Föhn wall. Several ranges in a row → violent turbulence.
- **Altimeter**: rapid height changes beat the capsule's response (**hysteresis**); plus temperature errors (colder than ISA → reads too high). Don't trust it for terrain clearance.

### Variation and extent (p.152)
- **Evening waves**: after sunset low-level stability + weaker wind → waves behind small/medium hills, until morning heating.
- More frequent in **winter** (steady winds increasing with height, suitable stability).
- Extend **100–200 km downwind** and into the stratosphere; wavelength **≈ 10 km or more**.
- Hazards: strong up/downdraughts (height-keeping, control), severe turbulence to great heights (structural), large unexpected winds, more icing, altimeter errors. Expect moderate–severe turbulence **20–30 NM** downwind.

### Exam traps
- Jet core minimum **60 kt**; vertical shear **5 m/s/km**; STJ **westerly**, TEJ **easterly** at **13°N, 15–16 km, Jun–Aug**; STJ over India **Oct–May**, southernmost **22°N in Feb**.
- STJ strengthens **downstream**; vertical shear greater **above** the core.
- Recognise a jet by **cirrus streaks**. Strongest CAT: **cold side, just below the axis** (left looking downstream, NH).
- Mountain waves: **30°**, **7 / 15 m/s**, **stable below**; worst turbulence in **rotor** clouds.

## 18. Tropical Systems

### Tropical discontinuities (p.154)
- In the tropics air-mass temperature contrasts are weak, so **fronts cannot be drawn**; instead lines of discontinuity in the **wind field** (upper-air charts) and cloud pattern = **tropical discontinuities / convergence zones**.

### ITCZ (p.154–155)
- **Inter-Tropical Convergence Zone** = narrow zone where Tm/Tc air from the north meets Tm/Em air from the south (converging trades). Usually along the **equatorial trough** (shallow trough in the doldrums). Also called the **equatorial front**.
- Moves with the sun: up to **~20°N in summer**, down to **Australia in winter** (so met when flying India → SE Asia/Australia in winter). Encircles the globe.
- **Active portions ~75–150 km** long with heavy showers and CB; inactive parts give scattered showers from large CU/CB.
- Rarely pushed into the south Bay of Bengal in winter → can start cyclones. In the rainy season the ITCZ coincides with the **monsoon trough**.

### Easterly waves (p.155–156)
- Troughs in the tropical easterlies, forming on the ITCZ at the Tm/Em boundary; **weak at the surface, better developed at 500 hPa and above**; wavelength **~1500 km**; move **east → west** at **20–25 km/h** (book text "W to E" is a slip; they travel westward with the easterlies). **Weather mostly in the rear** of the trough.
- India: in winter seen first in the **Andaman Sea**, move west across the south peninsula into the Arabian Sea (~**15 kt over sea, ~10 kt over land**); if the ITCZ enters the south Bay → 3–4 days of disturbed weather over the Bay Islands, Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Arabian Sea islands.
- Pre-monsoon: more easterly waves → help **pre-monsoon cyclones**. Monsoon: Pacific waves cross SE Asia to the head Bay; some become **depressions** moving NW. Post-monsoon: depressions/cyclones in the central & south Bay and Arabian Sea.

### Peninsular discontinuity (dry line) (p.156)
- Over **peninsular India south of 23°N** in **April–May**; between hot dry **Tc** air from N India and cooler moist air from the Bay; **central peninsula**.
- Afternoon TS, sometimes hail, strong NW squalls, heavy showers; stationary **3–4 days** before dissipating.

### Surge and shear line (p.157–158)
- **Surge (pulse)**: sudden strengthening of the wind **within the same air mass**; air arrives faster than it leaves → **velocity convergence** → ascent, CU, showers, squalls. Common over the **Arabian Sea in the monsoon**: ~**40 kt** behind (west of) the surge line vs **10–15 kt** ahead. Moves with the wind to the head Bay; activates the ITCZ, favours depressions.
- **Shear line**: in the same air mass wind speed falls sharply S → N (or vice versa); friction → small-scale ascent; if deep → thick **ST, AS, NS** up to **~500 km** across; with a fresh surge, CU/CB embedded at its leading edge.
- Surge and shear lines are features of a **vigorous monsoon**.

### Classification of lows (winds over sea; over land at 900 m) (p.158)

| System | Wind |
|---|---|
| Low | **< 17 kt** |
| Depression | **17–27 kt** |
| Deep depression | **28–33 kt** |
| Cyclonic storm (CS) | **34–47 kt** |
| Severe cyclonic storm (SCS) | **48–63 kt** |
| Very severe cyclonic storm (VSCS) | **64 – < 120 kt** |
| Super cyclone | **≥ 120 kt** |

### Monsoon depressions (p.158–159)
- Depression = low with **two or more closed isobars at 2 hPa**; surface winds **17–33 kt**; isobars elliptical.
- Form over the **head Bay of Bengal (N of 18°N) in Jun–Aug**, move N/NW/NE inland; in **September around 14°N**; Arabian Sea ones ~5° off the coast north of 12°N moving north; land depressions mostly NE India. Help advance the monsoon.
- Life **3–5 days**; move faster over the **plains**.
- **Worst weather in the SW sector** (steady heavy rain in a ~400 km belt); at **recurvature** the rain shifts to the **N–NE sector**; showers in the rear with strong S–SW winds.
- Upper circulation to ~**300 hPa**; **slopes SW with height** (surface over Kolkata → over Visakhapatnam at 300 hPa).
- Fair weather over **Assam** during the monsoon → a depression is forming/intensifying over the head Bay.

### Tropical cyclones (TC / TRS) (p.159–162)
- **Non-frontal** low with many TS, strong winds, heavy rain; over warm seas **> 26°C**, **5°–25°** latitude (no Coriolis below 5°; too cold above 25°).
- TC = max sustained wind **≥ 34 kt** (IMD: highest **3-minute** mean surface wind at 10 m; some countries 10 min). Called hurricane, typhoon, tropical storm, cyclonic storm, TRS, cyclone.
- Effects: damaging winds, torrential rain, high waves, **storm surge**, tornadoes; but relieve drought and carry heat poleward.
- **Formation needs**: (a) **warm sea > 26°C to ~60 m depth**; (b) **high RH up to ~7000 m**; (c) **instability** and enough **Coriolis**; (d) **very little vertical wind shear** (< 20 kt between 850 and 200 hPa).

| Part | Features |
|---|---|
| **Eye** | **Light winds, often clear sky, lowest pressure**; warmer by 0–2°C at surface, ≥ 10°C at 12 km (subsidence); inversion 1–3 km; diameter **30–65 km** |
| **Eye wall / inner storm area** | **50–150 km** wide; winds **> 64 kt**, violent squalls, torrential rain, thick CB **wall clouds**; **most hazardous**; steepest pressure gradient (concentric eye walls in some) |
| **Outer storm area** | to ~**400 km**; gales (≥ 34 kt), occasional squalls, **spiral bands** |
| **Edge** | weak winds, decreasing cloud |

- Size **150–800 km**; moves **300–500 km/day (15–20 km/h)**; moves **west up to ~25°N/S, then recurves east**.
- Central pressure **1000–900 hPa**; usually 5–6 hPa below surroundings (up to ~70 hPa less); gradient very steep within **100 km**.
- **Worst weather: right-forward sector in the NH** (left-forward in SH); winds stronger in the right half by about twice the storm's speed.
- Circulation to ~**40,000 ft**, axis near-vertical; hurricane-force winds up to ~7,000 ft in the inner area.
- Flying: spiral bands; unbroken cloud mass round the eye; bases to the surface, CB tops **18–20 km**; severe turbulence.

### Indian cyclones (p.162–163)
- Radius **50–2000 km** (average 300–600 km). Damage from wind, rain, **storm surge and tides**.
- Short sea track → usually immature at landfall → **less intense** than typhoons/hurricanes; life **3–5 days** (max 6–7; rarely 2 weeks).
- Pre-/post-monsoon Bay storms may recurve N/NE into **West Bengal, Bangladesh, Myanmar**; Arabian Sea storms into **Maharashtra, Gujarat**; low-latitude Bay storms cross **Tamil Nadu/S Andhra (Oct–Nov)**, can re-emerge into the Arabian Sea.
- Examples: **Phailin** (VSCS, Odisha, 12 Oct 2013, ~220 km/h), **Hudhud** (Oct 2014, AP/Odisha, 205 km/h, 960 hPa), **Nilofar** (Oct 2014, Arabian Sea, 215 km/h), **Vardah** (Dec 2016, A&N, TN, AP), **Biparjoy** (June 2023, Arabian Sea, ESCS, landfall near Naliya, Gujarat, 16 June).
- Frequency: almost nil **Feb–Mar**; frequent **May, June, Oct, Nov**; **maximum Oct–Nov**. Annual: **Bay 4.5, Arabian Sea 1.1**. None in the **S Atlantic** (ITCZ never goes south of 5°S there). NW Pacific: all year.
- **Avoidance**: whatever the course, **strong wind from port means the centre is ahead** → alter course until the wind is from **starboard and weakening**.

### Frontal (extra-tropical) depressions (p.163–164)
- Warm air bulges into a stationary **polar front** → wave; stable waves move east, unstable ones deepen into a depression with a warm front ahead and a cold front behind; move **E/ENE** with the westerlies.
- When one occludes, a trailing front at lower latitude spawns a **secondary**; **cyclone family** of 3–4 depressions, each **SW** of the previous one.

### Exam traps
- Eye = calm, clear, warm, lowest pressure; **eye wall** = most dangerous.
- TC needs SST **> 26°C**, latitude **5–15°/5–25°**, low shear; none at equator.
- Monsoon depression: **head Bay**, worst **SW sector**, slopes **SW**, life **3–5 days**.
- Surge = **same** air mass, **velocity convergence**; PD = **central peninsula, pre-monsoon**.
- Indian cyclone maximum **Oct–Nov**; Bay 4.5 / year vs Arabian Sea 1.1.

## 19. Climatology of India

### Overview (p.169–170)
- Tropical south; temperate and alpine Himalayan north (sustained winter snowfall). Strongly shaped by the **Himalayas** (block cold Central Asian winds → India warmer than other places at the same latitude) and the **Thar Desert**.
- Two rain seasons with a **seasonal reversal of winds**: winter dry cold **NW winds over the north, turning NE over the peninsula** (Coriolis); summer heating draws moist air from the Arabian Sea and Bay = **SW monsoon**, the dominant control.
- **~75% of annual rain falls June–September**.
- Extremes: **W Rajasthan < 13 cm/year**; **Mawsynram 1187.3 cm (467.4 in)**; **Cherrapunji 1177.7 cm (463.7 in)**. Rain days: ~180 in the NE vs ~20 in Rajasthan.
- **Monsoon** from Arabic **"Mausim"** = seasonal reversal of winds and rain. Driven by the sun's annual swing between the tropics (thermal equator) and differential land–sea heating. Main monsoon regions: Africa, S Asia, N Australia.

### Seasons
- Six **Ritus**: Vasant (spring, Chaitra–Vaisakh, Mar–Apr, 20–30°C, vernal equinox), Grishm (summer, Jyeshtha–Asadha, May–Jun, 40–50°C, summer solstice), Varsha (monsoon, Shravan–Bhadrapad, Jul–Aug), Sharad (autumn, Ashwin–Kartik, late Sep–mid Nov, 19–25°C, autumnal equinox), Hemant (late Nov–Dec, ends at winter solstice), Shishir (winter, Magh–Phalgun, Jan–Feb, can fall < 10°C).

| IMD season | Months |
|---|---|
| **Winter / cold weather** | **Jan–Feb** (Dec–Feb for NW India) |
| **Summer / pre-monsoon / hot weather / TS season** | **Mar–May** |
| **SW monsoon / rainy** | **Jun–Sep** |
| **Post-monsoon / NE monsoon / retreating / cool** | **Oct–Nov (–Dec)** |

### Winter (Jan–Feb) (p.171–173)
- **Pressure**: Sahara–Siberia high belt centred **45°N 105°E**; India on its southern edge: **high over N India, low over the Indian Ocean**; ~4 isobars **1012–1018 hPa**; shallow trough Kerala–Gujarat along the west coast; shallow ridge over the Gangetic plains.
- **Temperature**: Dec–Jan coldest; mean minimum **22°C far south → 10°C northern plains → 6°C Punjab**; diurnal range ~13°C (10°C coasts, 5°C Tamil Nadu).
- **Surface winds** light: **NE south of 25°N, NW over N India, E over Assam**.
- **Upper winds**: to ~10,000 ft NW (north/central) and NE (south); **westerlies above**, strengthening with height, reaching the tip of the peninsula; **STJ ~12 km, 27°N, 80–100 kt (max 200)**.
- **Systems**: STJ, **5–7 WDs/month** in the north; a few cyclones and easterly waves in the south.
- **Weather**: north — clear, dry, poor visibility, low humidity and temperature, broken by WDs: winds back NW → SW/SE, temperature rises, pressure falls, CI → AS, NS, ST, drizzle/rain (warm-front type), then CU/CB, pressure rise, TS (cold-front type); then clear, cold dry N–NW winds, **widespread radiation fog and cold waves** (temperature ≥ 4°C below normal minimum, lasting 2–3 days). **Cold day**: maximum ≤ 16°C in the plains. Cold waves common Jan–Feb in W India north of 20°N (J&K, Rajasthan, W MP, Gujarat; Punjab fewer severe ones). South: Bay moisture gives rain/TS over TN; cyclones occasionally.
- **Hazards**: **poor visibility (fog, mist, haze, smoke, inversion)** — worst after a WD over NW India, UP, Bihar, central India, N Bengal, Assam (S bank of the Brahmaputra); can persist days at polluted airfields like **Palam (Delhi)** with only 1000–2000 m at noon. Advection fog on coasts lifts by **1000–1100 IST**. **CAT** near the STJ (NE India); **mountain waves** over N and E India. **Freezing level lower → icing at lower levels**.

### Summer / pre-monsoon (Mar–May) (p.173–175)
- **Pressure**: transition; temperature rising, pressure falling; weak gradient — only **two isobars (1006, 1008 hPa)** parallel to the coast; peninsular trough along **78°E** (79°E near Chennai by May); weak low over Pakistan (upper Sindh), weak trough E UP/Bihar; low over Myanmar; closed high over the east-central Bay west of the Bay Islands.
- **Temperature**: central peninsula 30–35°C in April; mean max **40–42°C**; hottest in **May** (Andamans April, Saurashtra June, Rajasthan & Kashmir July). Max diurnal range **18°C** (Gujarat, Saurashtra, MP); ~6°C on coasts. **Heat wave**: ≥ 4–5°C above normal maximum.
- **Surface winds**: March — W north of 17°N, E south; April — N–NW to E UP and east to NE India; May — SW over Rajasthan, E from E UP to NE India, S over Odisha/WB/Bihar/Jharkhand/Chhattisgarh. **Dust-raising winds** over the Gangetic plains **0900–1700 h**.
- **Upper winds**: westerlies strengthen with height (April 40 kt at 300 hPa, 60 kt at 200 hPa north of 22°N); in May the westerly belt moves to 30°N and **15–20 kt easterlies** set in over the peninsula; June: westerlies to 700 hPa, easterlies above south of 25°N (50 kt at 100 hPa).
- **Weather**: widespread **dust haze** and very high temperatures; **dust storms over N/NW India**, **Nor'westers (Kalbaishakhi) over E and NE India**, triggered by WDs; a few cyclones hit Bengal; STJ weakens and moves north.
- **Hazards — most hazardous flying season**: violent TS, DS, line squalls; dust haze to **7–8 km**; **dust-raising winds 30–50 kt (0900–1700 h)**, visibility a few hundred metres.

### SW monsoon (Jun–Sep) (p.175–180)
- Lasts < 75 days in W Rajasthan to > 120 days in the SW. **Onset**: Bay Islands/S Andaman Sea **~20–22 May**, **Kerala 1 June**, **Mumbai 15 June**, **Delhi 1 July**, **whole country 8 July**. Advances in **two branches: Arabian Sea branch (to Kerala) and Bay of Bengal branch (Myanmar, Bangladesh, Bengal)**.
- **Withdrawal**: from **Rajasthan ~1 Sep**, NW India **15 Sep**, almost all India **by 15 Oct**, replaced by the NE (winter) monsoon.
- **Pressure**: **low over Baluchistan (994 hPa)**; **monsoon trough** Sriganganagar – Allahabad – Kolkata – head Bay; high over the S hemisphere at **30°S 60°E**; **seven isobars (994–1008 hPa)**; **steep gradient along the west coast**; shallow lows over Arunachal and Myanmar.
- **Surface winds**: **E–SE north of the trough, SW–W elsewhere** (more southerly over WB; NW on the Malabar coast); 5–10 kt over land, ~15 kt at sea; monsoon strength depends on these.
- **Temperature**: south ~26°C, Rajasthan ~31°C; spatial range ~9°C, diurnal 4–9°C; rises in breaks.
- **Upper winds**: to ~10,000 ft W–SW south of the trough, SE north of it; **easterlies from 500 hPa up** over almost all India; anticyclones over **Tibet** and Baluchistan at 200 hPa; **TEJ at 15–16 km, 70–80 kt (max 150) along ~13°N**.
- **Monsoon trough**: normal position Ganganagar–Allahabad–Kolkata–head Bay ≈ ITCZ; Bay branch north of it, Arabian Sea branch south; **slopes south with height** (700 hPa ≈ 22°N Gujarat–Odisha; 500 hPa ≈ 10–15°N); its N–S swings govern rainfall.
- **Active spells and breaks**: **break** = trough shifts to the **Himalayan foothills** → rain drops over the plains, **pressure rises**, heavy rain over the foothills (sub-Himalayan WB, Nepal, Bhutan) → floods in NE India and Bihar; strong surface westerlies over E UP/Bihar; pre-monsoon-type weather with dust haze; hot and humid.
- **Monsoon depressions**: 2–3 a month from the north Bay, moving N/W, spreading rain over central and north India.
- **Mid-tropospheric (subtropical) cyclone**: circulation at **500–700 hPa** around **18–28°N, 60–80°E** with a surface trough; heavy rain over **W India / Gujarat / NE Arabian Sea** (W/SW of centre); e.g. Mumbai floods 2005.
- **Onset date** fixed by pentads (72 five-day periods from 1 Jan): a significant, sustained rise in rain → mid-date of that pentad.
- **Flying**: smooth in layer cloud, turbulent in embedded CU/CB; **visibility exceptionally good** except in rain; gaps between layers usually give a cloud-free level except near depressions.

| Monsoon strength | Rain over land | Wind over sea |
|---|---|---|
| Weak | < ½ normal | up to 12 kt |
| Normal/moderate | ½ – 1½ × normal | 13–22 kt |
| Strong | 1½ – 4 × normal | 23–32 kt |
| **Vigorous** | **> 4 × normal** | **> 32 kt** |

- **El Niño** = unusual warming of the **eastern tropical Pacific**, the **warm phase of ENSO** (La Niña = cool phase), every **2–7 years**; weak SE trades → **weak Indian monsoon** (severe Indian droughts occur in El Niño years, but not every El Niño gives drought); fewer cyclones in our region. **La Niña** → strong trades → active monsoon. Normally trades push warm water west and cause upwelling off Ecuador/Peru/Chile.
- **Hazards**: very low cloud (**150–200 m**) and strong winds in strong monsoon and depressions; visibility good except in rain (dust haze in breaks, monsoon haze on the west coast); **CAT near the TEJ**; long-lived TS at onset, in breaks and revivals.

### Post-monsoon / NE monsoon (Oct–Dec) (p.180–182)
- Retreating monsoon; NE winds establish. **Main rain season for coastal AP, Rayalaseema, Tamil Nadu–Puducherry** (TN ~48% of annual rain; ~60% in coastal districts); rain mostly **2100–0300 h** in 3–4-day spells.
- **Pressure**: flat; **one isobar (1010 hPa)** encloses India; monsoon trough moves to the Bay along **13°N**; weak lows off Chennai and over Baluchistan.
- **Winds**: SW → **NE** by mid-October; NE winds pick up moisture over the Bay → rain on TN and parts of Kerala; light variable winds inland; sea breeze felt up to **160 km** inland.
- **Temperature**: nearly uniform **~18°C**; diurnal range 6–7°C; NW India mean falls from ~38°C (Oct) to 28°C (Nov).
- **Upper winds**: westerlies re-establish; **STJ reappears over J&K**; easterlies above 500 hPa only in the far south (~20 kt).
- **Weather**: 1–2 WDs/month in the north; **best flying season** (except S India): clear skies, good visibility. **Maximum cyclones: October in the Bay, November in the Arabian Sea**; SCS more in November; usually from the Andaman Sea towards TN/AP/Odisha; some recurve to WB/Bangladesh; some cross into the Arabian Sea and hit Maharashtra/Gujarat.
- **Hazards**: visibility deteriorating near industry at dawn/dusk, fog after WDs; low cloud, gales, heavy rain and TS with cyclones in the south; **CAT (STJ)** in the north.

### Route winds
| Period | Level | Winds |
|---|---|---|
| **Oct–May (8 months)** | Above 3 km | **Westerly** everywhere, increasing with height; **STJ (W, 80–150 kt) on the Delhi–Kolkata route at 12 km** |
| | Below 3 km | NW–W over N India, NE over S India |
| **Jun–Sep (4 months)** | Above 6 km | **Easterly**, increasing with height; **TEJ (E, 60–100 kt) at Chennai latitude, 15 km** |
| | Below 6 km | W–SW over S India, SE over N India |

### Monsoon forecasting (p.182–183)
- Since **2007**, two stages: **April** forecast and **June** update (with regional forecasts for NW India, central India, S Peninsula, NE India and a July forecast). Statistical models with **8 predictors** (e.g. N Atlantic SST, equatorial SE Indian Ocean SST, E Asia MSLP, NW Europe land temperature, equatorial Pacific warm water volume, Niño 3.4 SST tendency, N Atlantic MSLP, N central Pacific 1.5 km wind); model error **5% (April), 4% (June)**; July forecast **6 predictors, 9% error**; regional models **8% error**. Since **2005** an extended-range forecast of **Kerala onset**. Long-range forecasts also for winter precipitation (Jan–Mar, NW India) and NE monsoon (Oct–Dec).

### Exam traps
- Summer season = **Mar–May**; monsoon **Jun–Sep**; autumn/post-monsoon **Oct–Nov**.
- Break monsoon: trough on the **foothills**, **pressure rises**, rain on the foothills, strong winds E UP/Bihar.
- Monsoon low over **Pakistan/Baluchistan**; steep gradient on the **west coast**.
- Winter: westerlies aloft; monsoon: **easterlies aloft** (TEJ).
- El Niño = **warm phase** of ENSO → weaker monsoon. Maximum cyclones **post-monsoon**.

## 20. General Circulation

### Why there is a general circulation (p.187)
- Highs, lows and circulations on synoptic charts move and change; their study = **synoptic meteorology** (basis of forecasting). They sit within large, semi-permanent global pressure/wind patterns.
- The equator receives more solar energy per m² than the poles → imbalance → winds redistribute heat. The long-term average wind field = **general circulation**; it transports **heat and momentum from equator to poles**.
- Controlled by (a) **energy balance**: globally, incoming short wave ≈ outgoing long wave over a year, but there is a **surplus in the tropics and a deficit at the poles**; (b) **transport processes**: winds and ocean currents move the surplus poleward.

### Three-cell model (p.188–190)
- **Hadley cell (0–30°)**: strong equatorial heating → rising air, convective cloud, heavy rain = **ITCZ** (NE and SE trades converge; follows the sun). Air rises to the tropopause, flows poleward, is turned east by Coriolis → **zonal westerlies** by ~30°, strengthening with height (angular momentum + S–N temperature gradient) → **subtropical jet** just below the tropopause. Air piles up and **subsides** at ~30° → **subtropical high** belt; subsidence suppresses cloud → **deserts near 30°N/S**. Surface return flow towards the equator, turned west → **trade winds (NE in NH, SE in SH)**.
- **Ferrel cell (30–60°)**: surface air from 30° flows poleward, turned east → **surface westerlies**; rises near **60°** (clouds, rain) = **polar front** region; some returns equatorward aloft.
- **Polar cell (60–90°)**: air sinks and warms at the poles → **polar high**; surface outflow turned west → **polar easterlies**; meets warm subtropical air at the **polar front** (with the **polar front jet**).

### Surface features
| Belt | Latitude | Character |
|---|---|---|
| **Equatorial doldrums / ITCZ** | ~0° (moves with sun) | Rising air, calms, convection, rain; **equatorial low** |
| **Trade winds** | 0–30° | Steady **NE (NH) / SE (SH)** |
| **Horse latitudes** | **30–40°** | Descending Hadley branch: **calm, high pressure** (subtropical/tropical high) |
| **Westerlies (Roaring Forties, Furious Fifties, Crying Sixties)** | **35–60°** | Main mid-latitude flow; very stormy south of 40°S all year |
| **Polar front / temperate low** | ~60° | Boundary of polar and subtropical air |
| **Polar easterlies / polar high** | 60–90° | Cold outflow from the poles |

- Pressure belts from pole to pole: **polar high – temperate low – tropical (subtropical) high – equatorial low – tropical high – temperate low – polar high**.

### Upper winds (p.190)
- Subtropical highs are shallow; aloft the temperature gradient is **S → N**, so except near the equator the **upper troposphere is westerly = zonal westerlies**; **equatorial upper winds are easterly**.
- These upper flows steer disturbances: **mid-latitude systems move W → E**; **tropical disturbances move westward** (steered by upper easterlies); on reaching the transition zone they **recurve** and move the opposite way.

### Standing and Rossby waves (p.191–192)
- **Standing (stationary) wave**: two waves of equal amplitude and frequency moving in opposite directions; in the atmosphere = month/season-averaged deviations of the circulation caused by **mountains, continents, oceans, SST contrasts**; N/S flows carry heat and moisture, alter storm tracks.
- **Rossby (planetary) waves**: giant meanders (troughs and ridges) of upper westerlies in a rotating atmosphere/ocean (discovered by **Carl Rossby, 1930s**); the meandering jet streams are Rossby waves; strongly influence weather.
- Tropical lower stratosphere: large-scale vertical motion forced by westward Rossby waves (**Rossby-wave pumping**), periods of days to weeks; affects tropopause temperature and moisture entering the stratosphere.

### Exam traps
- Subtropical high ↔ **subsidence** ↔ **deserts at 30°**.
- **Horse latitudes 30–40°** (calm high); **doldrums** = equatorial calm.
- Trade winds NE (NH) / SE (SH); upper troposphere **zonal westerlies**, but **easterlies over the equator**.
- Radiation **surplus in the tropics**, deficit at the poles.

## 21. Meteorological Services for Aviation

### Purpose and organisations (p.194–195)
- Objective: contribute to the **safety and efficiency of air navigation** by giving met information to operators, ATC, airfield management and others.
- **WMO** (UN specialised agency) handles the weather side of aviation with **ICAO**: networks of observatories, services, rapid data exchange, research, training. Its **World Weather Watch (WWW)** uses the **Global Observing System (GOS)**, **Global Telecommunication System (GTS)** and **Global Data Processing System (GDPS)**.
- **WAFS (World Area Forecast System)**: gives Met Offices **global en-route forecasts (upper winds and temperatures)**. Two **WAFCs**: **Washington and London**. **19 RAFCs** (Regional Area Forecast Centres), one at **New Delhi**.
- WAFCs produce digital/gridded global forecasts of upper wind, temperature, humidity, tropopause FL and temperature, max wind, icing, turbulence, CB; plus **SIGWX** forecasts, radioactive-release and volcanic info; **4 times a day**, issued **within 6 h** of data time.
- **IMD** = India's national met service (mausam.imd.gov.in); **6 Regional Met Offices**: Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai, Guwahati, Nagpur.

### Aviation met offices in India
| Office | Number | Job |
|---|---|---|
| **AMO** Aerodrome Met Office | **18** (incl. 4 MWOs) | Major airports; 24-h watch; METAR/SPECI, RVR, TAF, TREND, aerodrome warnings, briefing, VOLMET, ATIS, radar/satellite; forecast cover for associated AMSs |
| **AMS** Aeronautical Met Station | **54** | Current observations; forecasts obtained from AMOs, during watch hours |
| **MWO** Met Watch Office | **4**: Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai | Watch over their **FIR**; issue **SIGMET** |
| **TCAC** | — | ICAO Tropical Cyclone Advisory Centre: position, movement, central pressure, winds |
| **OLBS** | — | Online briefing for registered operators: winds/temps FL50–FL530, 9 h and 30 h TAF, national & WAFC SIGWX, METARs, satellite, radar, route forecast (T3 format) for low-level flights, local/area forecasts, take-off data, SIGMETs |

### Notification by operators (p.196) — non-scheduled flights, before ETD
| Flight | Where | Notice |
|---|---|---|
| National | AMO | **3 h** |
| National | AMS | **18–24 h** |
| International | New Delhi, Kolkata, Mumbai, Chennai, Nagpur, Varanasi, Guwahati, Hyderabad, Thiruvananthapuram | **3 h** |
| International | other AMOs | **12 h** |
| International | AMSs | **18–24 h** |
- Scheduled flights: information kept ready routinely. Notice gives ETD, destination, route, level, met info needed, briefing time.

### Observations and reports
- **METAR**: routine aerodrome report, **every half hour**, with a **TREND** appended.
- **SPECI**: special report — **immediately for deterioration**; for **improvement after 10 min** once conditions stabilise; TREND appended.
- **AIREP / PIREP**: with air-ground data link, automated observations **every 15 min en route** and **every 30 s during climb-out for the first 10 min**. **Special** observations whenever **SIGMET-criteria weather** is met; wind shear, icing, turbulence passed to ATS ASAP; volcanic activity report submitted to the AMO on arrival → MWO of that region.
- Aircraft **exempt** from routine observations if: no RNAV equipment; flight **≤ 2 h**; **≤ 1 h** from landing; below **1500 m (5000 ft)**.
- AIREP sections: **1** identification, position, time, FL; **2** operational (ETA, endurance); **3** **met information** (temperature, wind, TS, turbulence, icing, humidity). Example: `AIREP IA120 BHP 1200 UTC FL350 MS54 270/40KT TS SCT CB TOP FL400=`.

### Types of forecast by range
- **Nowcasting**: **< 24 h** (TREND, TAF, SIGMET, local forecasts, aerodrome warnings).
- **Short range** 1–3 days · **Medium range** 4–10 days · **Long / extended range** > 10 days (e.g. monsoon onset).

### Aviation forecasts (p.197–199)
- **TAF**: by AMOs for own and associated AMSs; valid **min 9 h, max 30 h**. **9-h TAFs every 3 h**; **12–30 h TAFs every 6 h**, from 0000 UTC.
- **TREND (landing forecast)**: appended to **METAR/SPECI** by AMOs (except Jaipur and Safdarjung); valid **2 h**; **PROB not used**; **NOSIG** = no significant change.
- **Take-off forecast**: within **3 h before ETD**: surface wind (**2-min mean**), temperature, QNH, others.
- **ARFOR**: area forecast (coded/tabular): upper winds/temps, sig weather, cloud, freezing level, turbulence, icing, jet, tropopause, max wind, wind shear, warnings.
- **CODAR**: **upper-air report from an aircraft** (not a weather-reconnaissance aircraft), figure code.
- **WINTEM**: **forecast** upper wind and temperature.
- **ROFOR**: route forecast (coded): upper winds/temps, sig weather, cloud, freezing level, turbulence, icing, jet, tropopause, max wind.
- **Local / Area forecast**: **3 times a day**, valid **12 h**: issued 0700 (valid 0730–1930), 1300 (1330–0130), 1800 (1830–0930) IST. **Area = 100 NM** or more; **local = 50 NM**. Contains surface wind, visibility, weather, cloud, upper winds/temps, sunrise/sunset, warnings.
- **Global SIGWX charts** (WAFC): **4 per day** (00, 06, 12, 18 UTC data), valid **24 h**; **high level FL250–FL630**, **medium level FL100–FL250**.
- **National SIGWX** (India): all 4 MWOs prepare for their FIRs; **MWO Chennai** compiles one **medium-level (FL100–250)** and one **high-level (FL250–630)** chart; 4 per day, valid **24 h**.

### SIGMET (p.199)
- Information for **aircraft in flight**, issued by **MWOs** (Mumbai, Kolkata, Delhi, Chennai) for their FIRs, for **actual or expected** en-route weather affecting safety; passed to **FIC/ACC** for aircraft.
- Issued **every 3 h** (from 0100 UTC); validity **≤ 4 h**. **Volcanic ash and tropical cyclone**: up to **12 h** before validity, updated **every 6 h**.
- Phenomena: **TS** — OBSC, EMBD, FRQ, SQL TS, TSGR (heavy hail); **tropical cyclone** (10-min mean wind **≥ 34 kt** + name); **SEV TURB**; **SEV ICE** (non-convective cloud; incl. **FZRA**); **SEV MTW** (downdraught **≥ 3.0 m/s** or severe turbulence); **HVY DS**, **HVY SS**; **VA** (+ volcano name); **radioactive cloud**. Rain alone is **not** a SIGMET phenomenon.
- Coverage terms: **ISOL** individual TS **< 50%**; **OCNL** well separated **50–75%**; **FRQ** little/no separation **> 75%**.
- Separate local warnings for light/moderate hail and moderate icing go to the local ATC for light aircraft (not sent outside the aerodrome).
- **AIRMET**: SIGMET-type information for **low-level flights**, issued by the MWO for an FIR or sub-area (prepared by AMOs, exchanged with adjacent FIRs). **GAMET**: area forecast in abbreviated plain language for **low-level flights**.

### Warnings (p.200)
- **Aerodrome warning**: by AMOs, weather affecting aircraft on the ground and facilities (picketing, mooring); validity **≤ 4 h**. Wind **≥ 30 kt**, or direction of a 20-kt wind changing by **45°**; squall, TC, DS, SS, TS, hail, frost, snow, hoar frost/rime, freezing precipitation, rising sand/dust, VA, tsunami.
- **Light aircraft / gliders / helicopters**: warning when wind is expected **≥ 17 kt** (prefix "Warnings for Light Aircraft"); not sent beyond the aerodrome.
- **Wind shear warning**: observed/expected wind shear **up to 500 m (1600 ft)** above the runway on approach, take-off, circling or the runway; topographic shear above 500 m also included; appended to METAR/SPECI in abbreviated plain language, e.g. `WS WRNG MBST APCH RWY 30`; pilot reports include aircraft type (`WS WRNG B747 REPORTED MOD WS IN APCH RWY 28 AT 1000`).

### Exchange of met messages (p.201)
- **VOLMET**: met information for **aircraft in flight**, broadcast on a published schedule, 5-min slots per station on one HF/VHF frequency. **D-VOLMET** (data link): METAR, SPECI, 9-h TAF, SIGMET, special AIREPs, AIRMET. **Voice VOLMET** in India on **HF from Mumbai and Kolkata in turn every half hour** — Mumbai: METARs of Mumbai, Ahmedabad, Chennai, Colombo, Karachi, Male; TAFs of Mumbai, Male, Colombo. Kolkata: METARs of Kolkata, Delhi, Dhaka, Yangon, Kathmandu; TAFs of Kolkata, Delhi, Ho Chi Minh.
- **ROBEX**: METAR/SPECI of international aerodromes and alternates exchanged **outside India**; AIREPs too.
- **ATIS**: continuous broadcast of **half-hourly** current weather at important aerodromes.
- **Current weather display** systems at Kolkata, Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai.

### Exam traps
- **TREND 2 h**, **SIGMET ≤ 4 h** (VA/TC 12 h ahead, updated 6 h), **aerodrome warning ≤ 4 h**, **SIGWX 24 h**, **local/area 12 h**, TAF **9–30 h**.
- **18 AMOs, 54 AMSs, 4 MWOs, 6 RMOs**; **Chennai** compiles the national SIGWX.
- Area forecast **100 NM**, local **50 NM**. Wind warning **30 kt**, light aircraft **17 kt**, direction change **45°**. Wind shear warning **up to 500 m**.
- **ISOL < 50% · OCNL 50–75% · FRQ > 75%**.
- **CODAR** = aircraft upper-air report; **WINTEM** = forecast wind/temp; **ROBEX** = outside India.

## 22. Weather Radar and Met Satellites

### Weather radar (p.206)
- **RADAR = Radio Detection and Ranging**. Detects rain-bearing cloud (especially **CU and CB**), their speed, direction of movement and vertical extent. Aircraft use it to avoid hazardous cloud; on the ground it supports warnings (parked/moored aircraft). Coastal radars detect storms for storm warnings.
- India: integrated upper-air sounding system of indigenous **X-band weather-cum-wind-finding Multi Met Radars + radiosonde since 1975**; **S-band cyclone detection radars** along the coast; digital X-band radars incl. **Doppler Weather Radars (DWR)**; all to be replaced by DWRs in phases.
- **Range**: limited by **attenuation by rain** and **earth curvature**. Detect up to **500 km**; resolution better up to **250 km**. Normally echoes within **25 NM** displayed; long-range echoes up to **75 NM** also used.
- **Scopes**: **PPI (Plan Position Indicator)** = plan view, gives **bearing and range**; **RHI (Range Height Indicator)** = direct reading of **heights of CB cells**.

### Wavelength bands (Table 22.1)
| Band | Wavelength range | Nominal | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **K** | 0.75–2.5 cm | 1 cm | Cloud detection |
| **X** | 2.5–4 cm | **3 cm** | Cloud detection, **storm detection** (airborne radars) |
| **C** | 4–8 cm | 5 cm | — |
| **S** | 8–15 cm | **10 cm** | Rain, precipitation, **cyclones and depressions** = **Cyclone Detection Radar** |
| **L** | 15–30 cm | 20 cm | — |
- **Quantitative radar (10 cm)**: rate, total and intensity of precipitation, snowfall.
- **Ultra-sensitive radar**: convection before/after cloud forms; CAT, gravity waves, lee waves.

### Doppler Weather Radar (p.207–208)
- Uses the **Doppler effect**: change of frequency of the return signal shows whether targets move **towards or away**; a **coherent** radar (compares phase of outgoing and returning pulses).
- Reflectivity depends on target diameter and reflectivity: **snowflakes large but weakly reflective; raindrops small but highly reflective**. Melting snow coated with water looks like very large drops → over-estimated precipitation → **intense ring on the PPI at the melting level**. **Drizzle does not show** — return ∝ **sixth power of drop diameter**.
- Uses: prediction of TS/DS/SS, hail, tornadoes, heavy rain/floods; tracking cyclones and TS (and their winds); structure of clouds, cyclones, TS, hailstorms, tornadoes; **detection of wind shear, turbulence, CAT, gust front, microburst, downburst**, tornadoes; tornado genesis; vertical wind field and horizontal divergence.
- Modern radars use **pulse-Doppler** (precipitation rate, drop motion, radial wind); **dual-polarisation** radars identify precipitation type and size more accurately. Rain-rate derivation in clouds since the 1950s.

### Cloud echoes (p.208–209)
- **Stratiform cloud / steady rain**: diffuse, ill-defined, uniform-intensity echoes; on RHI a **bright band at the freezing level** (strong reflection from melting snowflakes) — seen only with **weak, uniform updraughts**; vanishes as convection increases.
- **Convective cloud**: **sharp-edged** echoes; **blurring of edges = first sign of decay**; squall lines show as line echoes. Exceptionally intense echo or top reaching about **−40 °C level** → **CB**.
- **Severe-storm signatures**: intense, sharp echoes (strong updraughts; anvil = fuzzy top); **hooks, appendages, protuberances → hail**; **"figure 6" echo → tornado**; **rapidly moving** echoes → strong winds; **slanting rain columns → vertical wind shear**; **dry holes** (echo-free areas inside extensive bright echo) → strong winds and heavy rain; **rapidly developing**, **large horizontal**, **converging** echoes → severe storm; **bright band** (a few hundred m thick, just below freezing level in stratiform rain) → **weak storm**; **spiral bands → tropical cyclone**.

### Airborne weather radar (p.209)
- Lightweight, generally **X-band**, multimode, digital. Functions: weather detection and analysis, **ground mapping**, transponder beacon interrogation.
- Beam **conical ~3° wide**, tilt **±15°**; scans about **70° either side** of heading; large drops and hail reflect well at **3 cm**.
- Colours (weak → strong): **Green → Yellow → Red → Magenta**; red areas can flash every **½ s**.
  - **Red/Magenta**: heavy precipitation, TS, hail, strong winds, tornadoes, severe turbulence — **always avoid**.
  - **Yellow**: moderate precipitation, very low visibility, moderate turbulence — avoid.
  - **Green**: light precipitation, little/no turbulence, reduced visibility.
- Rainfall: **Red > 12 mm/h** (severe), **Yellow 4–12**, **Green 1–4**.

### Met satellites (p.210–213)
- Geostationary satellites (mid-1970s) revolutionised satellite meteorology. Series: **NOAA** (USA), Meteor (USSR), **INSAT** (India), **METEOSAT** (Europe), Dundee station (UK). Carry **VHRR** radiometers for day/night imagery.
- **Polar orbiting**: **650–1500 km**; whole earth **twice in 24 h**, same place every **12 h** (e.g. **NOAA**).
- **Geostationary**: **~36 000 km** above the **equator**, period = earth's rotation, stays over one spot; **5** cover the globe; round-the-clock (e.g. **METEOSAT, INSAT**). Imagery interval 30 min, but generally **3-hourly** pictures supplied.
- **Kalpana-1**: launched 12 Sep 2002, **74°E**; **INSAT-3A**: 10 Apr 2003, **93.5°E**. Both have 3-channel VHRR: **VIS (0.55–0.75 µm), IR (10.5–12.5 µm), WV (5.7–7.1 µm)**; resolution **2 km VIS, 8 km IR/WV**; INSAT-3A also a 3-channel **CCD** (VIS, NIR, SWIR) at **1 km**. ~48 images/day from Kalpana-1, 9 from INSAT-3A (CCD daytime, 5 times).
- **INSAT-2E**: VIS 2 km; WV and TIR 8 km; CCD 1 km.
- **METSAT pictures** received **3-hourly from 0300 UTC**; processed in ~40 min; on the website after **1 h 15 min** (0300Z picture by 0415Z); **hourly** during cyclones.
- **INSAT-3A** also collects hydro-met data from unattended platforms; Indian area coverage **44.4°N–10.3°S, 46.4°E–105.6°E**.
- **INSAT-3D**: launched **25 Jul 2013** from French Guiana; gives **vertical temperature and humidity profiles**; resolution **1 km VIS/SWIR, 4 km MIR/TIR, 8 km WV**; at **82°E**. **INSAT-3DR**: launched **8 Sep 2016**, **74°E**. Together **48 passes/day**, new images every **15 min**; Indian land scanned **hourly**, Indian Ocean every **90 min**, swapping areas every 3 h.
- Products: SST, OLR, rainfall estimates, cloud motion vectors, water-vapour winds, upper-tropospheric humidity, land-surface emissivity, moisture profile, fog detection; vertical T/humidity profiles, cloud-top temperature, upper winds, convective/meso-scale systems.
- NOAA/METOP/MODIS polar-orbiter receiving stations at **IMD New Delhi, RMC Chennai, RMC Guwahati**; **OCEANSAT-2** for TC genesis and NWP.
- **POES** (NOAA K, L, M): daily global clouds, storms, temperature, heat balance. **METEOSAT** near **0°**, Japan's **GMS 130°E**, INSAT 83° and 93°E; USA's two **GOES** (east and west coasts), hourly temperature data.

### Reading satellite images (p.212–213)
- Identify clouds by **brightness, texture, pattern, shape, structure, size**; clouds cast shadows; some high cloud seen only by its shadow. **Thick and water clouds are brighter than ice clouds**. CU in small regular lines or bands.
- **Jet-stream cloud**: very long **cirrus streaks**.
- **Smoke** brown–grey (oil fires black); **haze** featureless pale grey/whitish; **dust** usually slightly tan (white, red, dark brown, black by mineral); **volcanic** steam/gas white, **ash brown**. Wave clouds have distinctive patterns.
- **IR images: coldest = whitest, warmest = dark grey/black**.

### Exam traps
- **S-band (10 cm)** = cyclone detection radar; **X-band (3 cm)** = airborne and storm detection.
- **PPI = bearing and range; RHI = height**.
- Drizzle invisible (return ∝ D⁶); **bright band** at the melting level = stratiform/weak storm; **hook/figure-6** = hail/tornado.
- Airborne colours Green < Yellow < Red < Magenta. Geostationary **36 000 km**, polar **650–1500 km**.

### Layout (Table 24.1, inland plain station)

## 25. Aerodrome Met Reports: METAR, SPECI and TREND

### The reports (p.227)
- Coded in aeronautical met codes (**WMO-No. 306**). **METAR** = Aerodrome Met Report (routine, **half-hourly or hourly**); **SPECI** = Aerodrome Special Met Report (any time, to set criteria). **Same code** for both.
- Code form: `METAR/SPECI CCCC YYGGggZ dddffGfmfmKT dndndnVdxdxdx VVVVDv VxVxVxVxDv RDRDR/VRVRVRVRi w'w' NsNsNshshshs (or VVhshshs / SKC / NSC) T'T'/Td'Td' QPHPHPHPH REw'w' WS RWYDRDR (WTsTs/SS) (RRRRERCReRERBRBR)`, or `CAVOK` in place of visibility, RVR, weather and cloud.

### Reporting rules
- **Wind**: `27010KT`. **VRB** when speed **< 3 kt** (`VRB02KT`), or at higher speed when direction varies **≥ 180°** (`VRB10KT`). **Calm** (< 1 kt) `00000KT`. Gust `30020G40KT`. Extreme directions `270V050` only when variation **60°–180°** and wind **≥ 3 kt**. **≥ 100 kt**: `P99KT` (240100KT → `240P99KT`).
- **Visibility steps**: **50 m** for 0–800 m; **100 m** for 800–5000 m; **1 km** for 5000–9000 m; **9999** = 10 km or more. Directional: `6000S`; if minimum < 1500 m and another direction > 5000 m report both, e.g. `1400SW 6000E`.
- **RVR** (touchdown zone) observed when visibility or RVR **< 1500 m**; steps **25 m** (0–400), **50 m** (400–800), **100 m** (800–2000); RVR 1500–2000 m only in **RMK**, not sent internationally. Format `R27/1200D`; tendency **U** up, **D** down, **N** no change (last 10 min); **P** = more than max (`R27/P1500`); **M** = below the instrument's lowest value (`R27/M0150`).
- **Cloud base steps**: **30 m (100 ft)** up to 3000 m; 300 m above. Amounts **FEW 1–2, SCT 3–4, BKN 5–7, OVC 8** octas; **SKC** clear; **NSC** no significant cloud. Layers: lowest regardless of amount; next if **≥ 3/8**; next if **≥ 5/8**; **CB always**. Height in hundreds of feet: `SCT015` = 3–4/8 at 1500 ft; `FEW020CB`. Sky obscured: `VVhhh` (vertical visibility).
- **Temperature**: x.5 rounded **up**: +2.5 → `03`, −2.5 → `M02`; e.g. `24/M02`.
- **Pressure**: QNH rounded **down** to whole hPa (996.6 → `Q0996`; 999.9 → `Q0999`); inches reported exactly (`A3005`). World range 850–1100 hPa.
- **Fog (FG)** visibility **< 1000 m** (except with MI, BC, PR, VC). **Mist (BR)** **1000–5000 m**. **HZ, SA, DU, FU, VA** only when visibility **≤ 5000 m** (except DRSA).
- **Weather abbreviations**: DZ, RA, SN, SG, PL/PE ice pellets, IC ice crystals, **GR hail ≥ 5 mm**, **GS small hail < 5 mm**, FG, BR, SA, DU, HZ, FU, VA, **PO dust devil**, SQ, **FC funnel cloud**, DS, SS, SH, TS.
- Qualifiers: **−** light, (none) moderate, **+** heavy; **VC** vicinity (**within 8 km**, not at station); descriptors **MI** shallow, **BC** patches, **PR** partial (MI/BC/PR with FG only), **DR** low drifting (< 2 m), **BL** blowing (≥ 2 m), **SH**, **TS**, **FZ** (with RA, DZ, FG).
- **Supplementary**: **RE** recent weather (`RERA`, `RETS`); **WS RWY09** / **WS ALL RWY** wind shear; sea temperature and state `W19/S4`; runway state; **RMK** remarks.
- **CAVOK** ("KAV-OH-KAY"): **all at once** — visibility **≥ 10 km**; **no cloud below 1500 m (5000 ft) or below the highest MSA, whichever is greater, and no CB/TCU**; **no significant weather**. Replaces visibility, RVR, weather and cloud.
- **Minimum Sector Altitude**: lowest altitude giving **300 m (1000 ft)** clearance over all objects within a sector of a **46 km (25 NM)** circle centred on a radio aid.

### SPECI criteria (p.230–231) — deterioration at once, improvement after 10 min
- **Wind**: direction change **≥ 60°** with speed ≥ 10 kt before/after; mean speed change **≥ 10 kt**; gust variation **≥ 10 kt** with speed ≥ 15 kt. (**Any one** triggers.)
- **Visibility** passes **800, 1500, 3000, 5000 m**.
- **RVR** passes **150, 350, 600, 800 m**.
- **Weather**: onset/cessation/intensity change of FZ precipitation, FZFG, moderate/heavy precipitation, drifting/blowing snow, sand, dust, DS, SS, TS, squall, funnel cloud.
- **Cloud**: BKN/OVC base passes **30, 60, 150, 300, 450 m**; amount below 450 m changes between SKC/FEW/SCT and BKN/OVC.
- **Vertical visibility** (sky obscured) passes **30, 60, 150, 300 m** (100, 200, 500, 1000 ft) where a ceilometer exists.
- **Temperature rise ≥ 2 °C** since the last report.
- **SPECIAL** (India, plain language, local ATC only): visibility **2000 or 4000 m**; cloud base **90 or 120 m** (with ceilometer).

### TREND (landing forecast) (p.233–235)
- Appended to METAR/SPECI, valid **2 h**; includes only elements expected to change significantly (SPECI criteria); **NOSIG** if none. If cloud changes, **all** cloud groups are given; the cause of reduced visibility is given.
- Change indicators: **BECMG** (gradual, permanent change; starts … completes …), **TEMPO** (temporary fluctuations, each **< 1 h** and in aggregate **< half** the period). Time: **FM** from, **TL** until, **AT** at (`BECMG FM1230 TL1300`, `BECMG AT1030 OVC010`). **NSW** = no significant weather. **PROB not used**.
- hshshs: 000 < 30 m, 001 = 30 m, 002 = 60 m, 003 = 90 m … (each unit = 30 m / 100 ft).

### Worked example (book)
`SPECI VIDP 230300Z 30015G35KT 270V050 1200NW 6000S R27/P1500 +SHRA FEW020 FEW025CB SCT100 BKN250 34/27 Q1004 RERA WS RWY27=` → special report Palam, 23rd 0300 UTC; wind 300/15 gust 35, varying 270–050; vis 1200 m NW, 6000 m S; RVR RWY 27 > 1500 m; heavy shower of rain; FEW 2000 ft, FEW CB 2500 ft, SCT 10 000 ft, BKN 25 000 ft; 34/27; QNH 1004; recent rain; wind shear RWY 27.
- TREND example: `TEMPO FM0830 TL0900 24025G45KT 2000 TSRA … BECMG FM0930 27005KT 6000 NSW`.

### Exam traps
- QNH rounds **down**, temperature x.5 rounds **up**. Q1003 = 1003.0–1003.9; 32 = 31.5–32.4.
- VRB < 3 kt; extreme directions only 60–180° and ≥ 3 kt; P99KT for ≥ 100 kt.
- Visibility steps 50/100/1000 m; 9999 ≥ 10 km. FG < 1000, BR 1000–5000.
- CAVOK = all three conditions. TREND 2 h, TEMPO < 1 h each.
- M0150 = below what the instrument can measure; P1500 = above.

## 26. Aviation Weather Forecasts: TAF, ARFOR and ROFOR

### TAF — Aerodrome Forecast (p.242–244)
- Code: `TAF CCCC YYGGggZ Y1Y1G1G1/Y2Y2G2G2 dddffGfmfmKT VVVV (or CAVOK) w'w' (or NSW) NsNsNshshshs (or VVhshshs, SKC, NSC) PROBC2C2 GGGeGe / TTTTT GGGeGe (TXTFTF/GFGFZ TNTFTF/GFGFZ)`. Terms as in METAR except:
- Valid **at least 9 h, at most 30 h**; **9-h TAFs every 3 h**, **12–30 h TAFs every 6 h** from 0000 UTC. **9-h TAF is not disseminated internationally** (domestic flights and VOLMET).
- Issue time follows CCCC (`102100Z` = 10th 2100 UTC). Validity `1200/1306` = 12th 0000 to 13th 0600 UTC; `1606/1615` = 16th 0600–1500 UTC.
- **PROB30 / PROB40** = 30% or 40% probability. Change period `1809/1812` = 18th between 0900 and 1200 UTC.
- Change groups **BECMG**, **TEMPO**, **FM**; **TAF AMD** = amended TAF.
- Max/min temperature: `TX42/10Z` (max 42 °C at 1000 Z), `TN05/01Z` (min 5 °C at 0100 Z).
- Example: `TAF VECC 102100Z 1100/1109 09012KT 2500 BR FEW040 SCT150 BECMG 1102/1104 16015KT 6000 TEMPO 1107/1109 FEW030CB=` → Kolkata, issued 10th 2100 UTC, valid 11th 0000–0900; 090/12 kt, 2500 m in mist, FEW 4000 ft, SCT 15 000 ft; becoming 0200–0400 160/15 kt 6000 m; temporarily 0700–0900 FEW CB 3000 ft.

### ARFOR — Area Forecast (p.247–249)
- Figure-code aviation forecast for a specific **area** (e.g. "VECC and 50 NM around"). `ARFOR (YYGGggZ) Y1Y1G1G1/Y2Y2G2G2 KT` then:
- **Section 1**: area AAAAA, visibility VVVV, weather **W1W1W1**, cloud groups `NsCChshshs` (e.g. `2AC100` = 2/8 AC at 10 000 ft; layer rules 1/8, 3/8, 5/8, plus CB), **7hfhfhf** freezing level, **6IchihihitL** icing, **5BhBhBhBtL** turbulence, **4hxhxhxTT dddff** wind & temperature, **2h'ph'pTpTp** tropopause.
- **Section 2 `11111`**: jet stream (QLaLaLoLo h'jh'j fjfjfj). **Section 3 `22222`**: maximum wind and vertical wind shear. **Section 4 `9innn`**: supplementary phenomena.
- **Indicator figures: 7 freezing level · 6 icing · 5 turbulence · 4 wind/temp · 2 tropopause · 11111 jet · 22222 max wind**.

| Code | Turbulence (B) | Icing (Ic) | W1W1W1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Nil | Nil | — |
| 1 | Light | Light | 111 TS |
| 2 | Moderate CAT occasional | Light in cloud | 222 tropical cyclone |
| 3 | Moderate CAT frequent | Light in precipitation | 333 severe line squall |
| 4 | Moderate in cloud occasional | Moderate | 444 hail |
| 5 | Moderate in cloud frequent | Moderate in cloud | 555 marked mountain waves |
| 6 | Severe CAT occasional | Moderate in precipitation | 666 widespread sandstorm |
| 7 | Severe CAT frequent | Severe | 777 widespread duststorm |
| 8 | Severe in cloud occasional | Severe in cloud | 888 freezing rain |
| 9 | Severe in cloud frequent | Severe in precipitation | — |

- Plain-language ARFOR example: `ARFOR VIDP 091400 AREA FCST UPTO FL460 VALID 1000/1100 FOR AREA 28N72E 30N80E 20N90E 15N75E 28N72E SYNOPSIS TC CENTRED 23N74E AT 0300UTC MOV NW 20KT INTSF=`.
- Chart symbols (Fig 26.2) include widespread haze/smoke/mist/fog, freezing fog, sand/dust haze, duststorm, drizzle, rain, snow, hail, shower, blowing snow, freezing precipitation, TS, tropical cyclone, severe line squall, moderate/severe turbulence, marked mountain wave, light/moderate/severe icing, volcanic eruption.

### ROFOR — Route Forecast (p.250–251)
- Same groups along a **route** (`CCCC` departure, then destination or `QLaLaLoLo` point), with optional sections 11111 (jet), 22222 (max wind and shear), 9innn (supplementary: lowest MSL pressure, fronts, changes at points, BECMG/FM/TEMPO).
- Wind/temperature group: indicator **4**, height (hundreds of ft, 3 digits), temperature (**M** = minus), then direction (2 digits, tens of degrees) and speed (3 digits) — e.g. `405020 28015` = 5000 ft, +20 °C, 280/15 kt.
- Icing `631209` = **light icing in precipitation** (6 indicator, Ic = 3) at **12 000 ft**, thickness code 9 (book: 2700 m / 9000 ft).
- Turbulence `523306` = moderate CAT occasional at 33 000 ft, thickness code 6 (≈ 6000 ft / 1800 m).
- Jet `11111 12775 40120` = quadrant 1, 27N 75E, 40 000 ft, 120 kt. Max wind `22222 38150 27115` = 38 000 ft, 150 kt, 270°, vertical shear 15 kt per 300 m.
- Thickness tL: each unit **300 m**; 0 = up to cloud top.

### Exam traps
- TAF 9–30 h; 9-h TAF domestic, every 3 h; 12–30 h every 6 h. Validity group = **date+hour/date+hour**.
- Issue time in IST = UTC + 5 h 30 min.
- Indicator numbers 7/6/5/4/2 and 11111 = jet, 22222 = max wind.
- In figure codes heights are in **hundreds of feet** (020 = 2000 ft; 380 = 38 000 ft).

## 27. Radar Report, SIGMET Message and Satellite Bulletin

### RAREP — radar report (p.255–257)
- Code: `RAREP IIiii YYGGg (CHARACTER) (bbb/rrr …) INTENSITY TENDENCY dsdsfsfs ALTD (bbb/HtHt/rrr)`. Normally at **synoptic hours**; extra reports for breaks or rapid development.
- **IIiii** station index; **YYGGg** date/time; **bbb** azimuth (3-digit degrees) of points on the echo periphery; **rrr** range (3-digit **km**).
- **Character of echoes**: **EYE** (eye wall of a TC), **SPRL BAND** (spiral band), **SQL LN** (squall line ≥ **60 km** long, length:width ≈ **10:1**), **BRKN LN** broken line, **SLD** area fully covered, **BRKN** 4/8–7/8 covered, **SCT** 1/8–4/8, **WDLY SCT** < 1/8, **ISLTD** isolated solid echo, **ECHO ALOFT** seen only at elevations above the beam width.
- Intensity / tendency: **INCG** increasing, **DEC** decreasing, **NO CHG**.
- **dsds** direction (tens of degrees) the echo moves towards; **fsfs** speed in **km/h**; **ALTD** = echo-height indicator; **HtHt** = height of echo top AMSL in **km**. Bright band and **AP (anomalous propagation)** in plain language.
- Points along lines are given **anticlockwise**; areas start at the **northernmost point**, anticlockwise, first point repeated last to close. Heights only within **200 km** of the station.
- Example: `RAREP 42375 15030 270/050 300/060 350/025 280/010 SLD INCG 1525 ALTD 270/09/050 340/10/060 280/12/010=` → Palam, 15th 0300 UTC, solid echo area bounded by those points, increasing, moving towards 150° at 25 km/h, tops 9, 10 and 12 km.

### SIGMET message format (p.257–258)
- Abbreviated plain language, ICAO abbreviations, in this order: **MWO** (e.g. VECC) → **SIGMET n** (sequence number) → **VALID ddhhmm/ddhhmm** → description (SEV, FRQ…) and phenomenon (TURB, TS…) → **OBS AT** / **FCST** time → location and level (e.g. `TOPS FL390 S OF 25N`, `OVER KOLKATA AT FL250`) → movement (`MOV E 25KT`) → intensity change **INTSF / WKN / NC** → **OTLK** (outlook: VA trajectory, TC positions beyond validity).
- **SIGMET** for subsonic aircraft; **SIGMET SST** for transonic/supersonic flight (separate numbering).
- Disseminated to all Indian forecast offices and on **AFTN with DD priority** to MWOs of neighbouring countries within **1100 NM** of local FIR boundaries; other MWOs' SIGMETs passed to the local **FIC**.
- Example: `VECC SIGMET 4 VALID 101200/101600 TC OBS 20.2N 89.2E AT 1130 UTC FRQ TS TOPS FL350 WI 150NM OF CENTRE MOV NW 10KT OTLK TC CENTRE 110300 21N 87.9E 111200 21.5N 86.4E`.

### Satellite bulletin (p.259)
- Analysed imagery sent as an abbreviated bulletin, e.g. `SAT BLTN BASED ON METSAT PIC OF 271200 UTC …` giving vortex centre (repeated: "RPT"), intensity **T number** (e.g. T1.5), cloud types and areas, convection (MOD/INT CONVTN), embedded cloud (EMBDD), with lat/long boxes.

### Exam traps
- RAREP range in **km**, echo speed in **km/h**, top heights in **km AMSL**, heights reported only within 200 km.
- Squall line echo ≥ 60 km, 10:1. SLD full, BRKN 4–7/8, SCT 1–4/8, WDLY SCT < 1/8.
- SIGMET SST for supersonic; AFTN DD priority; 1100 NM.

## 28. Met Documentation and Briefing

### What is supplied (p.260)
- AMOs supply written/printed material, digital data, **briefing, consultation or display** to: operators (pre-flight, in-flight, re-planning), flight crew before departure, aircraft in flight.
- Content: **winds** (upper winds, temperatures, max wind, jet, tropopause); **reports** (METAR, SPECI, TREND, AIREP, SIGMET, VA); **forecasts** (en-route, TAF, take-off, wind-shear warning, local/area); **charts** (SIGWX); **imagery** (satellite, radar).

### Briefing (p.260–261)
- Purpose: actual and forecast weather at departure, en route, destination and alternates; given in person **3 h before scheduled departure** (else by phone/telecom).
- **Low-level / VFR flights**: up to **FL100** (**FL150 or above in mountains**); poor visibility (**≤ 5000 m**), phenomena likely to lower widespread visibility below 5000 m, cloud affecting flight, **SIGMET**.
- **Oral briefing** when adequate notice (**3 h to AMOs, 12 h to AMSs**) was not given: recorded in the Briefing Register ("documentation not provided for want of adequate notice"), crew signs. Avoid as far as possible.

### Flight documentation
- Charts, tabular or cross-section forms: upper wind & temperature (+ tropopause, max wind, jet, en-route weather); SIGWX; METAR/SPECI/TAF of departure, destination, en-route and alternates, area/local forecast; relevant SIGMET, AIREP, cyclone advisory, VA report.
- **Met T-3 (tabular)** for flights **up to 500 NM**; **Met T-4 (cross-section)** for flights **beyond 500 NM**. Both give synoptic situation and surface pressure, en-route outlook, cloud amount/type (pictorial in T-4), upper winds/temps, freezing level, tropopause, surface visibility (below FL100 only).
- Significant en-route weather — **high-level flights**: TS, CB, squall line, hail, moderate/severe turbulence, icing, freezing precipitation, widespread DS/SS, marked mountain waves. **Low-level flights**: all of these **plus fog, precipitation and anything reducing visibility below 5000 m**.
- Chart sets: **international** — WAFC SIGWX (**FL250–FL630**) and **250 hPa** wind/temperature charts. **National** — national SIGWX + WAFC upper wind/temperature charts; low-level flights at **600, 1500 and 3000 m (2000, 5000, 10 000 ft)**. Met T-3/T-4 if fax/internet unavailable.

### National SIGWX charts (p.262)
- All four MWOs prepare for their FIRs → **MWO Chennai** compiles one **medium-level (FL100–250)** and one **high-level (FL250–630)** chart. **Zero-degree isotherm on the medium-level chart.**
- **4 per day** (00, 06, 12, 18 UTC data), issued **9 h after** observation time, valid **24 h**.
- Contents: tropical cyclones (≥ 34 kt); CB with TS, TC, line squall, hail, moderate/severe icing and turbulence; CAT; marked mountain waves; widespread DS/SS; freezing level and tropopause FL; volcanic eruptions and radioactive releases; jet streams.

### Heights in documentation
- **En-route** conditions (upper winds, turbulence, cloud base/top): **flight levels**.
- **Aerodrome** conditions (cloud base and top): **above aerodrome elevation**.
- In-flight information via **VOLMET / D-VOLMET** (Mumbai, Kolkata): METAR, SPECI, TAF, SIGMET, AIREP, upper winds/temps, VA and TC advisories.

## 29. Flight Forecast (Tabular) and Cross-Section Forecast

### Met T-3 — tabular flight forecast (sample, Table 29.1)
- Header: date, route (e.g. **VECC → VIDP via VILK**), validity for departure (0300–0500 UTC) and arrival (0600–0800 UTC); **heights in feet AMSL**.
- **Special features**: e.g. "A WD lies over W Rajasthan with upper-air cyclonic circulation up to 20 000 ft".
- Columns by route zone (Kolkata, Lucknow, Delhi): **upper winds/temperatures** at 30 000, 20 000, 10 000, 5000 ft (e.g. 30 000 ft: M33, 300/60 … 270/70, M30); **clouds** (SCT ST 1000, FEW SC 2500, SCT AC 12 000, ISOL EMBD CB 2000/35 000, BKN CS 35 000); **surface visibility** (6 km; 3000 m in rain, 1500 m in TS); **significant weather** (ISOL TS; icing and turbulence in CB 25 000/15 000); **0 °C isotherm** (15 000, 14 000 ft); **forecast QNH** (1006, 1008 hPa).

## 30. Appendices A–J

### A. Synoptic systems
- **Depression / cyclone**: intense low with **2 or more closed isobars at 2 hPa** interval and strong winds.
- **Trough in the westerlies**: mid-latitude wave moving **W → E**; affects northern India.
- **Trough in the easterlies / easterly wave**: shallow trough in the tropical easterlies moving **E → W**, clearer in upper winds than in surface pressure; cloud and showers; affects **S peninsular India**.
- **Western disturbance (WD)**: surface low or mid/lower-tropospheric cyclonic circulation/trough from the **Mediterranean, Caspian and Black Seas**, moving across N India; with ≥ 2 closed isobars = **western depression**.
- **Induced low / induced cyclonic circulation**: develops **south of a WD** under its influence.
- **High**: pressure higher than surroundings at the same level, ≥ 1 closed isobar.
- **Shear line**: line/zone of abrupt change in horizontal wind component (maximum horizontal shear). **Wind discontinuity**: line of abrupt change in wind direction.

### B. Meteorological scales
| Scale | Size / life | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Micro** | ≤ 1 km, ≤ 10 min | Local turbulence, gusts |
| **Meso** | 5–500 km, ≤ 1 day (~5 h) | TS, squall lines, fronts, cyclones, mountain waves, land/sea breezes |
| **Synoptic** | Large areas, ~1 day+ | Extratropical cyclones, troughs, ridges, frontal zones, jets |
| **Global / macro / planetary** | ≥ 5 days, longer than seasonal cycles | ENSO, ITCZ, Rossby waves |

- **Fujita scale (tornado winds)**: **F0 35–62 kt** light · **F1 63–95** moderate (cars pushed off roads) · **F2 96–135** considerable (roofs off, trees uprooted) · **F3 136–180** severe (heavy cars thrown) · **F4 181–225** devastating (houses levelled) · **F5 226–275** incredible.
- **Saffir–Simpson**: Cat **1** minimal > 980 hPa, 64–83 kt, surge 4–5 ft · Cat **2** moderate 965–979, 84–96 kt, 6–8 ft · Cat **3** extensive 945–964, 97–113 kt, 9–12 ft · Cat **4** extreme 920–944, 114–135 kt, 13–18 ft · Cat **5** catastrophic < 920 hPa, > 135 kt, > 18 ft.
- **T (Dvorak CI) numbers**: Low **T1.5**, D **T2.0**, DD **T2.5**, CS **T3.0**, SCS **T3.5**, VSCS **T4.0–6.0**, SuCS **T6.5–8.0**.

### D. Miscellaneous terms
- **Iso-lines**: **isobar** pressure · **isohyet** rainfall · **isotach** wind speed · **isogon** wind direction · **isotherm** temperature · **isallobar** pressure change · **isopycnic** density · **isohel** sunshine · **isomer** % of average annual rainfall in a month · **isopleth** any quantity (generic) · **isohypse / contour** height · **streamline** tangential to wind direction.
- **Met sub-divisions**: **NW India** J&K, HP, Punjab, Haryana, Delhi, E & W UP, Rajasthan, Uttarakhand · **East** Jharkhand, Bihar, Odisha, WB, Sikkim · **NE** Arunachal, Nagaland, Mizoram, Manipur, Tripura, Assam, Meghalaya · **South** TN, AP, Kerala, Karnataka, Lakshadweep, A&N · **West** Konkan & Goa, Madhya Maharashtra, Marathwada, Gujarat, Saurashtra & Kutch · **Central** MP, Chhattisgarh, Vidarbha.
- **Spatial distribution** (stations with ≥ 2.5 mm): **widespread (most places) ≥ 75%** · **fairly widespread (many) 51–74%** · **scattered (a few) 26–50%** · **isolated ≤ 25%**.
- **Rain intensity (per day)**: trace 0.01–0.04 mm · light 2.5–7.5 mm · heavy 64.5–124.4 mm · very heavy 124.5–244.4 mm · **rainy day ≥ 2.5 mm** · mainly dry = very light rain · dry day = no rain.

- **Parts of the day (IST)**: early hours 0000–0400 · morning 0400–0800 · forenoon 0800–1200 · afternoon 1200–1600 · evening 1600–2000 · night 2000–2400.

### E. Major climates
- **Polar** (South Pole, extreme N, Greenland): very cold, dry all year. **Tundra** (N extremes of N America/Asia, S coast of Greenland): very cold all year. **Subarctic/boreal** (50–70°N): long very cold winters, short cool summers, low precipitation (more in summer).
- **Temperate** (N America, Europe, N Asia): cold winters, mild summers. **Oceanic/marine** (45–63°, NW Europe, NW N America, SE/SW S America, SE Australia, NZ): cloudy, frequent fronts, cool summers, mild winters, few TS.
- **Mediterranean** (Mediterranean lands, S of S America, S California): mild winters, hot dry summers.
- **Subtropical / warm temperate** (25–40°): long hot humid summers, summer rain from TS and monsoons; moderate winters.
- **Arid** (N Africa, central Asia, SW USA, inland Australia): hot and dry all year. **Tropical** (S America, Africa, SE Asia, Pacific islands): hot and wet.

### F. Met charts
- **National SIGWX** (high FL250–630, medium FL100–250): forecast positions and movement of synoptic systems; FL of base/top of significant cloud inside **scalloped** lines; **CB implies moderate or severe turbulence, icing and hail**; symbols for TS, cyclone, icing, turbulence; **freezing level as a dotted line** with FL in boxes; high-level chart also shows FLs of jet, max wind, CAT and tropopause in boxes.
- **WAFC wind/temperature charts** (made for S Asia by IMD Chennai from WAFC London data): validity and level (e.g. 250 hPa / FL340); wind by shaft, feathers and pennants; temperature beside the shaft; **positive temperatures prefixed PS** (PS12 = +12 °C).
- **Tropopause and max-wind chart**: tropopause FL in boxes (e.g. FL400); jet path as bold arrows with FL; **0 °C isotherm as short dashes**.
- **Spot wind/temperature chart**: forecast wind and temperature at grid points for several altitudes (e.g. `30 280100 −32` = 30 000 ft, 280°/100 kt, −32 °C) plus the freezing level.

### G. Other systems influencing the SW monsoon
- **Tibetan High**: warm anticyclone at **300 hPa** over the Tibetan plateau (~28°N 98°E), 70–110°E; its easterly outflow feeds the **TEJ** (13–15°N near Chennai, **150 hPa**, July), running from Vietnam to W Africa; accelerates S China Sea → S India, decelerates beyond. Shifts E/W vary monsoon activity; a far-west shift can push the monsoon into Pakistan/N Iran.
- **Somali jet**: low-level (**1–1.5 km**) cross-equatorial flow reaching jet speed along the **E coast of Africa**; from near Mauritius/N Madagascar; reaches India's W coast in June, strongest in **July**; stronger jet → stronger monsoon.
- **Indian Ocean Dipole (IOD)**: SST difference between the **Arabian Sea and eastern Bay of Bengal**; positive / neutral / negative. **Positive IOD → better rain**; negative → less rain, more breaks. **EQUINOO** (Equatorial Indian Ocean Oscillation) arises from it; positive IOD and EQUINOO increase rain.
- **Atlantic Niño**: Atlantic warming **weakens** the monsoon; cooling strengthens it.
- **Mascarene High**: semi-permanent high in the S Indian Ocean (~4000 km from India), develops from mid-April; drives cross-equatorial flow; **stronger high → stronger surge, heavier W-coast rain**.
- **Madden–Julian Oscillation (MJO)**: eastward-moving (W → E) equatorial wave of cloud, rain, wind and pressure, **30–60-day** cycle (Madden & Julian, **1971**); over the Indian Ocean in the monsoon → good rain; **shorter (~30-day) cycle → better monsoon** (2–3 good spells); ≥ 40 days reduces rain.
