## 1. Definitions and Abbreviations

### Aircraft and their kinds (p.3–38)
- **Aircraft**: any machine that can derive support in the atmosphere from the **reactions of the air** other than reactions of the air against the earth's surface (so a hovercraft is **not** an aircraft). Includes balloons (fixed or free), airships, kites, gliders and flying machines.
- **Aerodyne**: support derived **dynamically** from surfaces moving relative to the air: aeroplanes, helicopters, gyroplanes, gliders and kites.
- **Aeroplane**: **power-driven heavier-than-air**, lift chiefly from surfaces that **remain fixed**. **Glider**: the same but **non-power-driven**. **Airship**: **power-driven lighter-than-air**. **Helicopter**: heavier-than-air, supported by power-driven rotors on substantially vertical axes.
- **Seaplane**: takes off and alights **solely on water**. **Amphibian**: on **land or water**.
- **Small aeroplane**: MCTOM **5,700 kg or less**. **Large aeroplane**: **over 5,700 kg**.
- **Microlight**: single-seater MAUW **≤ 330 kg**, two-seater **≤ 450 kg**, wing area **≥ 10 m²** in both; excludes hang gliders and para-planes. Flown on a **Permit to Fly**.
- **State aircraft**: military aircraft and aircraft used exclusively in government service (posts, customs, police).
- **Foreign aircraft**: registered in a country other than India.
- **RPA**: unmanned aircraft piloted from a remote pilot station; **RPAS** = RPA + station(s) + command-and-control links.

### Aerodrome areas (p.4–36)
- **Aerodrome**: defined area on land or water (with buildings and equipment) for arrival, departure and surface movement of aircraft. **Aerodrome elevation** = elevation of the **highest point of the landing area**.
- **Manoeuvring area** = take-off, landing and taxiing, **excluding aprons**. **Movement area** = manoeuvring area **+ aprons**.
- **Apron**: for loading/unloading, fuelling, parking or maintenance. **Aircraft stand**: designated parking area on an apron.
- **Threshold**: beginning of the runway usable for landing. **Displaced threshold**: not at the extremity of the runway. **Touchdown zone**: where landing aeroplanes first contact the runway.
- **Clearway**: area beyond the runway over which an aeroplane may make part of its initial climb. **Stopway**: area beyond the TORA prepared for stopping after an abandoned take-off.
- **RESA**: area at the end of the strip to reduce damage from undershoot/overrun. **Runway strip**: runway + stopway area protecting aircraft running off or flying over.
- **Runway turn pad**: area for a 180° turn. **Rapid exit taxiway**: joins the runway at an **acute angle** to let landing aircraft turn off at higher speed.
- **Runway-holding position** (RTF: "holding point") protects the runway, an obstacle limitation surface or the ILS critical/sensitive area. **Intermediate holding position**: traffic control point on taxiways. **Road-holding position**: for vehicles.
- **Runway guard lights** warn that you are about to enter an active runway. **Barrette**: three or more closely spaced lights in a transverse line that look like a short bar.
- **Frangible object**: low mass, designed to break, distort or yield on impact. **FOD**: object on the movement area with no aeronautical function that can harm aircraft.
- **Near-parallel runways**: extended centre lines converge/diverge by **15° or less**.
- **Wet runway**: any visible dampness or water **up to and including 3 mm** deep. **Dry**: no visible moisture, not contaminated.
- **Temporary aerodrome**: used for **not more than 6 months**.

### Declared distances (p.14)
- **TORA**: runway length available for the **ground run** of a take-off.
- **TODA** = TORA **+ clearway**. **ASDA** = TORA **+ stopway**. **LDA**: runway length for the ground run of a landing.

### Vertical references (p.8–36)
- **Altitude**: vertical distance from **MSL**. **Height**: from a **specified datum**. **Elevation**: of a point **on or affixed to the earth**, from MSL.
- **Flight level**: surface of constant pressure related to **1013.2 hPa**. QNH → altimeter shows altitude; QFE → height above the QFE datum; 1013.2 → flight levels.
- **Transition altitude**: at or below it, vertical position is controlled by **altitudes**. **Transition level**: **lowest flight level** available above the TA. **Transition layer**: airspace **between** TA and TL.
- **Pressure altitude**: a pressure expressed as the altitude it corresponds to in the Standard Atmosphere.
- **Geoid**: equipotential surface coinciding with undisturbed MSL extended through the continents. **Geoid undulation**: geoid above (+) or below (−) the reference ellipsoid (WGS-84).
- **Altimetry system error (ASE)**: indicated altitude (correct setting) minus pressure altitude of the undisturbed air.

### Minimum altitudes (p.18–21)
- **MSA**: lowest altitude giving **300 m (1,000 ft)** clearance above all objects within a sector of a **46 km (25 NM)** circle centred on a radio aid.
- **MEA**: en-route altitude giving nav-aid reception, ATS communications, airspace compliance **and** obstacle clearance.
- **MOCA**: gives only the required **obstacle clearance** for a segment.
- **OCA/H**: lowest altitude/height used to meet obstacle clearance criteria (OCA from MSL; OCH from threshold, or aerodrome elevation for NPA/circling).
- **MDA/H**: in a **2D** approach or circling, do not descend below without the required visual reference. MDH is referenced to aerodrome elevation, or threshold elevation if that is more than **2 m (7 ft)** below aerodrome elevation; circling MDH to aerodrome elevation.
- **DA/H**: in a **3D** approach, the point where a **missed approach must be initiated** if the visual reference is not established. DA from MSL, DH from **threshold elevation**.

### Approaches and runways (p.16–19)
- **NPA**: 2D approach Type A. **APV**: PBN 3D approach Type A. **PA**: ILS, MLS, GLS, SBAS Cat I; 3D Type A or B.
- **Type A** = minimum **250 ft or above**; **Type B** = **below 250 ft**.
- **Non-precision approach runway**: visibility **not less than 1,000 m**.
- **Cat I**: DH **not lower than 60 m (200 ft)** and visibility **≥ 800 m** or RVR **≥ 550 m**.
- **Cat II**: DH **below 60 m but not below 30 m (100 ft)**, RVR **≥ 300 m**.
- **Cat III**: DH **below 30 m (100 ft)** or no DH, and RVR **below 300 m** or no RVR limit.
- **CDFA**: NPA final segment flown as a continuous descent, no level-off, from FAF altitude to about **15 m (50 ft)** above threshold.
- **Base turn**: turn between outbound and final track, tracks **not reciprocal**. **Procedure turn**: turn away then opposite turn to intercept the **reciprocal**. **Racetrack**: to lose height or establish inbound when reversal entry is impractical. **Reversal procedure**: to reverse direction in the initial segment.
- **Circling approach**: extension of an IAP giving visual circling before landing.
- **MAPt**: point at or before which the missed approach must start to protect obstacle clearance.
- **IAF** begins the initial segment; **IF** begins the intermediate segment; **FAF/FAP** begins the final segment.

### Airspace and ATS (p.5–35)
- **FIR**: airspace where **FIS and alerting service** are provided. **Control area (CTA)**: controlled airspace from a **specified limit above the earth**. **Control zone (CTR)**: controlled airspace **from the surface** to a specified upper limit. **TMA**: control area at the confluence of ATS routes near major aerodromes. **Airway**: control area in the form of a **corridor**.
- **ATZ**: around an aerodrome to protect aerodrome traffic. **Advisory airspace / advisory route**: air traffic advisory service available.
- **Controlled airspace** = Classes **A, B, C, D and E**.
- **Prohibited area**: flight **prohibited**. **Restricted area**: flight **restricted** by conditions. **Danger area**: **dangerous activities may exist** at specified times. Prohibited and restricted areas lie over a State's **land or territorial waters**; danger areas can be anywhere, including high seas.
- **ADIZ**: airspace with special identification/reporting procedures beyond ATS ones.
- **ATC service** prevents collisions between aircraft and, on the manoeuvring area, between aircraft and obstructions, and expedites an orderly flow. **ATS** is the generic term (FIS, alerting, advisory, ATC: area, approach, aerodrome).
- **Aerodrome traffic**: all traffic on the manoeuvring area and all aircraft in the vicinity (in, entering or leaving the circuit). **Air traffic**: all aircraft in flight or on the manoeuvring area.
- **Clearance limit**: point to which a clearance is granted. **Downstream clearance**: from a unit that is not the current controlling unit.
- **Accepting unit / transferring unit / transfer of control point** describe hand-over between ATC units.
- **ATIS**: automatic routine information for arriving and departing aircraft. **VOLMET**: met information for aircraft in flight (METAR, SPECI, TAF, SIGMET by continuous voice broadcast). **D-ATIS / D-VOLMET**: by data link.
- **Air-taxiing**: helicopter/VTOL above the aerodrome surface, normally in ground effect, below **20 kt (37 km/h)** groundspeed.

### Flight plans and times (p.13–35)
- **Filed flight plan**: as filed, without later changes. **Current flight plan**: filed plan + later ATC clearances. **Repetitive flight plan (RPL)**: for regular recurring flights with identical features. **Operational flight plan**: the operator's plan for safe conduct of the flight.
- **EOBT**: estimated time the aircraft starts moving for departure. **EAT**: time ATC expects a delayed arrival to **leave the holding fix**.
- **ETA (IFR)**: over the point from which the instrument approach starts (or over the aerodrome if no aid). **ETA (VFR)**: over the aerodrome. **Total EET** is defined the same way from take-off.
- **Flight time (aeroplane)**: from first moving for take-off until finally coming to rest = **block to block / chock to chock**. **Helicopter**: from rotors starting to turn until rest with rotors stopped.
- **Night**: end of evening civil twilight to start of morning civil twilight (sun's centre **6° below** the horizon).
- **Ceiling**: height of the base of the lowest cloud layer **below 6,000 m (20,000 ft)** covering **more than half** the sky.
- **Visibility**: the greater of (a) distance a black object can be seen against a bright background (= MOR) and (b) distance lights of about **1,000 candelas** can be seen against an unlit background.
- **RVR**: range over which a pilot on the runway centre line can see runway markings or lights.

### Emergency and SAR words (p.8–31)
- **INCERFA** = uncertainty phase (uncertainty about safety). **ALERFA** = alert phase (**apprehension** about safety). **DETRESFA** = distress phase (**reasonable certainty** of grave and imminent danger).
- **RCC**: organises SAR in a search and rescue region (**SRR**); **RSC**: rescue sub-centre under it. **Alerting post**: intermediary between someone reporting an emergency and the RCC.
- **ELT(AF)** automatic fixed (permanently attached); **ELT(AP)** automatic portable (attached, readily removable); **ELT(AD)** automatic deployable (deployed by impact / hydrostatic sensor, manual too); **ELT(S)** survival (removable, **manually** activated by survivors).
- **Accident**: between boarding with intention of flight and disembarkation: a person **fatally or seriously injured**, aircraft **damage/structural failure**, or aircraft **missing**/inaccessible. **Serious incident**: an accident **nearly occurred**. **Incident**: anything else affecting or able to affect safety.
- **Ditching**: forced landing on water.

### Operations and people (p.4–34)
- **Commercial air transport**: passengers, cargo or mail for remuneration. **Aerial work**: industrial/commercial work, not air transport. **General aviation**: everything else.
- **PIC**: pilot designated by the operator (or owner, in GA) as in command and charged with safe conduct. **Co-pilot**: licensed pilot in any piloting capacity other than PIC, excluding someone aboard only for instruction. **Student PIC**: trainee acting as PIC under an instructor's observation; if the instructor touches the controls it becomes **dual**.
- **Cruise relief pilot**: performs pilot tasks in cruise so PIC or co-pilot can rest.
- **Operational control**: authority over initiation, continuation, diversion or termination of a flight.
- **MMEL**: by the **type-design organisation**, approved by the **State of Design**. **MEL**: by the **operator**, same as or **more restrictive** than the MMEL.
- **State of Registry**: on whose register the aircraft is. **State of the Operator**: operator's principal place of business. **State of Design**: type-design organisation. **State of Manufacture**: final assembly. **State of Occurrence**: where the accident happens.
- **Psychoactive substances**: alcohol, opioids, cannabinoids, sedatives, hypnotics, cocaine, other stimulants, hallucinogens, volatile solvents; **coffee and tobacco excluded**.
- **Petroleum in bulk**: receptacle **over 900 litres**.

### Navigation words (p.12–38)
- **Heading**: direction the longitudinal axis points. **Track**: projection of the path on the earth.
- **DME distance**: **slant range**. **Change-over point (COP)**: where you switch from the VOR behind to the VOR ahead.
- **RNAV specification**: no on-board performance monitoring and alerting (e.g. RNAV 5, RNAV 1). **RNP specification**: **with** monitoring and alerting (e.g. RNP 4, RNP APCH).
- **Fly-by waypoint**: turn anticipation, tangential interception. **Fly-over waypoint**: turn starts **over** the waypoint.
- **Significant point**: nav-aid, intersection or waypoint.
- **ADS-B**: broadcast via data link. **ADS-C**: by contract (event, demand, periodic, emergency). **ACAS**: SSR-transponder based, independent of ground equipment.
- **ACN/PCN** applied until 27 Nov 2024; **ACR/PCR** from 28 Nov 2024.

### Exam traps
- **Movement area includes aprons; manoeuvring area does not.**
- **TODA adds clearway; ASDA adds stopway.**
- **CTR starts at the surface; CTA starts above the surface.**
- MSA = **1,000 ft within 25 NM**; ceiling = **below 20,000 ft, more than half the sky**.
- Cat I **200 ft / 550 m RVR**; Cat II **100 ft / 300 m**; Cat III **below 100 ft / below 300 m**.
- Small aeroplane is **5,700 kg or less** (exactly 5,700 kg is small).
- Wet runway: up to and **including 3 mm**.
- DH is referenced to the **threshold**; MDH to the **aerodrome** (or threshold if more than 7 ft lower).

## 2. International Organisations and Conventions

### Paris Convention, 1919 (p.39)
- First truly international agreement on civil aviation: **Convention Relating to the Regulation of Aerial Navigation**, signed **13 October 1919** at the Paris Peace Conference (League of Nations).
- Set preliminary technical standards and created **ICAN** (International Commission on Air Navigation), the forerunner of ICAO. Superseded by the Chicago Convention.

### Chicago Convention, 1944: key articles (p.39–45)
- **96 articles**, signed **7 December 1944**; India has ratified it. Applies to **civil aircraft only**, not state aircraft.
- **Art 1 Sovereignty**: every State has **complete and exclusive sovereignty** over the airspace above its territory. **Art 2 Territory**: land areas **and territorial waters** adjacent.
- **Art 3**: military, customs and police aircraft = **state aircraft**; they may not overfly or land in another State without special agreement.
- **Art 4**: no misuse of civil aviation.
- **Art 5 Non-scheduled flights**: may fly into/across and make **non-traffic stops** without prior permission (1st and 2nd freedoms), but the State may require landing, routes or permission.
- **Art 6 Scheduled services**: need **special permission** of the State.
- **Art 7 Cabotage**: a State may refuse foreign aircraft the right to carry traffic **between two points within its own territory**.
- **Art 8 Pilotless aircraft**: need special authorisation.
- **Art 9 Prohibited areas**: for military necessity or public safety, applied **uniformly** to aircraft of other States.
- **Art 10**: landing/departure at a designated **customs airport** may be required.
- **Art 11**: a State's air regulations apply to all aircraft **equally, regardless of nationality**.
- **Art 12 Rules of the Air**: every aircraft over a State follows that State's rules; aircraft carrying its nationality mark follow them **wherever they are**; **over the high seas the rules are those established under the Convention** (Annex 2).
- **Art 13** entry and clearance (immigration, customs, quarantine) laws must be complied with. **Art 14** prevent spread of disease (cholera, typhus, smallpox, yellow fever, plague).
- **Art 15 Airport charges**: facilities open to all; charges to foreign aircraft **not higher** than to national aircraft.
- **Art 16**: authorities may **search** aircraft on landing or departure and inspect documents.
- **Art 17**: aircraft have the **nationality of the State of registry**. **Art 18**: **no dual registration** (registration may be transferred). **Art 19**: registration under national law. **Art 20**: must bear nationality and registration marks. **Art 21**: report registration/ownership to ICAO.
- **Art 22–23** facilitation; **Art 24 Customs duty**: aircraft admitted **temporarily free of duty**; fuel, oil, spares, equipment and stores retained on board exempt.
- **Art 25**: assist aircraft in distress. **Art 26 Accident investigation**: the **State of occurrence** investigates; the **State of registry may appoint observers** and receives the report.
- **Art 27**: aircraft in flight **cannot be seized for patent claims**. **Art 28**: States provide airports, navigation, met, radio, charts to Convention standards.
- **Art 29 Documents carried** (international flight): **C of R, C of A, crew licences, journey log book, radio station licence** (if radio fitted), **passenger list** with embarkation and destination, **cargo manifest** and declarations.
- **Art 30**: radio licensed by the **State of registry**, used only by licensed flight crew. **Art 31**: C of A issued or rendered valid by the **State of registry**. **Art 32**: crew licences issued or rendered valid by the **State of registry**; a State may refuse to recognise licences given to **its own nationals** by another State for flight over its territory.
- **Art 33**: certificates and licences of the State of registry are **recognised** by others if issued to at least ICAO minimum standards.
- **Art 34** journey log book. **Art 35**: no **munitions of war**. **Art 36**: States may regulate **photographic apparatus**.
- **Art 37**: ICAO adopts SARPs. **Art 38 Departures from standards**: a State that cannot comply gives **immediate notification** to ICAO; after an amendment, notice to the Council **within 60 days**; the Council notifies all States.
- **Art 39–40**: non-compliant certificates/licences must be **endorsed**; endorsed ones fly internationally only with permission of the States entered.
- **Art 41**: airworthiness standards don't apply to prototypes submitted within **3 years** of adoption. **Art 42**: licensing standards don't apply to licences first issued within **1 year** of adoption, but do apply to all licences still valid **5 years** after.

### ICAO (p.45–47)
- Created by the Chicago Convention; specialised UN agency (linked to ECOSOC) since **1947**. HQ **Montreal, Canada**.
- **Assembly**: highest body, **all contracting States, one vote each**, meets **at least once in 3 years**.
- **Council**: **36 States** elected by the Assembly; President elected by the Council. Adopts SARPs.
- **Air Navigation Commission**: **19 members** appointed by the Council. Also Air Transport Committee, Legal Committee, Committee on Joint Support (9–11), Finance Committee (9–13), Committee on Unlawful Interference (15).
- **Secretariat** headed by the **Secretary-General** (appointed by the Council): Air Navigation, Air Transport, Technical Assistance, Legal, and Administration bureaux.
- **193 member States** (includes the Cook Islands, not a UN member). Non-members: Liechtenstein, Niue, Tuvalu, Vatican City.
- **7 regional offices, 9 regions**: Bangkok (Asia-Pacific), Cairo (Middle East), Dakar (W & C Africa), Lima (South America), Mexico City (N/C America & Caribbean), Nairobi (E & S Africa), Paris (Europe & N Atlantic). **RAN meetings** consider regional requirements.

### ICAO publications (p.47–49)
- **Standards**: uniform application **necessary**; non-compliance must be notified (Art 38). **Recommended Practices**: **desirable**; Council invites (but does not oblige) notification.
- **PANS**: approved by the **Council** for worldwide use; procedures not mature enough for SARPs or too detailed / often amended. **SUPPS** (Doc 7030): like PANS but for **regions** only.
- **Technical manuals** amplify SARPs and PANS. **Air Navigation Plans**: facilities per region. **Circulars**: specialised information.
- **Annexes 2, 5, 7 and 8** contain **Standards only** (no RPs). The other 15 contain both.
- 19 Annexes: **1** Personnel Licensing · **2** Rules of the Air · **3** Meteorology · **4** Charts · **5** Units of measurement · **6** Operation of aircraft (I commercial, II general aviation, III helicopters) · **7** Nationality and registration marks · **8** Airworthiness · **9** Facilitation · **10** Aeronautical telecommunications (I nav-aids, II comm procedures, III comm systems, IV surveillance & ACAS, V spectrum) · **11** ATS · **12** SAR · **13** Accident investigation · **14** Aerodromes (I aerodromes, II heliports) · **15** AIS · **16** Environment (I noise, II emissions) · **17** Security · **18** Dangerous goods · **19** Safety management.
- Key Docs: **4444** PANS-ATM · **7030** SUPPS · **8400** abbreviations and codes · **8168** PANS-OPS (aircraft operations) · **7910** location indicators · **8585** operator designators · **8643** aircraft type designators · **9432** RTF manual · **9613** PBN manual · **9674** WGS-84 · **9433** interception · **9981** PANS-Aerodromes · **10066** PANS-AIM.

### Freedoms of the air (p.49)
- **International Air Services Transit Agreement 1944** (ratified by India): the two **technical freedoms** for scheduled services: **1st** fly across without landing; **2nd** land for **non-traffic** purposes.
- **International Air Transport Agreement 1944** (**not** ratified by India): adds the **commercial freedoms**: **3rd** put down traffic **from** the home State; **4th** take on traffic **to** the home State; **5th** carry traffic between two **other** States (on a service to/from home).

### Unlawful acts conventions (p.50–53)
- **Tokyo 1963** (offences and certain other acts on board): applies to **penal offences** and acts jeopardising safety or good order, on aircraft of a contracting State in flight or on the high seas. In flight = **power applied for take-off until the landing run ends**. Not to military, customs or police aircraft.
- Tokyo: **State of registration** has jurisdiction. Another State may interfere with an aircraft in flight only if the offence affects its territory, involves its nationals/residents, is against its security, breaks its flight rules, or is needed for a multilateral obligation.
- Tokyo, commander's powers: for this purpose in flight = **all external doors closed after embarkation until a door opens for disembarkation**. Commander may impose **reasonable measures including restraint**; may **require** crew help and **request (not require)** passenger help. Restraint ends at landing unless the State refuses disembarkation, forced landing, or the person agrees. Notify the authorities of the landing State, if possible **before landing**.
- **Hague 1970**: suppression of **unlawful seizure** (hijacking); severe penalties and **extradition**.
- **Montreal 1971**: unlawful acts against the **safety of civil aviation**: violence on board, destroying/damaging aircraft, placing devices, damaging navigation facilities, false information.
- **Montreal Protocol 1988**: extends to **violence at airports** serving international civil aviation.
- **Montreal 1991**: **marking of plastic explosives** for detection.
- India has ratified Tokyo, Hague, Montreal 1971 and 1991, and the 1988 Protocol. **Beijing 2010** (aircraft as weapons, BCN weapons, cyber attacks): **India has not ratified**.

### Liability conventions (p.54–56)
- **Warsaw 1929**: international carriage of persons, baggage and goods **for reward** (and free carriage by an air transport undertaking); not postal. Unifies **documents (ticket, baggage check), carrier liability and jurisdiction**. Loss of ticket does not void the contract, but carrying a passenger without a ticket loses the liability limits. Gross negligence removes the limit.
- Amendments: Hague Protocol 1955, Guadalajara 1961, Guatemala City 1971, Montreal Protocols 1–4 1975, **Montreal Convention 1999** (replaces the Warsaw system). India ratified **Warsaw and the Hague Protocol** (per the book).
- Montreal 1999: **no financial limit** for death/injury; up to **113,100 SDR** the carrier cannot contest; above it the carrier may prove it was not at fault.
- **Carriage by Air Act, 1972** (amended 2009) governs carriers' rights and liabilities in India, domestic and international.
- **Rome 1952**: damage by foreign aircraft to **third parties on the ground**; operator liable, limit proportionate to aircraft **weight**, compulsory insurance. India has **not** ratified. Montreal Protocol 1978 amended it.
- **Geneva 1948**: rights in aircraft (seller's rights under hire purchase, lease, mortgage). **Cape Town 2001** convention and protocol: international interests in mobile equipment (aircraft finance); India ratified in **2008**.
- **Denunciation**: written notification to **ICAO** as Depositary. Security conventions (Tokyo, Hague, Montreal 1971, 1988 Protocol, Beijing): **6 months**. Montreal 1999: **90 days**.

### Indian organisations (p.53–54)
- **Ministry of Civil Aviation** (Rajiv Gandhi Bhavan, Safdarjung, New Delhi): national policy; administers the Aircraft Act and Rules; controls **DGCA, BCAS, IGRUA**, AAI, Pawan Hans.
- **DGCA**: the **regulator**, primarily safety: registration, airworthiness and C of A, licensing of pilots, AMEs, flight engineers and ATCOs, aerodrome and CNS/ATM certification, AOCs, accident prevention, amending rules/CARs to match ICAO, noise and emissions, dangerous goods approvals.
- **AAI**: formed **1 April 1995** by merging IAAI and NAA: builds and runs airports, **manages Indian airspace** (including beyond territorial limits as accepted by ICAO), provides **ATS, CNS aids and AIS** (AIP, NOTAM, AIRAC, PIB).

### Exam traps
- Over the **high seas**: rules **established under the Convention** (Annex 2), not those of any State.
- Difference from a Standard: **immediate** notice; from an **amended** Standard: within **60 days**.
- Annexes with **Standards only: 2, 5, 7, 8**.
- **Cabotage** = traffic between two points in the same foreign State (Art 7).
- 5th freedom involves **two other States**. India ratified the **Transit** agreement (freedoms 1–2), not the Transport agreement.
- Tokyo: in flight for jurisdiction = take-off power to end of landing run; for commander's powers = **doors closed to doors open**.
- Council = **36**; ANC = **19**; Assembly at least every **3 years**.

## 3. Aircraft Nationality and Registration Marks

### Annex 7 basics (p.69)
- Annex 7 covers how States choose **nationality marks** from the **radio call-sign symbols allotted by the ITU**, the letters/numbers used, where marks go, and the **certificate of registration** (which must be **carried in the aircraft at all times**).
- **Common mark**: for aircraft of an international operating agency registered jointly by two or more States; symbols **4YA to 4YZ** allotted by the ITU; the common mark is assigned to the common mark registering authority by **ICAO**.
- **Identification plate**: at least the **nationality (or common) mark and registration mark**, made of **fireproof metal** or similar, secured in a prominent position **near the main entrance**. Unmanned free balloon: on the exterior of the payload. RPA: near the main entrance/compartment, or on the exterior.
- **Registration mark**: letters, numbers or both, **1 to 5 characters**, assigned by the **State of Registry or common mark registering authority**.
- Letter combinations must not be confusable with the **five-letter** combinations of the International Code of Signals, the **three-letter Q-code** groups, **SOS**, or urgent signals such as **XXX, PAN, TTT**.

### Size of marks (p.70)
- Heavier-than-air: on **wings at least 50 cm**; on **fuselage and vertical tail at least 30 cm**; letters and numbers in each group of **equal height**.
- Lighter-than-air (other than unmanned free balloons): **at least 50 cm**. Unmanned free balloons: as decided by the State of Registry, according to payload size.

### Indian provisions (p.70–73)
- Registering authority in India: the **Central Government** (register kept by the **DGCA**, open to public inspection and on the DGCA website).
- C of R contents: aircraft type, constructor's number, year of manufacture, nationality and registration marks, owner's name, nationality and address, usual station, date of registration and validity. Leased aircraft: also lease validity and lessor/lessee details.
- India was allotted **AT to AW** by the ITU but keeps the pre-independence marks: **VT** for civil aircraft, **VU** for Air Force aircraft, then a **hyphen and three letters** (e.g. **VT-EPC**), Roman characters without ornamentation.
- Indian **unmanned aircraft**: **U followed by 6 alphanumeric characters** (e.g. UB67DFC).
- Marks are **painted** or fixed with similar permanency, inscribed with the owner's full name and address on the **owner's name plate**, and kept **clean and visible**.
- **Cancellation by DGCA** if: registration not per the rules; obtained by **false information**; aircraft more suitably registered elsewhere; **destroyed or permanently withdrawn**; inexpedient in the public interest; the **lease** expired or was terminated; or the **C of A has expired for 5 years or more**.
- No **advertisement** or lettering except as permitted by the DGCA. The aircraft's name and the owner's name/emblem are allowed if they cannot be confused with the marks. Civil aircraft must not bear **State aircraft marks**. National flags may be shown if not confusable with military markings.

### Leasing (p.73)
- **Lease**: lessor furnishes an aircraft to a lessee for compensation or hire, for a period or number of flights. **Lessor** furnishes; **lessee** uses.
- The **lessee operator** must hold the economic and operating authority and exercise **operational control**, providing flight and cabin crew, ground staff, dispatchers and facilities (i.e. a dry lease).
- Leasing companies registered in the **IFSC** at **GIFT City**, Gandhinagar (India's first IFSC, regulated by the **IFSCA**) may lease aircraft.

### Exam traps
- Fuselage/tail marks **30 cm**; wings and lighter-than-air **50 cm**.
- Registration mark assigned by the **State of Registry or common mark registering authority** (not ITU, not ICAO).
- Common mark assigned by **ICAO**; symbols allotted to ICAO by the **ITU**.
- Avoid confusion with **XXX, PAN, TTT, SOS**, 3-letter **Q** groups and 5-letter code-of-signals groups.
- India: **VT** civil, **VU** Air Force; ITU block **AT–AW** unused.
- Registration cancelled if C of A expired **5 years or more**.

## 4. Rules of the Air

### Applicability and responsibility (p.77–78)
- Rules apply to all aircraft **over Indian territory** and to **Indian-registered aircraft wherever they are**, unless they conflict with the rules of the State overflown. Over the high seas where a State provides ATS by regional agreement, the "appropriate ATS authority" is that State's.
- Every operation in flight or on the movement area follows the **general rules**, plus in flight either **VFR or IFR**.
- The **PIC** is responsible for complying with the rules **whether manipulating the controls or not**, and may depart from them only when **absolutely necessary for safety**. The PIC has **final authority** as to the disposition of the aircraft.
- **Pre-flight action**: become familiar with all available information; for flights away from the vicinity of an aerodrome and **all IFR flights**, study current weather reports and forecasts, fuel requirements and an alternative course of action.
- **Safety-sensitive personnel** must not work under the influence of any psychoactive substance that impairs performance, nor engage in problematic use.

### General rules (p.78–81)
- No **negligent or reckless** operation.
- **Minimum safe height** over congested areas or open-air assemblies: high enough to land **without undue hazard** after an emergency (unless taking off/landing or with DG permission). DG may permit crop spraying, mining exploration at specified heights.
- **Cruising levels**: **flight levels** at or above the lowest usable FL / above TA; **altitudes** below the lowest usable FL / at or below TA.
- **Dropping or spraying**: only under **Rule 26** of the Aircraft Rules 1937 and as cleared by ATS. **Parachute descents**: only in emergency or by written order of the Central Government.
- **Towing**: no aircraft shall tow an aircraft; **towing of aircraft is not permitted in India**; objects only as prescribed by the DG.
- **Aerobatics**: licensed pilot (or trainee with an instructor); passengers' **written consent**; everyone strapped in; **complete the manoeuvre by 600 m (2,000 ft) AGL** (or higher if the C of A says). Not hazardous to traffic; **not within 2 NM of an aerodrome perimeter below 1,800 m (6,000 ft)**; not over a city, town, village or populous area, nor over a public gathering unless the promoters applied in writing to the DG.
- **Formation flight**: **not allowed for civil aircraft in India**. ICAO allows it by pre-arrangement: formation acts as a **single aircraft** for navigation and reporting, separation within it is the leader's and pilots' responsibility, each aircraft within **1 km (0.5 NM)** laterally/longitudinally and **30 m (100 ft)** vertically of the leader.
- **ACAS RA**: nothing relieves the PIC of taking avoiding action, including following resolution advisories.

### Right of way (p.80–81)
- The aircraft with right of way **maintains heading and speed**. The one giving way must not pass **over, under or in front** unless well clear, allowing for wake turbulence.
- **Head-on**: **both alter heading to the right**.
- **Converging** at about the same level: the aircraft that has the other **on its right gives way**, except: **power-driven heavier-than-air gives way to airships, gliders and balloons; airships to gliders and balloons; gliders to balloons**; power-driven aircraft give way to aircraft **seen to be towing**.
- **Overtaking**: approaching from the rear within **70°** of the other's plane of symmetry (at night you could see neither navigation light, only the white tail light). The overtaken aircraft has right of way; the overtaker keeps clear by **altering heading to the right**, whether climbing, descending or level, until entirely past and clear.
- **Landing**: aircraft in flight or on the ground/water give way to aircraft **landing or on final**. Of two landing aircraft, the **higher gives way to the lower**, but the lower must not cut in front of one on final; **power-driven heavier-than-air aircraft give way to gliders**.
- **Emergency landing**: give way to an aircraft known to be compelled to land.
- **Take-off**: aircraft taxiing on the manoeuvring area give way to aircraft **taking off or about to take off**.
- **Surface movement**: head-on → **stop or turn right**; converging → the one with the other **on its right** gives way; overtaken has right of way. Stop at **all runway-holding positions** unless cleared, and at **all lighted stop bars** (proceed when they are switched off).

### Lights to be displayed (p.81–82, 113–115)
- **Sunset to sunrise**, aircraft **in flight**: **anti-collision lights** and **navigation lights**; no lights that can be mistaken for them. Landing lights and floodlights may be added for conspicuity.
- On the **movement area** at night: navigation lights; lights showing the **extremities** of the structure (unless stationary and lit); lights to attract attention; lights showing **engines running**.
- Aircraft fitted with anti-collision lights display them **also by day**, in flight and on the movement area. The pilot may switch off or dim flashing lights that hinder duties or dazzle observers.
- **Navigation lights**: **red** on the left, **110°** (from dead ahead to 110° left); **green** on the right, **110°**; **white** tail light, **140°** (70° each side of dead astern).
- Seeing **red + green** → head-on; only **white** → it is moving away; **red + white** → crossing right to left; **green + white** → crossing left to right. A light that stays at a **constant relative bearing** = collision risk: **green** → you have right of way; **red** → give way by turning right. ("Red to red, green to green: safe.")

### Simulated instrument flight (p.82)
- Only with **fully functioning dual controls** and a qualified **safety pilot** in a control seat with adequate forward and side vision (or a competent observer in communication with the safety pilot).

### At or near an aerodrome (p.82)
- Observe other traffic; conform with or avoid the pattern; **all turns to the left** after take-off and before landing unless told otherwise; land and take off **into wind** unless safety or traffic says otherwise.
- Where a tower operates: keep a **continuous listening watch** on its frequency (or watch for visual signals) and get **prior authorisation** (radio or visual) for taxi, take-off and landing manoeuvres.

### Flight plans (p.83–84)
- In India, every flight's information goes to the ATS unit, except **nano RPA up to 50 ft** and **micro RPA up to 200 ft AGL** in uncontrolled airspace or enclosed premises.
- A flight plan is required for: any flight to receive **ATC service**; any **IFR flight in advisory airspace**; flights in designated areas/routes to facilitate FIS, alerting and SAR, or coordination with military/adjacent units to avoid interception; and **any flight across international borders**.
- Before departure the operator ensures approval for any **RNP/RVSM/RCP/RSP** specification on the route.
- Submit **at least 180 minutes (3 h)** before and **not earlier than 120 hours** before **EOBT**. **RPA**: at least **24 hours** before ETD.
- Delay of **15 minutes or more**: notify ATC of the revised time (amend, or cancel and refile).
- Contents: relevant items **up to and including the alternate aerodrome(s)**.
- **Changes**: IFR and controlled VFR flights report **all changes**; other VFR report **significant** changes. Wrong **endurance or persons on board** at departure is a significant change.
- **Closing**: an **arrival report** in person, by RTF or data link, at the earliest moment after landing, for any flight whose plan covered the whole flight or the rest of it to destination.

### Time (p.84)
- **UTC**, hours and minutes (seconds if needed) of the 24-hour day from midnight. **Time check before a controlled flight**. ATS clocks within **±30 s** of UTC; data link time within **1 s**.

### ATC clearances and deviations (p.85–86)
- Clearance must be obtained **before** a controlled flight, requested by submitting a flight plan. Adhere to the **current flight plan** unless a change is requested and cleared.
- **Off track**: adjust heading to regain track **forthwith**. **Deviation from assigned Mach/IAS**: inform ATS **immediately**.
- **Mach/TAS** at cruising level varies by **±M 0.02** or **±10 kt (19 km/h) TAS** from the flight plan: inform ATS.
- **Time estimate** for the next reporting point, FIR boundary or destination changes by **more than 2 minutes**: notify ATS (automatic via ADS-C where active).
- **VFR controlled flight losing VMC**: request an amended clearance to stay VMC (to destination/alternate or to leave controlled airspace); or if none, continue VMC and tell ATC you are leaving or landing at the nearest suitable aerodrome; or in a **CTR** request **special VFR**; or request **IFR**.
- **Position reports**: time and level over each **compulsory reporting point**, and others on request.
- **Communications**: controlled flights keep a **continuous air-ground voice watch**; **SELCAL** satisfies this; the watch continues **after CPDLC** is established.

### Radio communication failure (p.86–87)
- **In VMC**: continue in VMC, **land at the nearest suitable aerodrome**, report arrival to ATC by the quickest means (or complete IFR if advisable).
- **In IMC / IFR**: **non-radar** airspace: keep last assigned **speed and level** (or minimum flight altitude if higher) for **20 minutes** after failing to report over a compulsory reporting point, then follow the **filed flight plan**. **Radar** airspace: the same for **7 minutes** after the later of reaching the last assigned level/MFA, **setting 7600**, or missing a compulsory report.
- If being **vectored** or offset by RNAV, rejoin the flight-plan route **no later than the next significant point**.
- Proceed to the destination aid/fix, hold if needed, **begin descent at or close to the EAT** last acknowledged (or the flight-plan **ETA** if no EAT), fly the normal instrument approach, and **land within 30 minutes** after ETA or last EAT, whichever is later.

### VMC minima (p.87–88)
| Altitude band | Class | Flight visibility | Distance from cloud |
|---|---|---|---|
| At and above 3,050 m (10,000 ft) AMSL | A–G | **8 km** | **1,500 m horizontally, 300 m (1,000 ft) vertically** |
| Below 10,000 ft and above 3,000 ft AMSL or 1,000 ft above terrain (higher) | A–G | **5 km** | 1,500 m horizontally, 1,000 ft vertically |
| At or below 3,000 ft AMSL or 1,000 ft above terrain (higher) | A–E | **5 km** | 1,500 m horizontally, 1,000 ft vertically |
| same band | F, G | **5 km** | **clear of cloud and in sight of the surface** |
- If the TA is below 10,000 ft, use **FL 100** instead of 10,000 ft. Class A minima are guidance only (VFR not accepted in Class A).
- ATS may permit visibility down to **3,000 m** (slow enough to see and avoid, or low traffic, aerial work); helicopters down to **1,500 m**. In India, F and G low band: **3,000 m**, helicopters **1,000 m**.

### Visual flight rules (p.88–89)
- Without ATC clearance, VFR must not take off, land or enter the ATZ/circuit in a **CTR** when **ceiling < 450 m (1,500 ft)** or **ground visibility < 5 km**.
- India: VFR only from **20 minutes before sunrise to 20 minutes after sunset** (except local and flying-club training flights cleared by ATC). The book defines **flight by night** as from **½ hour after sunset to ½ hour before sunrise**.
- Unless authorised, VFR not **above FL 150**, not at **transonic/supersonic** speed, not **more than 100 NM seaward** in controlled airspace. Never above **FL 290** where RVSM applies.
- VFR minimum heights: over congested areas or assemblies **300 m (1,000 ft) above the highest obstacle within 600 m**; elsewhere **150 m (500 ft)** above ground or water.
- VFR in level cruise **above 900 m (3,000 ft)** above ground/water uses the **cruising levels for its track**.
- VFR complies with ATC in **Classes B, C, D**, as aerodrome traffic at controlled aerodromes, and as special VFR.
- VFR changing to IFR: send changes to the flight plan, or file one, and **get a clearance** before proceeding IFR in controlled airspace.

### Special VFR (p.89)
- Authorised by the **approach control unit** when traffic permits, each request individually.
- **Ground visibility ≥ 1,500 m**: may enter a CTR to land, take off and depart, cross, or operate locally. **Performance Class 1 and 2 helicopters: ≥ 1,000 m** (state it in field 18 and on RTF).
- In India, special VFR only by pilots with an **instrument rating, Assistant Flight Instructor or Flight Instructor rating**.

### Instrument flight rules (p.89–91)
- Suitable instruments and navigation equipment for the route.
- **Minimum level** (where the State has none): over **high terrain or mountains**, **600 m (2,000 ft)** above the highest obstacle within **8 km** of the estimated position; elsewhere **300 m (1,000 ft)** within **8 km**.
- **IFR to VFR**: tell ATS specifically "**IFR flight cancelled**"; do so only if the flight will continue for a reasonable time in **uninterrupted VMC**.
- IFR in controlled airspace: cruising levels (or cruise climb) from the tables; modified tables above **FL 410**. Outside controlled airspace: levels appropriate to track (except at or below **3,000 ft AMSL** if specified); keep a watch and report position where required. Advisory service users: flight plan changes are **not** subject to clearance.

### Distress and urgency signals (p.91–92)
- **Distress** (grave and imminent danger, immediate help): **SOS** (···---···), **MAYDAY**, data-link MAYDAY, **red rockets or shells fired one at a time at short intervals**, **red parachute flare**.
- **Urgency** (must land, no immediate help needed): **repeated switching on and off of landing lights**, or of navigation lights (distinct from flashing).
- **Very urgent message** about the safety of a ship, aircraft, vehicle or person: **XXX**, **PAN PAN**, data-link PAN PAN.

### ADIZ and air defence clearance (p.92)
- ADIZs: **North, South, East, West, Central** and sub-ADIZ South East.
- **ADC** required before take-off through ATC for flights to, through or within an ADIZ, except **local flights within 5 NM of the ARP and below 1,000 ft AGL**.
- One ADC is valid for the **entire route**. Flights entering from abroad get ADC from the FIC **10 minutes before entering Indian airspace**.
- Validity: **EOBT −15 to +45 minutes** (scheduled, RPL/FPL); non-scheduled domestic **−15 minutes to +3 hours**. If communications fail at departure, a radio-equipped aircraft may take off and get ADC immediately after airborne.
- Flying clubs without ATC: ADC from the nearest **IAF ATC unit**. Return flights the same day may get ADC for the return too, but need a fresh one if **delayed more than 30 minutes**.

### Interception (p.92–96)
- **Intercepted aircraft**: follow the interceptor's instructions; contact on **121.5 MHz** (repeat on **243 MHz** if practicable); **squawk A 7700**; select ADS-B/ADS-C emergency mode. If radio instructions from other sources conflict with the interceptor's **visual** signals, **follow the visual signals** and ask for clarification; if they conflict with the interceptor's **radio** instructions, follow the **interceptor's radio** instructions.
- Call signs: "INTERCEPT CONTROL", "INTERCEPTOR (call sign)", "INTERCEPTED AIRCRAFT". Phrases are said twice: interceptor — CALL SIGN, FOLLOW, DESCEND, YOU LAND, PROCEED; intercepted — CALL SIGN, WILCO, CAN NOT, REPEAT, AM LOST, MAYDAY, HIJACK, LAND (place), DESCEND.
- **Series 1**: interceptor **rocks wings and flashes nav lights** from slightly above, ahead and normally to the **left** (right for a helicopter), then a slow level turn = "**You have been intercepted, follow me**". Reply: rock wings, flash lights, follow.
- **Series 2**: **abrupt break-away**, climbing turn of 90° or more without crossing the intercepted aircraft's path = "**You may proceed**". Reply: rock wings.
- **Series 3**: **gear down, steady landing lights, overfly the runway** = "**Land at this aerodrome**". Reply: same and land if safe.
- **Series 4** (from intercepted): **gear up and flashing landing lights** while passing over the runway at **1,000–2,000 ft** (helicopter **170–330 ft**), circling = "**Aerodrome is inadequate**".
- **Series 5**: **regular switching on and off of all lights** = "**Cannot comply**". **Series 6**: **irregular flashing of all lights** = "**In distress**".
- Warning of a **restricted, prohibited or danger area**: projectiles from the ground every **10 seconds** bursting into **red and green** lights/stars.

### Light signals from the tower (p.96–97)
| Signal | In flight | On the ground |
|---|---|---|
| Steady green | **Cleared to land** | **Cleared for take-off** |
| Steady red | **Give way and continue circling** | **Stop** |
| Green flashes | **Return for landing** | **Cleared to taxi** |
| Red flashes | **Aerodrome unsafe, do not land** | **Taxi clear of the landing area** |
| White flashes | **Land here and proceed to apron** | **Return to starting point** |
| Red pyrotechnic | **Do not land for the time being** (whatever earlier instructions) | — |
- Acknowledgement in flight: by day **rock the wings** (not expected on base or final); by night **flash landing lights twice** (or nav lights). On the ground: by day **move ailerons or rudder**; by night flash landing (or nav) lights twice.

### Visual ground signals (p.97–98)
- **Red square with two yellow diagonals**: **landing prohibited**, likely to be prolonged.
- **Red square with one yellow diagonal**: **special precautions** in approach/landing (bad manoeuvring area).
- **White dumbbell**: land, take off and **taxi on runways and taxiways only**. **Dumbbell with black bars across each disc**: land and take off on runways only; other manoeuvres need not be confined.
- **White or yellow crosses** on runways/taxiways: area **unfit for movement**.
- **White or orange landing T**: land and take off **parallel to the shaft towards the cross-arm** (lit or outlined in white at night).
- **Two digits displayed vertically** near the tower: take-off direction in tens of degrees magnetic.
- **Right-hand arrow** of conspicuous colour: **right-hand circuit**.
- **Black C on yellow**, displayed vertically: **ATS reporting office**.
- **Double white cross**: **gliders operating**.

### Marshalling (p.98–112)
- Only signalmen **trained, qualified and approved by the DGCA** may guide aircraft; they wear a **fluorescent vest**; by day fluorescent wands, bats or gloves; **illuminated wands at night** or in low visibility.
- Signalman faces the aircraft, for fixed-wing on the **left side** where the pilot sees best. Engines are numbered from the **signalman's right to left** (No. 1 = port outer).
- Key signals: **straight ahead** (bent arms, wands up and down chest to head); **turn** (one arm out at 90°, the other beckoning); **normal stop** (arms out at 90°, slowly raised until wands cross); **emergency stop** (abruptly raise and cross); **set brakes** (open palm closed to fist); **release brakes** (fist opened); **chocks inserted** (wands jab inward to touch) / **removed** (jab outward); **start engine** (right hand circling, left pointing at engine); **cut engines** (slicing motion across the throat); **slow down** (patting downwards); **move back** (arms rotate forward at waist); **all clear** (right arm up / thumbs up); **hold position** (arms down at 45°); **dispatch** (salute); **do not touch controls**; **connect/disconnect ground power** (a "T" over the head).
- Helicopters: **hover** (arms out at 90°), **move up/down** (palms up/down), **move left/right**, **land** (arms crossed downwards in front).
- ARFF emergency hand signals (from the **left front**): **recommend evacuation** (beckoning arm), **recommended stop** (arms crossed at wrists in front of head), **emergency contained** (umpire's "safe" signal), **fire** (fanning wand pointing at the fire).
- Pilot to signalman: **brakes engaged** (fingers extended, then clench fist); **released** (fist, then extend fingers); **insert chocks** (palms out, hands move in to cross in front of face); **remove chocks** (crossed hands move out).

### Cruising levels (p.112–113)
- **RVSM** (1,000 ft separation) between **FL 290 and FL 410 inclusive** in India.
- Magnetic track **000–179°**: IFR **odd** levels (FL 10, 30, 50 … 290, 310 … 410), VFR **odd + 500** (FL 35, 55 … 285). Track **180–359°**: IFR **even** (FL 20, 40 … 280, 300 … 400), VFR **even + 500** (FL 45, 65 … 285).
- Above **FL 410**: 000–179 → FL 450, 490 …; 180–359 → FL 430, 470 … (2,000 ft steps each way).
- Non-RVSM (CVSM) above FL 290: eastbound 290, 330, 370, 410 …; westbound 310, 350, 390, 430 … (4,000 ft between same-direction levels).
- **VFR not allowed in and above RVSM airspace**.
- Polar areas above 70° latitude use grid tracks.

### Unlawful interference (p.115–116)
- Try to keep the **assigned track and level** until you can notify ATS or are in radar/ADS-B cover.
- If forced to leave track or level without RTF contact: broadcast warnings on the VHF in use or the emergency frequency, use transponder/data link, follow Doc 7030 contingency procedures, or else fly a level offset from normal IFR levels by **500 ft where 1,000 ft separation applies** (**1,000 ft where 2,000 ft applies**).

### Exam traps
- Head-on: **both turn right**. Converging: the one with the other **on its right** gives way. Overtaking: overtaker turns **right**; within **70°** of the tail.
- Powered aircraft give way to **airships, gliders, balloons**; gliders give way only to **balloons**.
- Of two landing aircraft, the **lower** has priority, but powered aircraft still give way to **gliders**.
- Flight plan **≥ 3 h before**, **≤ 120 h before** EOBT; delay **15 min** → tell ATC.
- RCF: **20 min** (non-radar) / **7 min** (radar); land within **30 min** of ETA/EAT.
- VMC: **8 km** at and above 10,000 ft, else **5 km**; **1,500 m / 1,000 ft** from cloud; F/G low: clear of cloud, surface in sight.
- Steady red in flight = **give way and keep circling**; red flashes in flight = **aerodrome unsafe**; red pyro = **do not land for the time being**.
- Two yellow diagonals = **landing prohibited**; one diagonal = **special precautions**.
- Aerobatics must **finish by 2,000 ft AGL**; not within **2 NM** of an aerodrome below **6,000 ft**.
- Interception: follow **visual** signals over other radio instructions; squawk **7700**; 121.5 MHz.

## 5. Air Traffic Services

### Objectives and divisions (p.135–136)
- ATS objectives: (a) prevent **collisions between aircraft**; (b) prevent collisions **on the manoeuvring area** between aircraft and obstructions; (c) **expedite and maintain an orderly flow**; (d) give **advice and information** for safe, efficient flight; (e) **notify** organisations about aircraft needing **SAR** and assist them.
- **ATC service** achieves a, b, c and is split into: **area control** (a, c) for controlled flights en route; **approach control** (a, c) for the arrival and departure parts; **aerodrome control** (a, b, c) for aerodrome traffic.
- **Flight information service** achieves (d); **alerting service** achieves (e).
- India (AIP): establish radio contact with aerodrome/approach control **before taxiing** and before entering **Class C or D**. In C and D only **direct controller–pilot** communication (no interpreter); aircraft need **two-way VHF and a radio compass**. To enter, cross or operate in a **CTR or ATZ**, request a clearance, pass position, level, track and crossing estimate, keep a listening watch and obey instructions.

### Designating airspace (p.136–137)
- Where FIS and alerting are given → **FIR**. Where ATC is given to **IFR** flights → **control areas / control zones**. Where ATC is also given to **VFR** → Classes **B, C or D**. CTAs and CTRs inside an FIR are part of it. Aerodromes with ATC for aerodrome traffic → **controlled aerodromes**.

### Airspace classes (p.137–139)
| Class | Flights | Separation | Service | Speed limit | Radio | Clearance |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | IFR only | all from all | ATC | — | continuous 2-way | yes |
| **B** | IFR, VFR | **all from all** | ATC | — | continuous | yes |
| **C** | IFR | IFR from IFR **and VFR** | ATC | — | continuous | yes |
| C | VFR | VFR from IFR | ATC for separation from IFR; VFR/VFR traffic info | **250 kt below 10,000 ft** | continuous | yes |
| **D** | IFR | IFR from IFR | ATC + traffic info about VFR | 250 kt < 10,000 ft | continuous | yes |
| D | VFR | **nil** | traffic info on all others | 250 kt | continuous | yes |
| **E** | IFR | IFR from IFR | ATC + traffic info on VFR as practical | 250 kt | continuous | yes |
| E | VFR | nil | traffic info as practical | 250 kt | **no** | **no** |
| **F** | IFR | IFR from IFR **as far as practical** | **advisory** + FIS | 250 kt | continuous | **no** |
| F | VFR | nil | FIS | 250 kt | no | no |
| **G** | IFR | nil | FIS | 250 kt | continuous | no |
| G | VFR | nil | FIS | 250 kt | no | no |
- **Controlled airspace = A–E**. Class E **not used for control zones**. (FL 100 replaces 10,000 ft when the TA is lower.)
- India: A and B "under consideration" (designated parts of TMA/CTA/CTR). **C** = designated controlled airspace; **D** = controlled airspace; **E** = ATS route segments **outside** controlled airspace; **F** = designated ATS route segments outside controlled airspace (advisory); **G** = outside ATS routes and outside controlled airspace.
- Class C around airports handling **> 20 movements/hour** with primary and secondary radar: **4,000 ft to FL 285**; up to **30 NM** around them is **Class D**; above FL 285 proposed **Class A**. VFR into Class C needs a **pre-departure clearance**; follow SID/STAR speed limits; VFR remains responsible for **terrain clearance**.
- Where classes adjoin vertically, a flight at the common level gets the **less restrictive** class (B less restrictive than A, C less than B…).
- Classification inside **restricted areas** applies only to parts controlled by civil ATC under **flexible use of airspace**.

### ATS units and FIRs (p.139–140)
- **FICs** give FIS and alerting outside controlled airspace. An FIR includes all airspace within its lateral limits unless limited by an **upper FIR (UIR)**.
- **Location indicators**: 4 letters. ICAO divides the world into **22 AFSRAs** (non-overlapping). **1st letter = AFSRA** (India: **V**), **2nd = State/FIR** (India: **A** Mumbai, **E** Kolkata and Guwahati sub-FIR, **I** Delhi, **O** Chennai), **3rd–4th = place** (e.g. **VIDP** Delhi, **VABB** Mumbai, **VOMM** Chennai). Assigned by States, supervised by ICAO.
- Chart identifiers: **NDB** 2 (sometimes 3) letters (SP Sarsawa); **VOR** 3 letters (BBB Mumbai); **ILS** 4 letters **starting with I** (IGHT); aerodrome 4 letters; **significant point 5 letters with two vowels** (GOLEM); **routes** one letter + **1–999** (A791), domestic India **W** routes; A, B, G, R, W spoken **Amber, Blue, Green, Red, White**; SID/STAR = fix name + number + letter (LETPU 1D, SSB 1A).
- **P/R/D areas**: nationality letters + **P/R/D** + number not repeated in the State, e.g. **VA(P)-2** (Mumbai FIR prohibited area 2), **VO(R)-81**, **VE(D)-72**. Prohibited = **not at any time**; danger area times notified by **NOTAM**.
- **TSA** (temporary segregated area): **exclusive** use, **no transit**. **TRA** (temporary reserved area): reserved but **transit possible with ATC clearance**. Both numbered **501–999** in one series: Mumbai **501–600**, Kolkata **601–700**, Delhi **701–800**, Chennai **801–900**, 901–999 reserved. E.g. TSA801(A) [Thanjavur].

### Local and training flights (p.141)
- Operators' local training/test flights: flight plan as for non-scheduled flights. Flying clubs in the ATZ may file by fax or phone.
- Where no ATZ or local flying area exists, the **vicinity of the aerodrome** for VFR = **5 NM radius of the ARP, up to 3,000 ft AGL**.
- Flying-club/GA cross-country from fields without an ARO: file with the **FIC** by fax/phone, with accumulated EETs to FIR boundaries and the PIC's phone number in field 18.

### Control areas and zones (p.141–142)
- CTA lower limit **not less than 200 m (700 ft)** above ground or water. Upper FIR/CTA may cover several lower ones.
- **CTR** lateral limits at least **9.3 km (5 NM)** from the aerodrome centre in the directions of approach; may contain several aerodromes. Holding aircraft count as arriving.
- Naming: **ACC/FIC** after a nearby town or feature; **tower/approach** after the **aerodrome**; **CTR/CTA/FIR** after the **unit** with jurisdiction.

### Emergencies (p.142)
- An aircraft in emergency (including unlawful interference) gets **maximum consideration, assistance and priority**. It may squawk **7700** (emergency) or **7500** (unlawful interference), use ADS emergency mode, or CPDLC.

### ATC service (p.142–144)
- Provided to: **IFR in Classes A–E**; **VFR in B, C, D**; all **special VFR**; all **aerodrome traffic at controlled aerodromes**.
- Area control by an **ACC** (or by approach in a small CTR/CTA without an ACC); approach control by a tower or ACC (combined) or a separate **approach unit**; aerodrome control by the **tower**. Apron management may be the tower or a separate unit.
- Separation by clearances: **all flights in A and B**; **IFR from IFR in C, D, E**; **IFR from VFR in C**; **IFR from special VFR**; **special VFR from each other** when prescribed.
- **Mandatory read-back**: **ATC route clearances**; clearances to **enter, land on, take off from, hold short of, cross and backtrack on a runway**; **runway in use, altimeter settings, SSR codes, level, heading and speed instructions, transition levels** (even from ATIS). Controller checks the read-back. **CPDLC messages need no voice read-back.**
- **ATFM**: balances demand with capacity. AAI's **Central ATFM**: **Central Command Centre at Delhi** with Flow Management Positions; phase I at **6 airports** (Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru, Chennai, Kolkata, Hyderabad, ~**60%** of traffic), phase II 30 more.

### Flight information service (p.144–145)
- To all aircraft **under ATC** or otherwise **known to ATS** that may be affected.
- Includes SIGMET (and AIRMET — **not provided in India**), volcanic activity and ash, radioactive/toxic releases, nav-aid serviceability, aerodrome condition, unmanned free balloons; weather at departure, destination and alternates; **collision hazards in C, D, E, F, G**; surface vessels over water on request. Special air-reports passed on to other aircraft, met office and ATS units. VFR: traffic and weather that may make VFR impracticable.

### ATIS (p.145–147)
- Voice-ATIS where needed to cut R/T load, on a **discrete VHF** (or a **VOR** voice channel, **never an ILS**), **continuous and repetitive**, ideally **≤ 30 seconds**. D-ATIS identical in content and format.
- One aerodrome per ATIS; updated **immediately** on significant change; ATS prepares it; messages lettered in **ICAO alphabet order**; aircraft **acknowledge receipt** on first contact with approach/tower; ATS then gives the **current altimeter setting** (always repeated even if in ATIS).
- Order (arrival + departure): aerodrome, arr/dep indicator, contract type (D-ATIS), **designator**, time, approach type, runway(s), runway surface/braking, holding delay, **transition level**, other info, **surface wind**, **visibility/RVR**, present weather, **cloud below 5,000 ft or highest MSA** (whichever greater) and CB, temperature, dew point, **altimeter setting(s)**, significant weather/wind shear in approach and climb-out, **trend**, ATIS instructions. Visibility, weather and cloud are replaced by **CAVOK** when applicable.
- Runway surface condition terms (from Nov 2021): DRY, WET, STANDING WATER, FROST, ICE, WET ICE, SLUSH, DRY/WET SNOW, COMPACTED SNOW and combinations "on top of".

### Advisory service (p.147)
- On **Class F** ATS routes outside controlled airspace. IFR users follow controlled-flight procedures, but the flight plan and changes are **not subject to clearance**; the unit only advises on essential traffic.

### Alerting service and emergency phases (p.147–148)
- To all aircraft under **ATC**; as far as practicable to others with a **flight plan** or known to ATS; and to any aircraft subject to **unlawful interference**. **FIC or ACC** is the central collecting point and informs the **RCC**.
- **INCERFA**: no communication **30 minutes** after it was due (or from the first failed attempt, whichever earlier), or aircraft **fails to arrive within 30 minutes of ETA** (later of notified or estimated).
- **ALERFA**: inquiries after INCERFA reveal nothing; **cleared to land but not landed within 5 minutes** of estimated landing time and no contact; efficiency impaired but forced landing **not** likely; or **unlawful interference**.
- **DETRESFA**: wider inquiries fail and distress is probable; **fuel considered exhausted** or insufficient; efficiency impaired so a **forced landing is likely**; or a forced landing is about to happen or has happened.
- Tell other aircraft in the vicinity the nature of an emergency, but **never mention unlawful interference** on R/T unless the aircraft itself did first and it will not make things worse.

### ATS route designators (p.148–150)
- Basic designator = **one letter + 1–999**; total **max 6 characters**, preferably **5**.
- **A, B, G, R**: regional, **not RNAV**. **L, M, N, P**: regional **RNAV**. **H, J, V, W**: non-regional, not RNAV. **Q, T, Y, Z**: non-regional RNAV.
- Prefix: **K** low-level helicopter route; **U** upper airspace; **S** supersonic. Suffix: **F** advisory service only; **G** FIS only.
- India: ATC clearance **≥ 10 minutes** before entering controlled airspace; to join/cross an ATS route **10 min** before if on direct VHF, **20 min** if via en-route radio. FIR boundary estimate (with level) **10 min** before entering a foreign→Indian FIR (not between Indian FIRs).
- Join or cross routes at or near a **reporting point**, crossing at **90°** at a level for the magnetic track; VFR cross outside controlled airspace at a VFR level at right angles.
- Position reports at each designated reporting point; on routes without them, after the **first 30 minutes** then **hourly**.
- Minimum flight altitude on ATS routes: **1,000 ft above the highest obstacle within 10 km either side** of centre line.
- **No flying within 15 NM of India's international border** unless on an ATS route, to/from an aerodrome inside 15 NM, or specially permitted. **Flying off ATS routes is prohibited** in Indian FIRs unless ATC authorises.
- **Strayed aircraft**: deviated significantly or reports lost. (One unit's strayed aircraft may be another's unidentified aircraft.)
- No distinction between upper and lower controlled airspace in India. Since **4 Nov 2021**, AIP ENR 3.1 = **Conventional navigation routes**, ENR 3.2 = **Area navigation routes**.

### Exam traps
- **Aerodrome** control achieves objectives **a, b and c**; area and approach achieve **a and c** only.
- Class **B**: VFR separated from **everyone**. Class **C**: VFR separated only from **IFR**. Class **D**: VFR gets **no separation**. Class **E**: VFR needs **no clearance or radio**. Class **F**: **advisory**.
- **250 kt below 10,000 ft** applies to C(VFR), D, E, F, G — not A, B or IFR in C.
- CTR at least **5 NM**; CTA base at least **700 ft**.
- INCERFA **30 min**; ALERFA **5 min** after landing clearance; **7500** hijack, **7600** radio, **7700** emergency.
- Read back **SSR codes, altimeter settings, levels, headings, speeds, runway clearances, TL**; not CPDLC.
- **ILS** voice channel never used for ATIS. ATIS ≤ **30 s**.
- India border buffer **15 NM**.

## 6. Separation Methods and Minima

### General (p.169)
- **Vertical or horizontal** separation is provided: between **all flights in A and B**; **IFR from IFR in C, D, E**; **IFR from VFR in C**; **IFR from special VFR**; **special VFR from each other** when prescribed. Exception: IFR in **D and E by day**, cleared to climb/descend **maintaining own separation in VMC**.
- **Composite separation**: vertical + horizontal combined, each element may be reduced to **not less than half** its normal value; only by **regional agreement**.

### Vertical separation (p.169–170)
- **1,000 ft below FL 290; 2,000 ft at or above FL 290**, except **1,000 ft between RVSM-approved aircraft in RVSM airspace** (FL 290–410).
- **Cruise climb is not permitted in Indian FIRs.**
- An aircraft may be cleared to a level **vacated** by another once that one **reports vacating it**, except when **severe turbulence** exists, both are in the **same holding pattern**, or **performance differences** could erode separation — then wait until the other reports at/through a level separated by the minimum.
- Pilots in direct contact may, with their agreement, be cleared to keep a specified vertical separation during climb/descent.
- **VMC climb/descent** (own separation): on request and with the other pilot's agreement, in **Classes D and E**, **daylight**, **VMC**, from **one other aircraft**; only for a portion **at or below 10,000 ft**; **essential traffic information** given; IFR flights get alternative instructions in case VMC fails.

### Horizontal separation: three kinds (p.170)
- **Lateral**, **longitudinal**, and **ATS surveillance (radar)**.

### Lateral separation (p.170–172)
- By **different geographic locations** (position reports, visual or nav-aid).
- **VOR**: radials diverging **at least 15°** and at least one aircraft **15 NM or more** from the VOR.
- **NDB**: tracks to/from the NDB diverging **at least 30°**, at least one aircraft **15 NM or more** from it.
- **GNSS/GNSS** or **VOR/GNSS**: tracks with angular difference **15–135°**; at least one aircraft at least **15 NM** (FL 010–190) or **23 NM** (FL 200–600) from the common point (ground distances). Confirm the aircraft is navigating by GNSS and not using a strategic lateral offset; **not during reported RAIM outages**.
- Different aids or RNAV: the **protected airspaces must not overlap**.

### Track definitions (p.172)
- **Same track**: angular difference **less than 45° or more than 315°** (protection areas overlap).
- **Reciprocal**: **more than 135° but less than 225°**.
- **Crossing**: everything else (**45–135°** and **225–315°**).

### Longitudinal separation by time (p.172–176)
- **Same cruising level, same track**: **15 min**; **10 min** if nav-aids allow frequent position/speed checks; **5 min** if the **preceding aircraft is ≥ 20 kt (37 km/h) TAS faster** and both departed the same aerodrome, or both reported over the same exact significant point, or a departing aircraft can be spaced 5 min where it joins the route behind an en-route one; **3 min** in those cases if the leader is **≥ 40 kt (74 km/h)** faster.
- **Same level, crossing tracks**: **15 min**; **10 min** with frequent position fixing.
- **Climbing/descending through another's level, same track**: **15 min**; **10 min** with frequent fixing; **5 min** if the level change starts **within 10 min** of the second aircraft reporting over an exact reporting point.
- **Crossing tracks, climbing/descending**: **15 min**; **10 min** with ground aids or GNSS; **5 min** if the level change starts within **10 min** of the second aircraft reporting over the common point (third-party/CPDLC clearances get a restriction to ensure it).
- **Reciprocal tracks** without lateral separation: vertical separation for **10 minutes before and after** the estimated passing time.
- Time separation may use voice reports, CPDLC or ADS-C; calculations must be **cross-checked**.

### Longitudinal separation by DME/GNSS distance (p.176–179)
- Requires **direct controller–pilot VHF voice**; all reports from the **same DME**; controllers specifically request **GNSS distance** from RNAV aircraft. GNSS from an integrated nav system counts as GNSS.
- **Same level, same track**: **20 NM** (same on-track DME / collocated waypoint / same waypoint, checked with simultaneous readings); **10 NM** if the **leader is ≥ 20 kt faster**.
- **Same level, crossing tracks** (angle **< 90°**, distances from the crossing point): **20 NM**; **10 NM** if the leader is 20 kt faster.
- **Climbing/descending, same track**: **10 NM**, one aircraft maintaining level, simultaneous readings.
- **Reciprocal tracks**: may climb/descend through each other's levels once **positively passed and at least 10 NM apart**.

### Surveillance separation in India (p.179)
- Radar / ADS-B / MLAT: **5 NM** within surveillance coverage; **3 NM** where authorised at **Delhi, Mumbai, Bengaluru, Kolkata and Shamshabad (Hyderabad)** radars. ADS-B/MLAT combined with radar → radar minima; MLAT alone → as notified.

### Summary (p.179–180)
- **15 min**: no frequent fixes — same level same/crossing track, climb/descent same/crossing track.
- **10 min**: with frequent fixes — the same cases; reciprocal tracks (no level change 10 min either side).
- **5 min**: same track, leader **20 kt** faster, common departure/reporting point; climb/descent within 10 min of the second aircraft's report; holding vs route aircraft.
- **3 min**: same track, leader **40 kt** faster.
- **DME/GNSS**: **20 NM** same level same/crossing track; **10 NM** with leader 20 kt faster, climb/descent same track, reciprocal after passing.

### Mach number technique (p.180–181)
- Turbojets on the same track (level, climbing or descending): **10 minutes** with MNT, or less by differential Mach on prescribed routes.
- RNAV distance with MNT: **80 NM (150 km)** in place of 10 minutes on same-direction tracks.
- Oceanic/remote: each aircraft holds an **assigned Mach ±0.01**, reports via HF/CPDLC/SATCOM, no speed change without clearance. Typical: **10 min** if the leader is **≥ M 0.02** faster, **9 min** for **0.03**, **8 min** for **0.04**.

### Performance-based separation (p.181–182)
- En-route RNP types: **RNP 1, 4, 10, 12.6, 20**. PBN specifications per Doc 9613.
- **RCP n** = seconds for an instruction to go up and the acknowledgement to come back (RCP 10, 60, 120, 240, 400). **RSP n** = seconds for surveillance data to reach ATS (RSP 180, 240, 400).
- Same track or crossing < 90°: **50 NM** (RNP 10 or RNP 4, RCP 240, RSP 180; ADS-C reports every 27 / 32 min); **30 NM** (RNP 2 or 4; 12 min reports); **5 min** (RNP 2, 4 or 10; 14 min reports).
- FMS/RNAV may fly conventional procedures if the AFM allows and raw data is monitored.

### Essential traffic (p.182)
- **Essential traffic**: controlled traffic that should be separated by ATC but, relative to a particular flight, is not (or will not be) separated by the minimum. **Essential traffic information** must be given to both flights.

### Exam traps
- Vertical: **1,000 ft below FL 290, 2,000 ft above**, RVSM **1,000 ft** to FL 410.
- VOR **15° and 15 NM**; NDB **30° and 15 NM**.
- Same track **< 45°**; reciprocal **135–225°**.
- 5 min needs **20 kt**; 3 min needs **40 kt**; DME 10 NM needs **20 kt**.
- Reciprocal: **10 min** either side; DME: **10 NM** after passing.
- India radar **5 NM** (3 NM at five big radars). No **cruise climb** in India.
- MNT **10 min** at **M 0.02**; RNAV + MNT **80 NM**.

## 7. Separation in the Vicinity of Aerodromes

### Departing aircraft (p.195–196)
- Departure clearances specify as needed: take-off direction and turn, heading/track before joining the cleared track, level to hold before further climb, time/point/rate of level change. Where SIDs exist, normally **clear the aircraft for the SID**.
- **1 minute** between departures if tracks **diverge by at least 45° immediately after take-off**.
- **2 minutes** if the preceding aircraft is **40 kt or more faster** and both follow the **same track**.
- **5 minutes** (while vertical separation does not exist) if a departing aircraft will climb **through the level of a preceding departure** on the same track; must be maintained or increased.

### Departing vs arriving aircraft (p.196–197)
- Arrival flying a **complete instrument approach**: departures may take off **in any direction until the arrival starts its procedure turn or base turn**; after that, only in a direction **at least 45° different from the reciprocal of the approach direction**, and **at least 3 minutes before** the arrival is estimated over the beginning of the instrument runway.
- Arrival flying a **straight-in approach**: any direction **until 5 minutes before** the arrival is over the instrument runway; in a direction **at least 45° from the reciprocal** until **3 minutes before** it is over the runway, or before it crosses a **designated fix** on the approach track.

### Reduced separation near aerodromes (p.197)
- Minima may be reduced when: the **aerodrome controller can see each aircraft continuously**; or each crew sees the other continuously and reports it can **maintain own separation**; or a following aircraft reports the preceding one **in sight** and can keep separation.

### Information for departing aircraft (p.197–198)
- Pass **without delay** significant changes in take-off/climb-out weather (surface wind, visibility, RVR, temperature for turbine aircraft, TS/CB, moderate/severe turbulence, wind shear, hail, moderate/severe icing, severe squall line, freezing precipitation, severe mountain waves, sand/dust storm, blowing snow, tornado, waterspout) and changes in **visual or non-visual aids** needed for take-off and climb, unless the crew already has them.

### Arriving aircraft (p.198–199)
- Clear arrivals for the **STAR** where established; give the **type of approach and runway** as early as possible. After coordination, the ACC may clear the first arrival straight for the approach instead of to a holding fix.
- **Visual approach**: an IFR flight completes all or part of the approach by **visual reference to terrain**. May be **requested by the pilot or initiated by the controller** (not if the crew may be unfamiliar with the aerodrome). Separation is still provided from other arriving and departing traffic. Once cleared, the **pilot is responsible for terrain clearance**. May be cleared direct to base or final.
- Pilot may request one with the **runway in sight** and visual reference to terrain if: ground visibility is not below the higher of the NPA minima or **2,800 m (Cat A/B), 3,200 m (Cat C), 3,600 m (Cat D)** — **5 km** if the runway has only a circling approach — **and** the ceiling is at or above the beginning of the initial approach segment, **or** the pilot reports at that level (or any time on the approach) that a visual approach and landing can reasonably be completed. Give a position report when requesting.
- Tell ATC at once if weather deteriorates and terrain can't be kept in sight, if you can't keep following the preceding aircraft, or if you need more spacing.
- **Instrument approach**: approach control specifies it; the crew may request another.

### Own separation in VMC (p.199–200)
- On request, with the other pilot's agreement and ATS authorisation: Classes **D and E**, **VMC, daylight**, from **one** aircraft, a specified part of climb/descent **at or below 10,000 ft**. IFR gets alternative instructions; pilot must tell ATC **before entering IMC**. Essential traffic information is given.

### Expected approach time (p.200)
- Determined for arrivals delayed **10 minutes or more**; passed as soon as practicable, preferably **before the start of initial descent** from cruise.
- Revised EAT passed without delay if it changes by **5 minutes or more**.
- Passed by the quickest means if holding is expected to last **30 minutes or more**. Identify the holding fix if not obvious.
- **Onward clearance time**: when held en route or at a fix other than the IAF, the time the aircraft can expect to **leave that fix**; also advise if more holding is expected later.

### Information for arriving aircraft (p.200–201)
- On first contact with approach, in order: **type of approach and runway in use**; **met information**; **runway surface** conditions; changes in **visual/non-visual aids**. Tell the crew at once if the approach or runway changes.
- **At the start of final approach**: significant changes in **mean surface wind** (as components: **headwind 10 kt, tailwind 2 kt, crosswind 5 kt**); wind shear/turbulence on final; **visibility/RVR** and trend.
- **During final approach**, without delay: sudden hazards (e.g. **unauthorised traffic on the runway**), significant wind variations (min/max), runway surface changes, aid status changes, RVR/visibility changes.

### Exam traps
- Departures: **1 min / 45°**, **2 min / 40 kt same track**, **5 min** climbing through.
- Arrival on full procedure: departures any direction **until procedure/base turn starts**, then **45° off the reciprocal and 3 min before**.
- Straight-in: any direction until **5 min** before; 45° off until **3 min** before.
- Visual approach visibility: **2,800 / 3,200 / 3,600 m** (A-B / C / D); circling-only runway **5 km**.
- EAT if delay **≥ 10 min**; revise if change **≥ 5 min**; quickest means if holding **≥ 30 min**.
- Significant wind change components: **10 kt head, 2 kt tail, 5 kt cross**.

## 8. Procedures for Aerodrome Control Service

### Functions of the tower (p.205–206)
- Prevent collisions between: aircraft in the tower's **area of responsibility (including circuits)**; aircraft on the **manoeuvring area**; aircraft **landing and taking off**; aircraft and **vehicles** on the manoeuvring area; aircraft and **obstructions** on the manoeuvring area.
- Continuous watch on flights on and near the aerodrome and on vehicles and people on the manoeuvring area: **visual observation**, augmented by surveillance in low visibility. Follow local rules and Temporary Local Instructions (TLI). Coordinate circuits of all aerodromes in one CTR.
- Positions: **aerodrome controller** (runway and the air around it); **ground controller** (manoeuvring area **except runways**); **clearance delivery** (start-up and ATC clearances to departing IFR flights). Separate controllers for each of simultaneously used parallel runways.
- **Alerting**: the tower alerts **rescue and fire-fighting** when an accident occurs on or near the aerodrome, when an aircraft's safety may be or has been impaired, when the crew asks, or whenever else desirable.

### Start-up, take-off and landing (p.206–207)
- Before asking for start-up or push-back: **steps/aerobridge removed and doors closed**. With the start-up request give **total persons on board including crew** and that the **security check is complete**.
- Departure order follows taxi and holding-point sequence, except for **VIP aircraft** or traffic reasons. Expect delays for adjacent push-backs, clearance restrictions, or to establish separation behind preceding traffic or an overflight at the same level.
- **No take-off or landing without ATC clearance**. **No run-up on the runway in use** without permission (run-ups in the holding pan or holding position with approval).
- Minimise **runway occupancy**: finish checks before line-up and be able to roll immediately on clearance (tell ATC before taxi if not); vacate quickly via rapid exits after landing.
- **Cruise climb not permitted** in Indian FIRs; **transonic and supersonic flight not permitted** over India.

### Traffic and taxi circuit (p.207–208)
- Clearances are issued at designated positions in the taxi and traffic circuit, where practicable without waiting for the aircraft to call. Circuit legs: **upwind, crosswind, downwind, base, final**; the visual circuit lies within about **4 NM**, with the area beyond up to **8 NM** for joining traffic.
- **Taxi clearance**: concise, to follow the right route, avoid collisions and avoid entering an active runway by mistake. If the taxi limit is **beyond a runway**, it must contain an **explicit clearance to cross or an instruction to hold short**.
- **Taxiing on the runway in use** allowed if no delay or risk. If the tower cannot see a vacating/crossing aircraft has cleared, the aircraft **reports vacated** — when the **entire aircraft is beyond the runway-holding position**.
- **Back-track** needed: tell ATC **before taxiing**. Do not **hold on the runway in use** without permission. **Only one aircraft cleared to land** on the runway at a time (except military formations).
- Aircraft are held no closer than the **runway-holding position**; where none, **50 m** from the runway edge for runways **900 m or longer**, **30 m** for shorter ones. No line-up and hold on the approach end while another aircraft is landing, until it has passed the point of intended holding.

### Information from the tower (p.208–211)
- **Before taxiing for take-off**, in order: **runway**; **surface wind** (with significant variations); **QNH** (QFE regularly by local arrangement or on request); **air temperature** (turbine aircraft); **visibility** for the take-off direction if **less than 10 km**, or RVR; **correct time**.
- **Before take-off**: significant changes in wind, temperature, visibility/RVR, and significant weather in the take-off/climb-out area (CB/TS, moderate or severe turbulence, wind shear, hail, icing, severe squall line, freezing precipitation, mountain waves, sand/dust storm, blowing snow, tornado, waterspout).
- **Before entering the circuit or starting the approach**: **runway**, **surface wind**, **QNH** (and QFE as above).
- **Essential local traffic**: any aircraft, vehicle or person on or near the manoeuvring area, or traffic nearby, that may be a hazard.
- **Runway incursion / obstructed runway** after a take-off or landing clearance: **inform** the aircraft of the obstruction and where it is; **cancel take-off clearance** if the aircraft hasn't started to roll; tell a landing aircraft to **go around**.
- **Lost on the manoeuvring area**: pilot **stops** and **simultaneously tells ATS** (last known position). If the pilot knows the aircraft is **on a runway**: **tell ATS**, **vacate** via a nearby taxiway if possible, **then stop**. A **vehicle** driver: tell ATS, vacate to a safe distance, then stop.
- **Wake turbulence**: apply the minima; where avoidance is the pilot's responsibility, warn of expected wake hazards.
- **Movement area condition**: works, rough surfaces, water/snow/slush/ice/frost, de-icing fluids, snow banks, temporary hazards incl. parked aircraft, visual aid failures, power supply failure.

### Other traffic and priorities (p.211–213)
- The tower controls persons and **vehicles (including towed aircraft)** on the manoeuvring area. In **low visibility procedures**, keep them to the **essential minimum**, protect **ILS sensitive areas** during **Cat II/III** operations, and keep vehicles clear of taxiing aircraft.
- **Emergency vehicles** going to an aircraft in distress have **priority over all other surface traffic**. **Flashing runway lights** = vacate the runway/taxiway and watch the tower for light signals.
- **Priority order**: (a) aircraft in an **emergency compelling it to land** (engine failure, acute fuel shortage, total communication failure); (b) **military fighters** (fuel-limited; avoid altering their profile, depart without delay); (c) aircraft on a **live scramble**; (d) **medical flights** (sick/injured, medevac); (e) **VVIP** flights; (f) **SAR** missions; (g) large military exercise formations; (h) urgent coordinated military/RPA operations; (i) aircraft on **large weather deviations**; (j) aircraft **landing at an alternate** after diversion.

### Suspension of VFR (p.213)
- May be suspended by **approach control or the ACC**, the **aerodrome tower**, or the **ATS authority**, always through or notified to the **tower**.
- Tower then: **holds VFR departures**; **recalls local VFR flights** or gets special VFR approval; tells approach/ACC; tells operators the reason if needed.

### Circuit, departures and landings (p.213–215)
- Clearance to **enter the circuit** when the aircraft should approach per the circuit but a landing clearance can't yet be given; may join at any position. Instrument arrivals normally land **straight in** unless visual manoeuvring is needed.
- A **landing aircraft or one on final** normally has **priority** over a departure from the same or an intersecting runway.
- Departures cleared **in the order ready**, varied to get the most departures with least average delay.
- A departure may not start take-off until the **preceding departure has crossed the end of the runway or started a turn**, or all preceding **landing aircraft are clear** of the runway. A landing aircraft may not cross the threshold until the same is true.
- **Take-off clearance** when there is reasonable assurance separation will exist at the start of the roll; includes the **runway designator**. **Immediate take-off** clearance may be given before entering the runway: taxi on and take off **in one continuous movement**.
- **Landing clearance** when separation will reasonably exist at the threshold, but **not before the preceding landing aircraft has crossed the threshold**; includes the runway designator.

### Aeronautical ground lights (p.215–216)
- Operated **sunset to sunrise**; by day when **visibility is 5,000 m or less** (India); on pilot request; whenever desirable for safety.
- Lights not used for en-route navigation may be switched off if no operation is likely, provided they can be back on **at least 1 hour before** an expected arrival. Intensity tables guide controllers; adjust on request.
- **Visual approach slope indicators** (PAPI/VASI): operated **day and night, whatever the visibility**, when the runway is in use.
- **Runway lighting** (edge, threshold, centre line, end, TDZ, wing bars) not on unless the runway is in use (or for inspection/maintenance). After a take-off, keep one runway lit for a possible emergency return: at ATC aerodromes with central control **as long as necessary**; elsewhere **at least 15 minutes**.

### Wind shear, hot spots, friction (p.216–217)
- **Wind shear**: sustained change in wind along the flight path faster than the aircraft can accelerate/decelerate; **low-level wind shear** from the surface to about **1,500 ft**. A pilot's report is passed to following aircraft until it no longer exists or is in the **ATIS** and acknowledged; also passed to the **met office**.
- **Hot spots**: locations on the movement area with collision/incursion risk, designated by the **aerodrome operator** and charted per Annex 4.
- **Friction** (compacted snow/ice): **μ ≥ 0.40 good (5)**; 0.39–0.36 medium to good (4); 0.35–0.30 medium (3); 0.29–0.26 medium to poor (2); **≤ 0.25 poor (1)**. Reported for **each third** of the runway; the **first part** is the first third **in the landing direction**.

### Exam traps
- Ground controller: manoeuvring area **except runways**.
- Taxi limit beyond a runway → **explicit cross or hold short**.
- "Runway vacated" = **whole aircraft beyond the holding position**.
- Holding without a marked position: **50 m** (runway ≥ 900 m) / **30 m** (< 900 m) from the edge.
- Before taxi info order: **runway, wind, QNH, temperature, visibility (< 10 km), time**.
- Lost on a runway: **tell ATS, vacate, then stop**; lost elsewhere: **stop and tell**.
- Lights by day if visibility **≤ 5,000 m**; back on **1 h** before arrival; runway lit **15 min** after departure.
- PAPI on **day and night** when the runway is used.
- Friction **0.40+ good, 0.25− poor**.

## 9. Use of ATS Surveillance Systems

### Surveillance systems (p.221–223)
- **ATS surveillance system**: generic term for **ADS-B, PSR, SSR** or any comparable ground system that identifies aircraft.
- **PSR** is used where other systems alone would not meet requirements. **SSR** (especially **monopulse or Mode S**) or **MLAT** may be used **alone**, including for separation, if transponder carriage is **mandatory** in the area and **identification is established and maintained**.
- **ADS-B**: aircraft automatically **broadcast** position and data to ATC **and** to other equipped aircraft. **ADS-C**: the same on-board data sent only to specific **ATSUs/AOC** under a **contract** (which data, when); the aircraft can also send unsolicited **emergency** reports.
- ADS-B advantages: pilots see what controllers see (traffic, weather, terrain), fewer **runway incursions** (shows aircraft and vehicles on the surface), **cheaper and wider coverage** than radar (remote areas), more direct routes, less fuel and emissions; the basis of **NextGen**.
- **MLAT** (multilateration): several ground stations measure the **time difference of arrival (TDOA)** of replies from Mode A/C/S, military IFF and ADS-B transponders. **No extra avionics** needed.
- **Radar vectors** are always **magnetic headings**.

### Radar services (p.223–224)
- **Radar control service** in controlled airspace; **radar advisory service** in **Class F**; **radar flight information service** to identified aircraft anywhere in the FIR.
- All need **direct two-way communication** with the radar unit — but radar **separation** may be provided between two identified aircraft when **only one** is in contact.
- Uses: better airspace use, fewer delays, more safety; **vectoring** departures (efficient climb), arrivals (descent and sequencing), to resolve conflicts, to help navigation; separating and keeping traffic flowing around an aircraft with **radio failure**; monitoring progress and **detecting deviations** from route or level.
- **Approach**: sequencing, vectoring to a pilot-interpreted aid, and **surveillance radar approaches**. **Aerodrome**: surveillance radar as an **approach monitor**; **surface movement radar** for ground traffic.
- Displays show individual or combined symbols (PSR, SSR, ADS-B, MLAT), PSR blips and SSR responses.

### SSR without primary radar; transponder failure (p.224)
- SSR alone may separate aircraft if identity is kept by **discrete codes**. A transponder-equipped aircraft and one **without a working transponder** are separated by **non-radar** separation.
- Transponder fails in flight (where carriage is mandatory): normally allowed to **continue to the next landing**. Failure detected **before departure**: ATC may authorise flight without it if **requested in the flight plan**.

### Identification (p.224–226)
- Identify the aircraft by a Doc 4444 method and **tell the pilot**; if identification is lost, tell the pilot and give instructions to restore non-surveillance separation.
- **ADS-B**: aircraft identification in the **ADS-B label**; **transfer** of identification; compliance with "**TRANSMIT ADS-B IDENT**".
- **SSR/MLAT**: aircraft identification in the label; a **verified discrete code**; Mode S aircraft identification; **transfer**; compliance with an instruction to **set a code**; compliance with "**SQUAWK IDENT**".
- **PSR**: **position report** method (blip matches a reported position and track); **departing aircraft** method (identified within **1 NM of the runway end**; beware holding, overflying or missed-approach traffic); **turn method** (one or more heading changes of **30° or more**, only one blip responding, without leaving coverage).

### Separation and speed control (p.226–227)
- Surveillance minima only between **identified** aircraft with reasonable assurance identity will be kept. **Procedural** minima when the surveillance system fails, outside coverage, or for aircraft leaving coverage/entering an adjacent FIR without surveillance transfer.
- India: see Chapter 6 (**5 NM**, 3 NM at five radars). ICAO: **5 NM** standard, reducible to **3 NM** where capability allows, and **2.5 NM** between aircraft on the **same final approach track within 10 NM of the threshold**.
- **Speed control (India)**: below **10,000 ft**, IAS **not more than 250 kt**; arrivals below 10,000 ft **within 15 NM** of the aerodrome VOR/DME **not more than 220 kt**. ATC may add restrictions or cancel them with "**No speed restriction**".

### Termination of service (p.227)
- Tell the aircraft immediately when surveillance service is interrupted or ends. It ends **automatically** when an arrival is told to **contact the tower**; give the **distance from touchdown** before the change-over.

### Transponder procedures in India (p.227–228)
- Transponders **on at all times in flight** in the Chennai, Delhi, Guwahati, Kolkata and Mumbai FIRs, inside or outside SSR airspace.
- Departures get a code on departure and keep it unless told. **International arrivals keep the code from the adjacent FIR**. Domestic flights keep the **last assigned** code. No code assigned: **A 2000** before entering Indian FIRs.
- **Not on the ground**, except when entering the runway for take-off until vacating after landing.

### Mode C / ADS-B level verification (p.228)
- Tolerance: **±200 ft (60 m) in RVSM airspace**; **±300 ft (90 m)** elsewhere (an authority may set a smaller value, not below ±200 ft). **Geometric height is not used for separation**.
- Report the level you are **maintaining or passing on first contact** with a radar unit; the controller verifies once on initial contact by comparing with your R/T report. Within tolerance: you are **not told**. Outside: you are asked to **check the pressure setting and confirm level**; if it persists, "**STOP SQUAWK CHARLIE, WRONG INDICATION**" (or stop ADS-B altitude transmission).

### Exam traps
- ADS-B **broadcasts to all**; ADS-C reports **by contract** to specific units.
- MLAT uses **TDOA**, needs **no new avionics**.
- Radar advisory = **Class F**; radar FIS = anywhere in the FIR.
- Departing-aircraft identification within **1 NM** of runway end; turn method **≥ 30°**.
- ICAO **5 / 3 / 2.5 NM** (2.5 within 10 NM on the same final).
- Speed: **250 kt below FL 100**, **220 kt within 15 NM** for arrivals.
- Mode C tolerance **±200 ft RVSM**, **±300 ft** elsewhere. No code assigned → **2000**.

## 10. Aeronautical Information Services

### Purpose and who does it (p.233)
- AIS ensures the **flow of aeronautical data and information** needed for the safety, regularity, economy and efficiency of global ATM, in an environmentally sustainable way. Its importance grew with RNAV, PBN, computer navigation, PBC, PBS, data link and SATVOICE.
- Standard products: **AIP, AIP Amendments, AIP Supplements, AIC, NOTAM, aeronautical charts** (+ digital data sets).
- India: AIS provided by **AAI**, **except AICs, which DGCA publishes**.

### AIP (p.233–235)
- For information of a **lasting character** essential to air navigation: the basic source for **permanent information and long-duration temporary changes**. In **three parts**: **GEN, ENR, AD**.
- A **checklist** of the current date of every page is reissued frequently. Operationally significant changes follow **AIRAC** and are marked "AIRAC". Amend by **replacement sheets**, hand amendments kept to a minimum; the regular interval is stated in **GEN**.
- **AIP Amendment** = **permanent** change; consecutive serial numbers; AIRAC AMDT pages show an **effective date**; if none is due at the usual time, a **NIL notification** goes out via the monthly NOTAM list.
- **AIP Supplement** = **temporary changes of long duration (3 months or more)** and short-duration information with extensive text/graphics. Serial numbers consecutive **per calendar year**; kept while any part is valid; references the NOTAM it replaces; **checklist at least monthly** (via the monthly NOTAM list); printed on **yellow** pages; kept as the **first item** in each AIP part; errors or validity changes → a **new supplement**. If there isn't time to distribute a supplement, use a NOTAM.
- **eAIP**: AIP, AMDT, SUP and AIC in a format for screen display and printing.

### NOTAM (p.235–237)
- Types: **NOTAMN** (new), **NOTAMR** (replacement), **NOTAMC** (cancellation).
- **Trigger NOTAM**: when an AIP Supplement is published under AIRAC, a NOTAM giving a brief description, effective date/time and the supplement number, **in force on the same date**, so it appears in the **PIB** as a reminder.
- Originate a NOTAM for information that is **temporary and short-lived**, or for operationally significant permanent / long temporary changes made **at short notice** (except extensive text/graphics).
- **Not** by NOTAM: routine apron/taxiway maintenance not affecting safety; runway marking work when other runways are usable or equipment can be removed; temporary obstructions not affecting safety; partial lighting failures not directly affecting operations; partial comms failure when alternatives work; lack of apron marshalling or road traffic control; unserviceable location/direction signs; parachuting in uncontrolled airspace under VFR, or controlled at promulgated sites or in danger/prohibited areas.
- **At least 7 days' notice** of activating established danger, restricted or prohibited areas and temporary airspace restrictions (except emergencies); **24 hours** desirable at least.
- Unserviceability NOTAMs give the **expected period** or restoration time.
- **SNOWTAM**: special-series NOTAM on snow, ice, slush, frost, standing water on the movement area. **RWYCC** (runway condition code) assessed by the **aerodrome operator** for **each third** of the runway, for performance calculations. **ASHTAM**: volcanic activity/ash in its set format.
- Numbering: **series letter + 4-digit number / 2-digit year** (e.g. A0123/26), consecutive per calendar year. **One subject and one condition** per NOTAM.
- **Checklist of valid NOTAM** over the AFS at intervals of **not more than one month** (one per series), referring to the latest AIP AMDT, SUP and AIC. A **monthly printed plain-language list** goes to Integrated Package recipients. The originator chooses international distribution; selective lists where practicable.

### AIRAC (p.237–238)
- Changes on **common effective dates every 28 days** (reference date **29 January 1998**); not changed again for at least **28 days** after the effective date (unless temporary).
- **Part 1** items (must use AIRAC): limits, rules and procedures of **FIRs, CTAs, CTRs, advisory areas, ATS routes, permanent P/R/D areas and ADIZ, permanent interception areas**; positions, frequencies, call signs of nav-aids and comms; **holding, approach, arrival, departure, noise abatement** procedures; met facilities; **runways and stopways**.
- **Part 2** items: obstacles; **taxiways and aprons**; hours of service; customs, immigration, health; temporary danger/prohibited/restricted areas, navigational hazards, military exercises; temporary interception areas.
- Paper copies sent **at least 42 days before** the effective date to reach users **28 days before**; for **major changes**, **56 days** before where practicable.

### AIC (p.238–239)
- For information that qualifies **neither for the AIP nor for a NOTAM**: **long-term forecasts** of major changes in legislation, regulations, procedures or facilities; purely **explanatory or advisory** information affecting safety; explanatory/advisory technical, legislative or administrative matters.
- **Printed**; text and diagrams; serial number consecutive per **calendar year**; series identified by letter if several; **checklist at least once a year**; international distribution as for the AIP.

### Charts, pre- and post-flight (p.239–240)
- Charts for international aerodromes form part of the AIP or are supplied separately; electronic charts from digital databases/GIS.
- At international aerodromes, information for route stages from there is available to crews: Integrated Package elements, maps and charts (may be limited to national + adjacent States if a central library is reachable). Current NOTAM and urgent information as plain-language **PIBs**.
- NOTAM offices connected to the **AFS** (printed communications) and through it to **ACCs/FICs** and aerodromes with an information service.
- **Digital data sets**: AIP, **terrain**, **obstacle**, **aerodrome mapping**, **instrument flight procedure** data sets. **Aeronautical information products**: AIP, AIC, NOTAM, charts, digital data sets.

### India (p.240–245)
- AIS is part of **AAI**: **AIS HQ at Safdarjung Airport, New Delhi**; **four International NOTAM Offices (NOF): Mumbai, Kolkata, Delhi, Chennai**; AIS units at aerodromes. Responsible for all Indian territory and the high-seas airspace India controls.
- **Integrated Aeronautical Information Package**: AIP, AIP AMDT, AIP SUP, NOTAM and PIB, AIC, checklists and summaries. NOTAMs and monthly checklists via **AFS**; PIBs at the **ATS reporting office**; the rest **by mail**.
- **AIP amendments** by replacement sheets **once a year in April**. **Checklist of AIP Supplements** in force issued every **January**.
- AIP India: loose-leaf, in English, **two volumes**: Vol I = **GEN** + **ENR**, Vol II = **AD**.
- **GEN**: national regulations and requirements (authorities, entry/transit/departure of aircraft, people, cargo; instruments, equipment, documents; differences from ICAO), tables and codes (units, time, geodetic reference, marks, holidays, abbreviations, chart symbols, location indicators, nav-aid list, conversion and sunrise/sunset tables), services (AIS, charts, ATS, comms, met, SAR), **charges**.
- **ENR**: general rules (VFR, IFR, airspace classes, procedures, surveillance, altimeter setting, SUPPS, ATFM, flight plans, interception, unlawful interference, incidents); FIR/CTA/TMA; ATS, RNAV and helicopter routes, en-route holding; nav-aids and GNSS; **navigation warnings** (P/R/D areas, military areas, obstacles, aerial sports); **en-route charts**.
- **AD**: aerodrome availability, rescue and fire-fighting, lists, grouping, licensing status, then each aerodrome.
- **NOTAM series** (from the four NOFs): **A** — international locations, changes lasting **more than 2 hours**, general international distribution; **B** — international locations, **30 minutes to 2 hours**, limited distribution to **adjacent stations**; **C** — **domestic-only** locations; **D** — **military airfields** used by domestic flights; **G** — **general and lasting** information, **Delhi NOF only**. Numbering restarts at **0001 at 0000 UTC on 1 January**.
- **Checklist** of NOTAM current on the **1st of each month** over AFTN, plus a monthly summary; full summary every **1 January**.
- **PIB** available at **Kolkata, Chennai, Delhi, Mumbai**.
- **Post-flight information**: report malfunctioning visual/non-visual aids or other facilities, and **bird/wildlife hazards**, without delay.
- **ASBS** (Automatic Self Briefing System) stores and retrieves pre-flight briefing data.
- Briefing away from the aerodrome covers: crew fitness, met, fuel and alternates, performance and mass & balance, route and navigation (MSA, aids, frequencies), NOTAMs, duty time and documents, customs, emergencies and contingencies.

### Exam traps
- AIS by **AAI**; AIC by **DGCA**.
- Permanent change → **AIP AMDT**; temporary **≥ 3 months** or bulky → **AIP SUP (yellow)**; short temporary → **NOTAM**; neither → **AIC**.
- AIRAC cycle **28 days**; distribute **42 days** ahead to arrive **28 days** ahead; major changes **56 days**.
- **7 days'** notice to activate P/R/D areas.
- NOTAM checklist **monthly**; AIC checklist **yearly**; SUP checklist **monthly** (India: AIP SUP checklist each **January**).
- India NOTAM series: A > 2 h international; B 30 min–2 h; C domestic; D military; G general (Delhi only).

## 11. Search and Rescue

### Organisation (Annex 12) (p.253)
- States provide **SAR services within their territories**, individually or together, **24 hours a day**. SAR over the high seas or areas of undetermined sovereignty is assigned by **regional air navigation agreement**.
- **Search and rescue regions (SRRs)** must **not overlap** and neighbouring regions must be **contiguous**. Each SRR has a **Rescue Coordination Centre (RCC)**, with **rescue sub-centres (RSC)** as needed.

### India (p.253–254)
- Over **land**: the **National Aeronautical SAR Coordination Committee (NASARCC)**, chaired by the **Secretary, Ministry of Civil Aviation**. Over **oceanic areas**: the **National Maritime SAR Board (NMSARB)**, chaired by the **Director General, Indian Coast Guard**.
- India's aeronautical **SRR coincides with its FIR boundary**, **excluding the part of the FIR over Bhutan**; includes territorial waters and delegated high-seas areas.
- **AAI** coordinates aeronautical SAR through **four RCCs: Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai**. The **Indian Coast Guard** runs **three MRCCs: Mumbai, Chennai, Port Blair** for the oceanic parts of the Kolkata, Mumbai and Chennai FIRs.
- Other agencies help when needed: railways, P&T, All India Radio, police, district collectors, local bodies, airlines, flying clubs, professional pilots, merchant marine, port trusts, armed forces.
- India has a SAR arrangement with **Bhutan**; it can seek help from neighbouring RCCs under bilateral agreements. Foreign SAR units wanting to enter India send a request with full mission details.

### SAR equipment (p.254–255)
- SAR units carry equipment to locate and help at the scene. SAR aircraft can talk on the **distress and on-scene frequencies**, have a **homing device** for distress frequencies, and over the sea can talk to **vessels** (many ships use **2182 kHz, 4125 kHz, 121.5 MHz**, though 121.5 is not routinely monitored by ships) and carry the **International Code of Signals**.
- Dropped survival containers with **food and water** are marked by **blue streamers**.

### Pilot at the scene (p.255)
- On seeing another aircraft or a surface craft in distress, the PIC should if possible: **keep it in sight** until compelled to leave or told by the RCC it isn't needed; **fix its position**; **report** as much as possible to the RCC or ATS unit.
- If the **first aircraft on scene is not a SAR aircraft, it takes charge** of all later aircraft until the first SAR aircraft arrives.

### Pilot intercepting a distress call (p.255–256)
- **Acknowledge** it; **record the position**; **take a bearing**; **inform the RCC/ATS** with all information; at the pilot's discretion **proceed to the position** while awaiting instructions.

### Signals with surface craft (p.256)
- To **direct a ship** to a craft in distress, in sequence: **circle the ship at least once**; **cross its projected course close ahead at low altitude** while **rocking wings** (or opening/closing the throttle, or changing propeller pitch); **head in the direction** to be taken. Repeating means the same.
- **Assistance no longer required**: **cross the ship's wake close astern** at low altitude, rocking wings (or throttle/pitch).
- Ship replies: **understood** — hoist the **code pennant** (vertical red and white stripes) close up, flash **T's** in Morse, or turn to follow. **Unable to comply** — hoist **flag N** (blue and white chequered), or flash **N's**.

### Ground–air visual codes (p.257)
- **Survivors**: **V** require assistance; **X** require medical assistance; **N** no/negative; **Y** yes/affirmative; **↑ (arrow)** proceeding in this direction.
- **Rescue units**: **LLL** operation completed; **LL** found all personnel; **++** found only some personnel; **XX** not able to continue, returning to base; **two arrows** divided into two groups, each going the way shown; **arrow** information received that the aircraft is in this direction; **NN** nothing found, will continue to search.
- Symbols at least **2.5 m (8 ft)** long, as conspicuous as possible (fabric, parachute, wood, stones, tramping, oil stains); attract attention with radio, flares, smoke, reflected light.
- **Aircraft acknowledging a ground signal**: by day **rock the wings**; by night **flash landing lights twice** (or nav lights). **No signal = not understood.**

### Satellite-aided SAR (p.258)
- India participates in **COSPAS-SARSAT**, using **406 MHz** beacons; location accuracy normally **within 5 km**. Coverage includes India's SRR and those of Bangladesh, Myanmar, Bhutan, Indonesia, Kenya, Malaysia, Maldives, Mauritius, Nepal, Seychelles, Singapore, Somalia, Sri Lanka, Thailand and Tanzania.
- Two **Local User Terminals (LUTs)** at **Bengaluru and Lucknow**; the **Indian Mission Control Centre (INMCC)** at **Bengaluru**, linked to the four RCCs over the **AFS**; alerts go to the RCC automatically.

### Exam traps
- RCC and RSC organise SAR; SRRs **do not overlap**.
- India: AAI's **4 RCCs** (Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai); Coast Guard's **3 MRCCs** (Mumbai, Chennai, **Port Blair**). Land SAR chaired by **Secretary MoCA**; maritime by **DG Coast Guard**.
- First non-SAR aircraft on scene **takes charge** until a SAR aircraft arrives.
- Direct a ship: **circle, cross ahead rocking wings, head off**. Not needed: **cross astern**.
- **V** assistance, **X** medical, **N** no, **Y** yes; **LLL** completed, **XX** returning.
- Blue streamers = **food and water**. Symbols ≥ **2.5 m**. COSPAS-SARSAT **406 MHz**, **5 km**, INMCC **Bengaluru**.

## 12. Visual Aids for Navigation

### Indicators and signalling devices (p.263)
- At least **one wind direction indicator**, visible from the air and the movement area, clear of disturbed air; at least one **lit** at night aerodromes.
- **Landing direction indicator** (where provided): conspicuous place; in the form of a **T**.
- **Signalling lamp** at the **control tower** of a controlled aerodrome: **red, green and white** signals.
- **Signal area**: visible from all azimuths above **10°** from **300 m** height; an even horizontal surface at least **9 m square**, contrasting colour, **white border ≥ 0.3 m**.

### Markings: general (p.263–264)
- At runway intersections the **more important runway's** markings continue (order: **precision > non-precision > non-instrument**); the other's are interrupted (side stripes may continue or not). At a runway/taxiway intersection the **runway** markings continue.
- **Runway markings white**; **taxiway, turn pad and stand markings yellow**; **apron safety lines red in India** (contrasting with stand markings). Reflective materials at night aerodromes.

### Runway markings (p.264–268)
- **Designation**: at each paved threshold; **two-digit number** = whole number nearest **one-tenth of the magnetic bearing** from the approach direction (single digit gets a leading zero). Parallel runways add letters left to right: 2 → **L R**; 3 → **L C R**; 4 → L R L R; 5 → L C R L R or L R L C R; 6 → L C R L C R. With 4 or more, one set of adjacent runways uses the nearest tenth, the other the next nearest.
- **Centre line**: on paved runways between designation markings; stripe + gap **50–75 m**; stripe at least the gap or **30 m**, whichever greater. Width: **0.90 m** Cat II/III; **0.45 m** non-precision code 3–4 and Cat I; **0.30 m** non-precision code 1–2 and non-instrument.
- **Threshold**: on paved instrument runways and paved non-instrument code 3–4 runways for international commercial air transport; stripes start **6 m** from the threshold. Stripes by runway width: **18 m → 4, 23 m → 6, 30 m → 8, 45 m → 12, 60 m → 16**.
- **Transverse stripe** (≥ **1.80 m** wide) where the threshold is displaced or the end isn't square. **Permanently displaced** threshold: **arrows** on the runway before it. **Temporarily displaced**: markings before it obscured except the centre line, which becomes arrows.
- **Aiming point**: at each approach end of paved instrument runways code **2, 3, 4**; two conspicuous stripes. Starts at **150 m** (LDA < 800 m), **250 m** (800–1,200), **300 m** (1,200–2,400), **400 m** (≥ 2,400) from the threshold, or at the PAPI origin if a PAPI exists.
- **Touchdown zone markings**: on paved precision runways code 2–4; pairs by LDA: **< 900 m → 1; 900–1,200 → 2; 1,200–1,500 → 3; 1,500–2,400 → 4; ≥ 2,400 → 6**.
- **Side stripes**: where edges lack contrast (recommended on all precision runways); at the edges, or **30 m from the centre line** on runways wider than **60 m**; width ≥ **0.9 m** (runways ≥ 30 m wide) or **0.45 m**.

### Taxiway and apron markings (p.268–272)
- **Taxiway centre line** on paved taxiways, de-icing facilities and aprons code **3–4** (recommended 1–2), continuous guidance from runway centre line to stand; at least **15 cm** wide, continuous except at holding position markings. On a runway that's part of a taxi route if there's no runway centre line or it doesn't coincide.
- **Enhanced taxiway centre line**: dashes warning of a runway-holding position, from pattern A up to **47 m** away from the runway; part of runway incursion prevention.
- **Isolated aircraft parking position** (for unlawful interference etc.): as far as practicable and **never less than 100 m** from other stands, buildings or public areas; not over gas/fuel lines.
- **Runway-holding position marking**: **pattern A** (4 lines: 2 solid + 2 dashed) at non-instrument/non-precision/take-off runways and the one closest to a precision runway; **pattern B** (ladder) for further Cat II/III holding positions. From **26 Nov 2026** only the larger patterns **A2/B2** are valid.
- **Intermediate holding position marking** (recommended); at the exit of a remote de-icing facility.
- **VOR checkpoint marking**: circle **6 m** diameter, line **15 cm**; optional direction line extending 6 m with an arrowhead; preferably **white**.
- **Aircraft stand markings**: identification, lead-in, turn bar, turning line, alignment bar, stop line, lead-out (lines ≥ 15 cm). **Apron safety lines** ≥ **10 cm**, continuous, define vehicle areas and wingtip clearance.
- **Road-holding position marking** at all road entrances to a runway (local road rules).
- **Mandatory instruction marking** (where a sign can't be installed, or on taxiways > 60 m wide): **white on red**, on the **left** of the centre line on the holding side, ≥ 1 m from the lines; characters **4 m** high; NO ENTRY marking = white "NO ENTRY" on red.
- **Information marking**: **yellow** inscription (black background if needed) when replacing a **location** sign; **black** inscription (yellow background) when replacing a **direction or destination** sign.

### Lights: general (p.272–273)
- Protected zones against **lasers**: **LFFZ** (laser-beam free), **LCFZ** (critical), **LSFZ** (sensitive).
- Non-aeronautical lights that endanger or confuse must be extinguished, screened or modified, especially within **4,500 m** before the threshold/after the end and **750 m** either side (code 4 instrument runway); **3,000 m** for code 2–3; within the approach area for code 1 / non-instrument.
- **Emergency lighting** for at least the primary runway where there is no secondary power supply (non-instrument configuration as minimum).
- **Aerodrome beacon** at night aerodromes where navigation is mainly visual, low visibility is frequent, or the aerodrome is hard to find: **20–30 flashes per minute**; coloured flashes **green (land)** or **yellow (water)**, alternating with white, or white only. **Identification beacon**: flashing **green** (land) / **yellow** (water), **Morse** identity.

### Approach lighting (p.273–276)
- **Simple approach lighting** (non-instrument code 3–4 at night, recommended; **non-precision** runways, required where practicable): centre line row at least **420 m**, **crossbar 18 m or 30 m at 300 m**, lights every **60 m** (or 30 m), fixed lights; each centre line light a single source or a **barrette ≥ 3 m**.
- **Cat I**: centre line **900 m**, **crossbar 30 m at 300 m**, lights every **30 m** (innermost 30 m from threshold), fixed **variable white**; distance coding **1, 2, 3 sources** in the inner, middle, outer 300 m, or barrettes. Less than 900 m may restrict operations.
- **Cat II/III**: centre line **900 m** plus **two side rows to 270 m** (**red** barrettes) and **two crossbars at 150 m and 300 m**; inner 300 m centre line of **white barrettes**.
- Nothing except an ILS/MLS azimuth antenna may protrude through the light plane within **60 m** of the system's centre line (and then it's an obstacle). Barrette centre lines may have **sequenced flashing lights**, **twice a second**, running **towards the threshold**.

### Visual approach slope indicators (p.276–277)
- Required where: turbojets use the runway; judging the approach is hard (water, featureless terrain, no lights at night, deceptive terrain or slopes); objects in the approach area; hazardous undershoot/overrun; unusual turbulence.
- Standard systems: **PAPI** (wing bar of **four** units) and **APAPI** (**two** units), on the **left** side of the runway (second bar opposite for roll guidance if needed); day and night.
- **PAPI**: on slope **2 red + 2 white** (red nearest the runway); slightly high **1 red + 3 white**; too high **4 white**; slightly low **3 red + 1 white**; too low **4 red**. **APAPI**: on slope **1 red + 1 white**; high **2 white**; low **2 red**.

### Runway lights (p.277–283)
- **Circling guidance lights** where approach/runway lights don't identify the runway for circling: flashing **white**, steady white or gaseous discharge.
- **Runway lead-in lighting**: groups of at least **three flashing white** lights, flashing in sequence **towards the runway**, to guide along a path (terrain, noise).
- **Threshold identification lights**: flashing **white**, **60–120 per minute**, ~**10 m** outside each edge-light row, visible only on approach; for non-precision runways or displaced thresholds needing conspicuity.
- **Runway edge lights**: for night use and precision runways day or night (recommended for take-off below ~**800 m RVR** by day); full length, two rows, at or within **3 m** outside the edges; spacing **≤ 60 m instrument**, **≤ 100 m non-instrument**; **variable white**, except **red** towards the approach before a displaced threshold, and **yellow** on the last **600 m or one-third** of the runway (whichever less) at the far end.
- **Threshold lights** (where edge lights exist): row at right angles, no more than **3 m outside** the end; at least **6** lights (non-instrument/NPA); Cat I as if spaced every **3 m**; Cat II/III every **3 m** max. **Wing bars**: at least **5 lights** extending **10 m** outward. Both **fixed green** towards the approach.
- **Runway end lights**: at least **6**, ≤ **3 m** outside the end, **fixed red** towards the runway (Cat III spacing ≤ 6 m).
- **Runway centre line lights**: required on **Cat II/III** and for take-off below **400 m RVR**; recommended on Cat I (high speeds or edge lights > 50 m apart). Spacing about **15 m** (old 7.5 m kept), offset ≤ 60 cm. Colour: **white** to **900 m** from the end; **alternate red and white 900–300 m**; **red last 300 m** (runways < 1,800 m: alternate from the midpoint).
- **Touchdown zone lights**: Cat II/III; **900 m** from the threshold (not beyond the midpoint on runways < 1,800 m); pairs of barrettes (≥ 3 lights, ≤ 1.5 m apart, 3–4.5 m long) every **30 m or 60 m**; variable white. **Simple TDZ lights**: one pair each side, to help the go-around decision.
- **Stopway lights**: for night use; along both edges and across the end (≤ 3 m beyond); **fixed red** towards the runway.

### Taxiway and apron lights (p.283–286)
- **Taxiway centre line lights**: required for **RVR < 350 m** (except light traffic with edge lights and markings), recommended at night above that; **green**. On **exit taxiways**: **alternate green and yellow** from the runway centre line to the edge of the **ILS critical/sensitive area** (or inner transitional surface), then all green; the last light at the boundary is **yellow**. Spacing **≤ 30 m** (up to 60 m in good weather; **≤ 15 m** for RVR < 350 m); offset ≤ 30 cm.
- **Taxiway edge lights**: **blue**, fixed, visible up to **30°** above the horizontal; ≤ **60 m** apart (closer on curves), ≤ **3 m** outside the edge.
- **Rapid exit taxiway indicator lights (RETILs)**: yellow, show the distance to the nearest rapid exit (300, 200, 100 m patterns).
- **Stand manoeuvring guidance lights**: **fixed yellow**; stop position **fixed red**; spacing ≤ **7.5 m** on curves, **15 m** straight.
- **Runway turn pad lights**: guide a 180° turn back onto the centre line.
- **Stop bars**: at every runway-holding position for use in **RVR < 350 m**; controlled by ATS (also useful at night and above 550 m to prevent incursions). **Intermediate holding position lights** where no stop bar, RVR < 350 m.
- **ARIWS / runway status lights (RWSL)**: automatic incursion warning; **runway entrance lights (RELs)** turn **red** when unsafe to enter/cross; **take-off hold lights (THLs)** red when unsafe to start the roll.
- **Runway guard lights (Configuration A)**, if no stop bars: at every taxiway/runway intersection for **RVR < 550 m**, or **550–1,200 m** with heavy traffic. They warn that you are about to enter a runway.

### Signs (p.286–289)
- Signs are **frangible** and low enough to clear propellers and engine pods. **Only mandatory instruction signs use red.** Retro-reflective or lit at night (code 1–2 non-instrument). **Variable message signs** where information changes.
- **Mandatory instruction signs**: **white on red**: **runway designation**, **Cat I/II/III holding position**, **runway-holding position**, **road-holding position**, **NO ENTRY**. A place beyond which you **must not go without tower clearance**. Black outline 10 mm (code 1–2) or 20 mm (code 3–4) if needed.
- **Information signs**: **black on yellow** — direction, destination, runway exit, **runway vacated**, **intersection take-off** (shows remaining TORA), except the **location sign**: **yellow on black** with a yellow border if stand-alone ("**black square, you are there**").
- Runway vacated sign: where the exit has no centre line lights, marks the edge of the ILS critical/sensitive area or inner transitional surface. Location sign with each runway designation sign (except runway/runway) and with direction signs.
- **Numbers alone** are reserved for **runways**. Taxiways: a letter, two letters, or letter(s) + number, used once; avoid "inner/outer"; **no I, O or X** (confused with 1, 0 and the closed marking). Stand designators must differ from taxiway designators.
- **VOR checkpoint sign** and **stand identification sign**: black on yellow. **Aerodrome identification sign**: the aerodrome's name, characters ≥ **3 m** high. **Road-holding position sign**: **white on red**, **1.5 m** from the road edge.

### Markers (p.289–290)
- Frangible and low. Unpaved runway/taxiway edge markers, stopway edge markers (distinct from runway ones), snow-covered runway markers, taxiway edge markers (code 1–2 without lights).
- **Boundary markers** where there's no runway: **orange or red**, or **orange and white / red and white**.
- Obstacle markers visible from **1,000 m** in the air and **300 m** on the ground.

### Obstacles (p.290–293)
- Mark (and light for night use): fixed obstacles above a take-off climb surface within **3,000 m** of its inner edge; objects adjacent to it; obstacles above the horizontal surface or obstacle protection surface; **vehicles and mobile objects** on the movement area (apron-only service vehicles may be exempt); obstacles near taxiways. High-intensity lights by day may replace marking.
- **Chequered pattern** if unbroken surfaces ≥ **4.5 m** in both dimensions: rectangles **1.5–3 m** a side, darker colour at the corners, **orange/white or red/white**. **Single colour** (orange or red) if both dimensions < **1.5 m**. Mobile objects: **red** for emergency vehicles, **yellow** for service vehicles.
- **Low-intensity** obstacle lights: Type A and B **fixed red** (≤ **45 m** apart), Type E flashing red; on vehicles: **flashing blue** for emergency/security, **flashing yellow** for others and **follow-me** vehicles. **Medium-intensity**: Type A **flashing white**, Type B **flashing red**, Type C **fixed red**, ≤ **900 m** apart. **High-intensity**: **flashing white**. **Wind turbines**: medium-intensity lights.

### Restricted-use areas (p.293–294)
- **Closed marking** on permanently closed runways/taxiways (normal markings obliterated, lights off except maintenance); on temporary closures unless short and ATS warns.
- Paved **pre-threshold area > 60 m** not fit for normal use: **yellow chevrons** pointing to the runway, **0.9 m** wide.
- **Unserviceability markers** (flags, cones, boards) where a taxiway/apron/holding bay part is unfit but can be bypassed; at night **fixed red** unserviceability lights.

### Rescue and fire-fighting (p.294–296)
- Purpose: **save lives** in accidents at or near the aerodrome.
- **Aerodrome RFF category** by the **longest aeroplane** normally using it (and fuselage width): **1** < 9 m · **2** 9–12 m · **3** 12–18 m · **4** 18–24 m · **5** 24–28 m · **6** 28–39 m · **7** 39–49 m · **8** 49–61 m · **9** 61–76 m · **10** 76–90 m. If the highest-category aeroplanes make **fewer than 700 movements in the busiest 3 consecutive months**, protection may be **one category lower**. (A movement = a take-off or a landing.)
- **Principal agent: foam**; **complementary: dry chemical powder** (hydrocarbon fires), to BIS/ISO specs.
- **Response time**: **≤ 3 minutes** to any point of each operational runway and the movement area; objective **≤ 2 minutes** to any runway point in optimum conditions.
- Emergency access roads, especially to approach areas up to **1,000 m** from the threshold. Satellite fire stations if the response time can't be met. Discrete communication between fire station, tower and vehicles; alerting system at the fire stations and tower.
- **Radio altimeter operating area** in the pre-threshold area of Cat II/III runways.

### Declared distances, clearway, stopway (p.296)
- TORA, TODA (+ clearway), ASDA (+ stopway), LDA as in Chapter 1. **Clearway**: at least **75 m** each side of the extended centre line; ground not above a **1.25%** upward slope. **Stopway**: **same width as the runway**, able to support the aeroplane without structural damage.

### Wildlife and FOD (p.296–297)
- **Wildlife hazard management programme (WHMP)**: assessment, reporting and control of birds and animals.
- **FOD** control programme: **prevention, detection, removal, evaluation**.

### Exam traps
- Runway markings **white**; taxiway **yellow**; apron safety lines **red (India)**.
- Threshold stripes: **18→4, 23→6, 30→8, 45→12, 60→16**; start **6 m** in.
- Aiming point **150/250/300/400 m**; TDZ pairs **1/2/3/4/6**.
- Simple ALS **420 m**, crossbar at **300 m**; Cat I **900 m**, 30 m crossbar at 300 m; Cat II/III side rows **270 m** red, crossbars **150 m and 300 m**.
- PAPI **2 red 2 white = on slope**; **4 units**, left side; APAPI **2 units**.
- Edge lights **white**, **yellow** last 600 m / one-third; threshold **green**; end **red**; centre line **white → red/white at 900 m → red last 300 m**; taxiway centre **green**, edge **blue**; exit taxiway **green/yellow**.
- Stop bars for **RVR < 350 m**; runway guard lights **< 550 m** (or 550–1,200 m heavy traffic).
- Mandatory signs **white on red**; location sign **yellow on black**; other information signs **black on yellow**. No **I, O, X** for taxiways.
- Beacon **20–30 flashes/min**, green (land) / yellow (water).
- RFF response **3 min** (objective 2); foam principal; **700 movements** rule.

## 13. PANS-OPS: Aircraft Operations

### Doc 8168 (p.325)
- **Volume I – Flight Procedures**: for pilots and operations staff; explains the parameters behind the design criteria so crews fly procedures exactly.
- **Volume II – Construction of Visual and Instrument Flight Procedures**: for procedure designers; protected areas and obstacle clearance.

### General departure criteria (p.325–327)
- PANS-OPS procedures assume **all engines operating**; engine-out contingency procedures are the **operator's** responsibility.
- Procedures depict **tracks**: pilots correct heading for known wind (but **not** when radar vectored). Worked examples use **2,000 ft AMSL and ISA +15 °C**.
- **Protected areas**: primary area = **half** the total width (full MOC); each secondary area = **one-quarter** (MOC tapering to zero at the outer edge). With no track guidance in a turn, the whole width is primary.
- A **straight departure** (aligned with the runway centre line) is used wherever possible; a turn of **more than 15°** makes it a **turning departure**. If obstacles can't be cleared on instruments, aerodrome operating minima allow visual avoidance.
- **MOC on departure** is **zero at the DER** and grows by **0.8%** of horizontal distance; **90 m (295 ft)** in turn areas; more in mountains.
- **Procedure design gradient (PDG)**: **3.3%** unless published (helicopters **5%**) = obstacle identification surface (**OIS**) **2.5%** + **0.8%** margin. Higher gradients are published with the altitude to which they apply; gradients needed only to **200 ft (60 m)** or less (close-in obstacles) are not published, just noted. The departure ends at a significant point when clearance for the next phase is assured.
- DME or RNAV fixes may be published to monitor the climb.
- **Radar vectors on departure**: accept only if **above the minimum altitude** that keeps obstacle clearance after an **engine failure** (V1 to MSA / end of contingency procedure), or if the route is **not critical** for obstacles.
- **SID ends** at the first fix/facility/waypoint of the en-route phase.
- **Straight departure**: initial track within **15°** of the runway centre line.
- **Turning departure**: straight flight until at least **120 m (394 ft)** (helicopters **90 m / 295 ft**).
- **Omnidirectional departures** (no track guidance): begin at the **DER** (end of runway or clearway); assume no turn at 394 ft before **600 m** from the start of the runway; climb **3.3%** straight to **394 ft**, with **295 ft** of clearance before turns over 15°. Variants: standard case (turn any direction at 394 ft), specified turn altitude, specified PDG, sector departures. If impossible, fly a SID or ensure visual avoidance.

### RNAV / GNSS (p.330–331)
- RNAV computers can't detect **data input errors**.
- **GNSS** = constellations + receivers + integrity monitoring, augmented as needed. **GBAS** (ground transmitter), **GLS** (GBAS landing system), **SBAS** (geostationary satellites relaying corrections from ground stations; full-scale deflection **0.3 NM** on departure), **ABAS** (on-board).
- **PBN**: area navigation to performance requirements; protection based on a ground track defined by waypoints, path terminators etc.
- Departure turns: **fly-by**, **flyover**, **at an altitude/height** (may need to be flown manually), **fixed radius** (RNP).

### Fixes (p.331–332)
- A fix lies anywhere within its **fix tolerance area**. Radar: **TAR within 20 NM ± 0.8 NM**; **RSR within 40 NM ± 1.7 NM**. **DME: ± (0.25 NM + 1.25% of distance)**.

### Approach types and aircraft categories (p.332–333)
- **Straight-in** wherever possible; for an NPA acceptable if the final track is within **30°** of the runway centre line. Otherwise **circling**.
- Categories by **Vat** = **1.3 × Vso** or **1.23 × Vs1g** (higher) at max landing mass: **A < 91 kt**; **B 91–120**; **C 121–140**; **D 141–165**; **E 166–210**; **H** helicopters.
- Max circling speeds: **A 100, B 135, C 180, D 205, E 240 kt**. Max final missed approach: A 110, B 150, C 240, D 265, E 275 kt. Initial approach ranges A 90–150 (110 for reversal/racetrack), B 120–180 (140).

### Descent gradient (p.333)
- Final approach with FAF: **optimum 5.2% / 3.0° (318 ft/NM)**. Maximum **6.5% / 3.7°** (Cat A, B); **6.1% / 3.5°** (Cat C, D, E); **10% / 5.7°** (Cat H).
- **CDFA**: continuous descent (VNAV or manually calculated rate) without level-offs to about **50 ft** above threshold, passing at or above each step-down minimum.

### Approach segments (p.333–338)
- **Arrival** (STAR): from en route to the IAF; protection narrows at up to **30°** each side, starting **25 NM** before the IAF.
- **MSA/TAA**: at least **1,000 ft** clearance within **25 NM** of the aid, IAF or IF. **TAAs** (PBN "T"/"Y" with three IAFs around an IF) replace the 25 NM MSA where published.
- **Initial**: **IAF → IF**; interception angle **≤ 90° precision**, **≤ 120° non-precision**; MOC **300 m (1,000 ft)**. If there is no suitable IAF/IF: reversal procedure, racetrack or hold.
- **Reversal procedures**: **45°/180° procedure turn**, **80°/260° procedure turn**, **base turn**; entry restricted to a direction/sector; timing strictly flown (the airspace does not allow a racetrack or hold unless specified).
- **Racetrack**: turn 180° from over the fix onto the outbound track for **1, 2 or 3 minutes**, then 180° turn the same way back inbound (or DME/radial limit).
- **Intermediate**: speed and configuration set up for final, descent as shallow as possible; MOC reduces **300 m → 150 m (1,000 → 500 ft)**; starts on the inbound track of the PT/base turn/racetrack and ends at the **FAF/FAP**.
- **Final**: alignment and descent. Types: NPA with FAF, NPA without FAF, APV, PA. **Visual segment surface (VSS)** protects between MDA/DA and the threshold (not circling).
- **NPA with FAF**: FAF optimum **5 NM** from the threshold, normally **≤ 10 NM**. A **step-down fix** gives two OCA/H values (lower only if the fix is identified). **MOC 75 m (246 ft) with FAF**, **90 m (295 ft) without**.
- **NPA without FAF**: single facility is both **IAF and MAPt**; descend to MDA/H once established inbound; final track usually not aligned with the runway.
- **Precision**: final starts at the **FAP**, where the intermediate altitude meets the glide path, typically **1,000–3,000 ft** above the runway (**3–10 NM** for 3°). The **outer marker / DME fix** checks the glide-path/altimeter relationship; don't descend below its crossing altitude before crossing it. **FAF = NPA; FAP = precision**.
- **OCA/H** = highest obstacle + MOC (+ margin); **MDA/H** or **DA/H** adds an operational margin.
- **Missed approach**: one per procedure; start **not lower than DA/H** (PA/APV) or at the **MAPt** not lower than MDA/H (NPA). MAPt = glide path meets DA/H, or a facility/fix/distance from the FAF. Normal gradient **2.5%**. Phases: **initial** (MAPt to start of climb, **no turns**), **intermediate** (to where **50 m / 164 ft** clearance is obtained, track change ≤ **15°**; Cat H 40 m), **final** (turns allowed; to a new approach, hold or en route).

### Classification (p.338–341)
- **Type A** = minimum **≥ 250 ft (75 m)**; **Type B** = DH **< 250 ft**: **Cat I** DH ≥ 200 ft and visibility ≥ 800 m or RVR ≥ 550 m; **Cat II** DH 100–200 ft, RVR ≥ 300 m; **Cat III** DH < 100 ft or none, RVR < 300 m or none. A 3D approach with DH ≥ 250 ft is Type A. Cat II/III need **RVR reporting**. **2D** = lateral only; **3D** = lateral + vertical; 4D adds timing.
- **NPA**: conventional (VOR, NDB, LOC) or PBN **RNP APCH** (RNAV(GNSS), LNAV); CDFA with on-board VNAV counts as 3D. Non-precision runway visibility ≥ **1,000 m**.
- **APV**: PBN 3D Type A: Baro-VNAV, SBAS (LPV), LNAV/VNAV, RNP AR.
- **PA**: ILS, MLS, PAR, **GLS** (GBAS, ILS Cat I equivalent; neither PBN nor conventional), **SBAS Cat I**.
- **OCH/MDH/DH reference**: precision and APV → **threshold** elevation; NPA → **aerodrome** elevation (threshold if > **7 ft** lower); **circling → aerodrome elevation** always.

### Circling (p.342–344)
- Visual manoeuvring after an instrument approach to a runway not suited to straight-in. **Area**: arcs centred on each threshold joined by tangents; radius from category speed, **25 kt wind**, bank **20° average or 3°/s** (lesser).
- OCA/H for each category; a sector with a prominent obstacle may be excluded if outside the final and missed approach areas.
- Don't descend below **MDA/H** until visual reference is established and maintained, the **threshold is in sight**, and obstacle clearance can be kept in a position to land.
- Lose visual reference while circling: **climbing turn towards the landing runway**, overhead the aerodrome, then the missed approach track.

### Holding (p.344–347)
- Turns at **25° bank or 3°/s**, whichever needs less bank. Correct both **heading and timing** for known wind.
- Outbound timing starts **over or abeam the fix, whichever is later** (or when the outbound turn is complete). DME-limited leg ends at the DME distance; a **limiting radial** reached first is followed until the turn.
- **Entry sectors** (±5° flexibility): **Sector 1 parallel** — fly outbound parallel to the inbound track, turn left (for a right-hand hold) onto the holding side to intercept or return to the fix; **Sector 2 offset (teardrop)** — outbound on a track **30°** from the reciprocal of the inbound on the holding side, then turn right to intercept inbound; **Sector 3 direct** — turn right into the pattern. VOR intersection entries use only the radials; DME arc entry by sector 1 or 3.
- Outbound entry time: **1 minute** at or below **14,000 ft**; **1½ minutes** above.
- **Holding speeds (Cat A–E)**: up to **14,000 ft: 230 kt** (170 kt for Cat A/B-only holds); **14,000–20,000 ft: 240 kt**; **20,000–34,000 ft: 265 kt**; above **34,000 ft: M 0.83**. Turbulence: **280 kt or M 0.8** (less) up to 34,000 ft, only with ATC clearance.
- **Minimum holding level**: **300 m (984 ft)** above obstacles (up to **600 m** over mountains), rounded up to 50 m/100 ft.

### Parallel runways (p.347)
- **Mode 1** independent parallel approaches (no radar separation between adjacent ILS) — centre lines ≥ **1,035 m**. **Mode 2** dependent parallel approaches (radar separation prescribed) — ≥ **915 m**. **Mode 3** independent parallel departures — ≥ **760 m**. **Mode 4** segregated (one for arrivals, one for departures) — ≥ **760 m**, adjusted **30 m per 150 m** of stagger, absolute minimum **300 m**.

### RNAV approaches (VOR/DME) (p.348)
- Non-precision. STAR flight technical tolerance **2 NM** until 25 NM from the IAF, then **1 NM**. Final MOC **75 m (246 ft)**; FAF a **fly-by** waypoint; threshold and **MAPt flyover** waypoints; missed approach area splays **15°**; ends at the **MAHF**.

### Altimeter setting (p.348–350)
- At or below the **transition altitude**: altitudes on **QNH**. Above: **flight levels** on **1013.2 hPa**. Change to FLs at the **TA when climbing**, to QNH at the **TL when descending**. TL may be just above the TA or **1,000 ft** above it; between them the **transition layer**. No TA → fly flight levels en route.
- Terrain clearance, in order of preference: current **QNH** network; available QNH plus forecast lowest MSL pressure; climatological lowest levels. Approach: **QNH** (or **QFE** where specified).
- **FL 0** at **1013.2 hPa**; levels at least **500 ft** apart.
- **Lowest transition altitude in India: 4,000 ft**; TA height above the aerodrome normally **≥ 3,000 ft**, rounded **up** to the next **1,000 ft**.
- **Pre-flight altimeter check** at a known elevation: tolerance **± 60 ft** (test range to 30,000 ft) or **± 80 ft** (to 50,000 ft).

### SSR and transponders (p.350–352)
- **Mode A** = identity; **Mode C** = pressure altitude. Codes are **octal 0000–7777**.
- Transponder **on at all times in flight**; codes as ATC directs, or regional agreement, else **2000**; Mode C on continuously unless told. **CONFIRM SQUAWK**: check, reselect, read back. **No IDENT unless asked.**
- **7700** emergency (unless ATC gave another code — keep it unless there's reason); **7600** radio failure (ATC tests the receiver with IDENT or code changes); **7500** unlawful interference (or 7700 if needed; if asked to confirm 7500, confirm or **don't reply** — silence = confirmed). Transponder fails after departure: ATC tries to let you continue to destination.

### ACAS (p.352–354)
- Use for collision avoidance, awareness and visual search; the PIC keeps full authority.
- **Never manoeuvre on a TA alone**; prepare for an RA. On an **RA**: respond **immediately** (unless unsafe; **stall, wind shear and GPWS warnings take priority**); follow it **even against an ATC instruction**; **never manoeuvre opposite** to it; tell ATC as soon as possible (ATC doesn't know about RAs); follow modified RAs; deviate minimally; return to the clearance when clear and tell ATC.
- Surveillance about **14 NM** (at least **4.5 NM** guaranteed in dense areas). **TAs** against any transponder replying to Mode C (even without altitude); **RAs only against altitude-reporting aircraft, vertical plane only**; ACAS–ACAS RAs are coordinated, so not following one hurts both aircraft.

### Exam traps
- PDG **3.3% = 2.5% OIS + 0.8%**; MOC **0** at DER growing **0.8%**; turns not before **394 ft** and **600 m** from the runway start.
- Straight departure **≤ 15°**; straight-in NPA **≤ 30°**.
- Categories by **Vat**: **91 / 121 / 141 / 166 / 211 kt** boundaries; circling **100/135/180/205/240**.
- Final descent **5.2% (3°) optimum**, max **6.5% A–B**, **6.1% C–E**.
- MOC: initial **1,000 ft**, intermediate **1,000 → 500 ft**, final NPA **246 ft with FAF / 295 ft without**, missed approach **164 ft**, holding **984 ft**.
- Missed approach gradient **2.5%**; no turns in the initial phase; ≤ 15° in intermediate.
- Holding: **230 / 240 / 265 kt / M 0.83**; outbound **1 min ≤ 14,000 ft**, **1.5 min** above; teardrop **30°**.
- Parallel runways **1,035 / 915 / 760 / 760 m**.
- TA in India ≥ **4,000 ft**; altimeter check **± 60 ft**.
- RA beats ATC; TA alone → no manoeuvre; RAs only in the **vertical** plane.

## 14. National Law

### Bharatiya Vayuyan Adhiniyam, 2024 (p.387–389)
- Replaces the Aircraft Act 1934 as India's aviation law. Applies to: all of India; **Indian citizens anywhere**; **Indian-registered aircraft and people on them anywhere**; **foreign aircraft in Indian airspace**; aircraft operated by non-citizens whose main business or home is in India.
- Three bodies under the Central Government: **DGCA** (safety oversight), **BCAS** (security against unlawful acts), **AAIB** (accident investigation).
- **DGCA**: headed by the Director General (appointed by Govt); may issue **binding directions** on safety, ATC, maintenance, aerodromes, navigation, unlawful interference; Govt may delegate DGCA powers.
- **Central Government** makes rules on aircraft, licensing, airports and CNS, tariffs and economic regulation, airspace and no-fly zones, dangerous goods, public health, **land use** near airports, and international agreements. Can make rules against **slaughter, rubbish or pollution within 10 km** of an aerodrome and on **buildings and trees within 20 km**.
- **Enforcement**: detain unsafe/violating aircraft; restrict, suspend or cancel licences. **General penalty**: up to **2 years** prison and/or fine up to **₹1 crore**. **Slaughter or waste within 10 km**: up to **3 years** and/or **₹1 crore**.
- **Protections**: no suit for **trespass or nuisance** from flight at a reasonable height or ordinary noise; no action against officials acting in **good faith**. **Military aircraft exempt** (except public-health sections 13 and 14).

### Aircraft Rules, 1937: key rules (p.389–399)
- **Rule 1** scope: India; Indian aircraft anywhere; aircraft in/over India; foreign aircraft under lease/charter per Chicago **Art 83 bis**. **Rule 2**: nationality = State of registry. **Rule 4**: no one operates an aircraft except per these rules.
- **Rule 5**: no flight unless **registered** and showing **marks plus owner's name and address**. **Rule 5A**: Indian aircraft may not leave India for overseas flight without **DGCA permit** or fly to territories notified as prohibited.
- **Rule 6**: licensed crew (foreign aircraft: licensed by their State). **6A–6C**: fly only types endorsed on the licence, except flights to qualify for a type (in the local/designated flying area of a licensed or government aerodrome) and DGCA-authorised test/non-revenue flights.
- **Rule 7**: carry all valid documents. **7A**: aircraft may not enter India unless **everyone has a valid passport** (else the operator removes them). **7B**: Indian aircraft carry **cockpit and emergency checklists**.
- **Rule 8**: **arms, ammunition, explosives** only with **Central Government written permission**. **8A**: every passenger and hand bag screened. **Rule 9**: radio only by licensed persons. **Rule 10**: mail only with the **Director General of Posts'** written approval. **Rule 11**: public transport/scheduled aircraft use **licensed or approved aerodromes**.
- **Rule 12 – prohibited areas** (Schedule I): if a pilot strays in: **signal distress, leave, land at the nearest aerodrome**; don't go deeper or start descending over it. Examples: **1 mile** around the **Towers of Silence, Malabar Hill, Mumbai**; **Rashtrapati Bhavan** (ground to unlimited); near **Baroda** aerodrome (unlimited); **10 km** around **Mathura** refinery (unlimited); near **Bhubaneswar** (to **50,000 ft**); **10 km** around **Kalpakkam** (to **10,000 ft**).
- **Rule 13**: **no photography** at a DGCA-licensed aerodrome or **from an aircraft in flight** without written permission of DGCA (or JDG, DDG, Director R&I); DGCA may relax by gazette notification.
- **Rule 14**: only **Category A**-registered aircraft for aerial work or public transport (Govt may permit others).
- **Rule 15**: flight needs a valid **C of A** (or special one), maintenance per DGCA, conditions complied with, certificate on board; else a **special flight permit (Rule 55A)**. **15A**: drones **> 250 g** need a **UIN**.
- **Rule 16**: fly per **ICAO Annex 2** as issued by DGCA. **Rule 17**: produce licences, logbooks, certificates to authorised officials (magistrate, police, customs, defence, DGCA); if not carried, within **7 days**. **Rule 18**: illegal flights prevented by detention (written order, blocking, defuelling, removing parts, force). **Rules 19/19A**: cancellation/suspension/restriction of certificates.
- **Rules 21–23**: no dangerous low flying; no assault, threat, intimidation of crew or refusal of PIC instructions; no endangering behaviour.
- **Rule 24**: crew **no alcohol, sedatives or narcotics within 12 hours** of flight, never intoxicated; **no alcohol served or consumed on domestic flights**; no flying under psychoactive substances. **24A**: persons with mental disorders/epilepsy only if certified fit, no alcohol in 12 h, sedated and accompanied as needed. **24B** prisoners and **24C** animals/birds/reptiles: **DGCA permit**.
- **Rule 25**: smoking rules displayed; smoking only if C of A / Govt allows.
- **Rule 25A – fuelling**: petroleum in bulk (**> 900 L**) only from approved trucks, hydrants, barges. **No smoking/naked flames within 30 m**; engines off; ignition **OFF**; radar, radio and most electrics off; ground power units outside the **danger zone (3 m around wings and fuel vehicle)**, flame-proof or **15 m (gasoline) / 6 m (kerosene)** away, not switched during fuel flow; aircraft and truck **bonded and earthed**; only essential people within **15 m**; passengers on board only with a crew attendant present; no baggage loading or spark-making maintenance; extinguishers ready; **stop on a spill** (never wash into drains); stop if a jet's exhaust comes within **43 m**.
- **Rule 25B**: aircraft with fuel in tanks housed only in **non-flammable, ventilated** hangars under a competent person.
- **Rule 26 – dropping**: only **ballast** (sand/water), **emergency** fuel/cargo, **message bags**, nav aids, **paper or flower petals with DM/Police Commissioner permission**, **towing ropes**; parachutes only in emergency unless Govt permits.
- **Rule 27**: no riding on wings, undercarriage or other external parts.
- **Rule 28/28A – age**: **16** minimum for sole control. Professional pilots: **65 maximum**; **60–65 only in multi-crew** with the other pilot **under 60**; exception: single-pilot aircraft **≤ 5,700 kg** in India.
- **Rule 29/29B**: no interference, tampering, disorderly behaviour; **no sonic booms over India**; **no portable electronic devices in flight** except hearing aids, pacemakers, shavers and approved devices; **mobile phones only after landing and clear of the active runway**.
- **29C/29D**: DGCA adopts ICAO Annexes; **SMS mandatory** for operators, ATS, training organisations, manufacturers.
- **Rules 30/33 – registration**: **Category A**: owned by **Indian citizens, Indian companies, Central/State Govt**, or leased to Indian entities. **Category B**: owned by non-citizens/foreign businesses in India. Ownership change notified to DGCA.
- **Rule 38**: licences by Central Govt/DGCA. **Private** aircraft: valid **PPL** (not student). **Public transport / aerial work**: **CPL or ATPL**. Instructors: instructor rating or DGCA authorisation. Navigator/engineer/FRTOL as required.
- **Rule 38B – cabin crew**: seats **10–50: 1**; helicopters 20–50: 1; **51+: 2**, plus **1 per 50 seats (or part) above 99**.
- **38C**: flight dispatchers need **DGCA approval**. **39A**: licence may be suspended/cancelled for habitual alcohol/drug use, using an aircraft for crime, endangering safety, serious conviction, false information. **39B**: medical by a DGCA-approved medical authority.
- **Rule 42 – renewal**: expired **< 2 years**: normal renewal; **2–3 years**: **skill test + Air Regulations exam**; **> 3 years**: all tests as for a new licence. **Illness/injury** keeping you off duty **15 days or more**, or any injury affecting duties, invalidates the licence until a **new medical**; report to DGCA immediately.
- **Rule 52**: safety-affecting modifications/repairs need DGCA approval (manufacturer-approved ones deemed approved unless DGCA says otherwise).
- **Rule 65**: aeronautical lights/beacons only with **Central Government written approval**; damaging them is illegal. **Rule 66 – false lights** (lantern/wish kites, lasers, strong beams) within **5 NM** that look like aviation lights, dazzle, confuse or endanger: notice to **extinguish/screen immediately** (extinguish first, then appeal to DGCA via the airport operator).
- **Rules 67A/67B – logbooks**: in **ink**; kept **5 years** after the last entry; self-certified **monthly**; instructor certifies training entries; **quarterly** certification (end of March, June, September, December) by the Ops Manager/Chief Pilot (airlines), **CFI** (trainees) or a DGCA officer. Logging: PIC as PIC; co-pilot as co-pilot; **student logs PIC only when sole occupant**; instructor logs PIC with remark "instructional"; examiner as PIC; dual = student logs "dual"; supervised PIC duties as PIC only if rated and authorised, else co-pilot with remark; instrument time only when **solely on instruments**; **simulator time = instrument ground time**; navigators/engineers log only time on duty.
- **Rules 79–80**: aerodrome licences only for Indian citizens, companies, Govt bodies, registered societies; apply to DGCA with an **Aerodrome Manual**.
- **Rule 133A**: DGCA directions via NOTAMs, AIP, AICs, **CARs**, notices; **draft CARs on the DGCA website for 30 days** for comments.
- **Rule 134**: scheduled services need **Central Government** permission (Schedule XI); foreign airlines under an **air services agreement** (or Govt terms). **140**: operators meet DGCA minimum requirements. **140A**: new/altered/discontinued routes or new timetables need **DGCA concurrence**, **7 days' notice**. **140B**: DGCA-approved **Operations Manual** (responsibilities, crew and succession of command, normal/emergency procedures, minimum safe altitudes, aerodrome weather minima, radio and nav equipment) — a copy carried on every flight.
- **Rule 161**: penalties per **Schedule VI**; defence if due to accident, weather or unavoidable cause, or without the person's knowledge or fault.

### Indian Aircraft Rules, 1920 (customs) (p.396)
- International flights **arrive at and depart from designated customs aerodromes** (except emergencies); no prohibited goods; customs seals not broken; PIC lets customs board and examine.

### Indian Aircraft (Public Health) Rules, 1954 (p.396–398)
- Incubation periods: **yellow fever and plague 6 days**, **cholera 5 days**, **smallpox and typhus 14 days**.
- Commander sends a **health report at least 2 hours before landing** (ill persons, quarantinable disease). Health officer may examine, **disinfect** (mosquito-borne disease areas) and apply disease-specific measures. Departing persons certified fit; **pilgrims** need **cholera and yellow fever** certificates.
- Bodies of those who died of **yellow fever, plague, anthrax**: only **cremated ashes** in a sealed urn. Others: **triple sealed** (hermetic zinc shell, wooden coffin, zinc-lined case with treated sawdust).
- No throwing infectious matter from aircraft. Penalty: up to **3 months** and/or **₹1,000**.
- **APHO** (Airport Health Organisation, under the DGHS, Ministry of Health): IHR 2005 core capacities; yellow fever checks, vaccination and quarantine; disinsection/deratting; sanitation and food inspection; dead body clearance; aircraft medical emergencies; PHEIC screening.

### Other rules (p.398–399)
- **Aircraft Manual (India) Vol I**: Aircraft Act 1934, Aircraft Rules 1937, Indian Aircraft Rules 1920. **Vol II**: Part I national, Part II international conventions.
- **Demolition of Obstructions Rules 1994** (buildings, trees near aerodromes); **Carriage of Dangerous Goods Rules 2003** (ICAO Technical Instructions + DGCA); **Aircraft (Security) Rules 2011** (NCASP, ASG, Commissioner of Security (CA), BCAS); **Investigation of Accidents and Incidents Rules 2017** (Annex 13); **Drone Rules 2021** (simpler, liberal); **RTR (Restricted) Certificate and Licence Rules 2025** (only holders may operate aeronautical radio).

### Exam traps
- Three bodies: **DGCA safety, BCAS security, AAIB investigation**.
- Penalty **2 years / ₹1 crore**; slaughter/waste within **10 km**: **3 years**; buildings/trees **20 km**.
- Alcohol **12 hours**; minimum solo age **16**; max **65** (60–65 multi-crew with a pilot under 60).
- Renewal: **< 2 y** normal, **2–3 y** skill test + Air Regs, **> 3 y** everything. Sick **15 days** → new medical.
- Documents not on board: produce in **7 days**. Logbooks kept **5 years**, certified **quarterly**.
- Fuelling: **30 m** no smoking, **3 m** danger zone, **15 m / 6 m** equipment, **15 m** people, **43 m** jet exhaust, **900 L** bulk.
- Cabin crew: **1** for 10–50 seats, **2** above 50, +1 per 50 above 99.
- Category **A** registration for public transport/aerial work.
- Health report **2 h** before landing; yellow fever **6 days**.

## 15. Personnel Licensing

### General (p.415–418)
- **Annex 1** covers licensing of flight crew (pilots, flight engineers, navigators), ATCOs, aeronautical station operators, maintenance technicians and flight dispatchers; medical standards act as an early warning of incapacitating conditions.
- Licensing authority in India: the **Central Government** (DGCA), which may withhold grant or renewal.
- Education: **Class 10** for SPL, PPL, glider/balloon/microlight licences, FRTOL; **Class 10+2 with Physics and Mathematics** for **CPL, ATPL**, navigator and flight engineer licences.
- **Cross-country**: beyond **100 NM** from the departure aerodrome; for **PPL and microlight** beyond **50 NM**.
- **English language proficiency** (PPL, CPL, ATPL, navigator, engineer, ATCO, station operator): **Level 6 Expert** (no re-evaluation); **Level 5 Extended** (every **6 years**); **Level 4 Operational** (every **3 years**) — minimum acceptable; levels 3–1 not acceptable.

### Medicals (p.416–417)
- **Class 1**: CPL, ATPL (aeroplanes and helicopters), and PPL holders needing **instrument rating** privileges. **Class 2**: PPL, microlight, all student pilot licences, FRTOL (R). **Class 3**: student ATCO and ATCO.
- Validity: **Class 1: 1 year up to 60, then 6 months** (single-crew operations: 6 months above **40**). **Class 2: 2 years up to 50, then 1 year**. **Class 3: 4 years up to 40, 2 years 40–50, 1 year above 50**.
- Licence **invalid** without a fresh medical after **sickness or injury causing incapacity of 15 days or more**, or an injury likely to impair efficiency; holder/employer tells DGCA **immediately**. Don't use privileges while aware your fitness has fallen below standard. Unfitness **15–60 days**: special medical by a DGCA-empanelled Class 1 examiner. **In-flight incapacitation** reported by the operator to DGCA.

### Validity and ages (p.417)
- **All licences valid 10 years** (since **10 Oct 2023**), from the date of issue; renewal runs from expiry if applied for within **1 month** before expiry.
- Minimum ages: **sole control 16**; **SPL 16, PPL 17, CPL 18, ATPL 21**. Maximum: **65** for PIC or co-pilot in commercial air transport; **60–65** only multi-crew with the other pilot under 60 (exception: single-pilot certified aircraft ≤ **5,700 kg** within India in multi-crew).

### Student Pilot's Licence (p.418–419)
- **Oral** exam in Air Regulations, Navigation, Meteorology, Aircraft & Engines. **Police security clearance** obtained by the flying club/school before training.
- Renewed for up to **12 months** from a fresh medical, total validity not more than **24 months** from issue. Rated only on types whose Aircraft & Engines exam was passed.
- Privileges: **within India**, PIC of rated types, **under an instructor's/examiner's supervision**, **VFR only**, **no passengers, animals, goods or hire**; **no cross-country** until **10 hours solo** and passing Navigation and Meteorology. Issued by an authorised flying club/school.

### Private Pilot's Licence (Aeroplanes) (p.419–421)
- Written exams (Air Regs, Nav, Met, Aircraft & Engines; helicopter licence holders only Aircraft & Engines).
- Experience: **40 hours** including **20 solo**, **5 hours solo cross-country** with a flight of at least **150 NM** and full-stop landings at **two** different aerodromes, and **10 hours solo in the last 12 months**. Credit: 50% of microlight solo (last 24 months, max 10 h) and 50% of glider solo (max 10 h).
- Skill test within **6 months** before applying.
- Renewal: medical + **5 hours as PIC in the last 12 months** (or a flying test in the last 6 months).
- **Open rating** (all single piston aeroplanes ≤ **1,500 kg**): **250 h as PIC** and **4 types** on the licence, after familiarisation with an instructor/examiner.
- **Night rating** (to carry passengers at night): **50 h PIC** incl. **5 h night** with **5 take-offs and 5 landings** in the last 6 months; a **dual night cross-country of 100 NM** before solo night cross-country; **5 h dual instrument** (max 2½ h simulator).
- Privileges: PIC or co-pilot **not for remuneration**, carrying passengers; at night only with a night rating and **5 night take-offs and landings in the last 6 months**; IFR only with an instrument rating.

### Commercial Pilot's Licence (Aeroplanes) (p.421–424)
- Written exams + **Signals (practical)**; CPL(H) holders only Aircraft & Engines.
- Experience: **200 hours** including **100 h PIC** (15 h in the last **6 months**), **20 h cross-country as PIC** with a **300 NM** flight landing at **two** aerodromes, **10 h instrument** (max **5 h simulator**), **5 h night** with **10 take-offs and 10 landings** as PIC in the last 6 months. Helicopter pilots with **1,000 h PIC**: reduced to 75 h PIC etc.
- Needs a current **FRTOL**. **Skill test** within 6 months: general flying by **day** and **night**, **day cross-country ≥ 250 NM** with a full-stop landing elsewhere, **night cross-country ≥ 120 NM** returning without landing (level D simulator may be allowed).
- Renewal: medical + **10 h PIC in the last 6 months** (50% co-pilot time counts) or day and night flying tests; current FRTOL.
- **Open rating**: single piston ≤ 1,500 kg with **1,000 h** on such types incl. **500 h PIC** and 4 types.
- **Proficiency check** to be co-pilot of transport aeroplanes > **5,700 kg**: valid **6 months**, renewed 6 months at a time (from expiry if done within **2 months** before).
- Privileges: all PPL privileges; **PIC of aeroplanes ≤ 5,700 kg** on the rating (passengers at night need **10 night take-offs and landings in the last 6 months**); **co-pilot** of any rated aeroplane; IFR needs a current instrument rating. At **65**, privileges fall to PPL.

### CPL with Instrument Rating (p.424–427)
- Experience: **200 h within the last 5 years**, including **100 h PIC** (of which **30 h may be student PIC**: max 20 h cross-country, max 10 h circuits with ≥ 10 landings), **15 h PIC in the last 6 months**, **50 h cross-country PIC** incl. a **300 NM** flight with two landings, **50 h instrument** (max **20 h simulator**, 5 h in the last 6 months), **5 h night** with 10 take-offs/landings. SPIC time is logged only by the student, remark "SPIC" with the instructor's name.
- Skill tests as CPL plus an **instrument flying test**. Instrument privileges are included in the licence.

### Airline Transport Pilot's Licence (Aeroplanes) (p.427–430)
- Written and oral exams: Air Regs, Navigation, **Avionics (radio aids and instruments)**, Met, Aircraft & Engines, Signals; a CPL holder need not repeat **Air Regulations**.
- Experience: **1,500 hours**, including **250 h as PIC or co-pilot performing PIC duties under supervision** (at least **100 h cross-country**, including **50 h by night**), **500 h cross-country**, **75 h night**, **100 h instrument** (at least **50 h in actual flight**), **20 h in the last 6 months**. Co-pilot time credited at 50% toward total where a co-pilot is required.
- Must hold a **CPL** (not needed for armed forces pilots), a current **multi-engine instrument rating** and **FRTOL**. Skill test day and night on a **multi-engine** type within 6 months (CPL holders exempt if on a rated multi-engine type with 10 h PIC or 20 h co-pilot in the last 6 months).
- Renewal: medical + **10 h PIC in 6 months** (50% co-pilot counts) or flying tests; FRTOL; multi-engine IR.
- **Open rating**: all aeroplanes ≤ **5,700 kg** with **1,000 h PIC** on aeroplanes of **14,000 kg** or more.
- **Proficiency check** for PIC or co-pilot of transport aeroplanes > 5,700 kg: 6-month validity.
- Privileges: PPL and CPL privileges; **PIC of aeroplanes > 5,700 kg** after **100 h as co-pilot on type** followed by **10 consecutive route checks (5 at night)** under a check pilot (only **6 (3 night)** if already **100 h PIC** on transport aeroplanes > 5,700 kg), a proficiency check in the last 6 months, and a current IR for IFR. At **65**, PPL privileges only.

### Instrument Rating (Aeroplanes) (p.430–431)
- Written and oral (Air Regs, Nav, Met, IR) + signals practical.
- Experience: **100 h PIC** incl. **50 h cross-country**; **40 h instrument time** (max **20 h** instrument ground time), **5 h in the last 6 months**. Helicopter IR holders: 100 h PIC aeroplane, 25 h cross-country, 20 h instrument (max 10 sim).
- Needs a current pilot licence and FRTOL; instrument flying test within 6 months.
- **Validity 12 months** from the test; renewed 12 months from expiry if tested within **2 months** before expiry (else from the test date).
- Privileges: IFR on types tested. Test on a single-engine ≤ 5,700 kg → all single-engine ≤ 5,700 kg; multi-engine ≤ 5,700 kg → all multi-engine ≤ 5,700 kg; aeroplanes > 5,700 kg → **that type only**; a multi-engine IR covers single-engine types on the licence.

### Exam traps
- Cross-country **100 NM** (PPL/microlight **50 NM**).
- ELP **4 → 3 years, 5 → 6 years, 6 → never**.
- Class 1: **12 months to 60, 6 after** (single-crew > 40: 6 months); Class 2: **24 months to 50, 12 after**; Class 3: **4/2/1 years** at 40/50.
- Licences now valid **10 years**.
- Ages **16 / 17 / 18 / 21**.
- PPL **40 h (20 solo, 150 NM XC)**; CPL **200 h (100 PIC, 300 NM XC, 10 h IF, 5 h night)**; ATPL **1,500 h (500 XC, 75 night, 100 IF)**.
- CPL PIC limit **5,700 kg**; ATPL PIC > 5,700 kg after **100 h co-pilot + 10 route checks (5 night)**.
- Night passengers: PPL **5**, CPL **10** take-offs and landings in 6 months.
- IR **12 months**; proficiency check **6 months**; renew within **2 months** of expiry to keep the date.

## 16. Airworthiness of Aircraft

### Annex 8 (p.443–444)
- Aircraft must be designed, built and operated to the airworthiness requirements of the **State of Registry**, which issues a **Certificate of Airworthiness** declaring it fit to fly.
- **Annex 8** treats airworthiness from the **engineering** side; **Annex 6 Part I** (performance operating limitations) from the **operational** side. States should not impose on visiting aeroplanes operational requirements beyond those of the State of Registry, if those meet Annex 6.
- The **State of Registry** must **recognise and render valid** a C of A issued by another State if issued to standards at least equal to ICAO minimums (helps import/export, lease, charter, interchange).
- ICAO Standards don't replace national codes; each State may write its own detailed code or adopt another State's. Guidance: **Airworthiness Technical Manual, Doc 9760**.
- **Type Certificate**: issued by the **State of Design** when the type design meets the requirements.
- Annex 8 technical standards currently apply to **multi-engined aeroplanes over 5,700 kg**. Four parts: **I** definitions; **II** certification and continuing airworthiness; **III** large aeroplanes; **IV** helicopters.
- Security design features: protected systems, a **least-risk bomb location**, reinforced **cockpit door**, ceilings and floors.

### Indian provisions (p.444–445)
- Owner/operator applies to the **DGCA** for a C of A, a **special C of A**, or validation of a foreign C of A; issued when documents are provided and DGCA is satisfied the aircraft is airworthy.
- No safety-affecting **modification or repair** without the rules requiring it or **DGCA's prior approval**.
- C of A **deemed suspended** when the aircraft stops conforming to operation/maintenance/modification rules, is modified or repaired outside the rules, suffers **major damage**, or develops a **major defect** affecting safety.
- If DGCA has reasonable doubt about the safety of an aircraft or its type, it may **suspend or cancel** the C of A or require modifications, inspections and flight tests.
- **No flight while the C of A is suspended**; DGCA may issue a **special flight permit** on application.
- Where a **flight manual** is required, DGCA endorses the C of A. Aircraft carry the instruments, equipment and radio specified for the flight.

### Exam traps
- **Type Certificate → State of Design**; **C of A → State of Registry** (DGCA for VT aircraft).
- Annex 8 = engineering; Annex 6 = operational.
- Annex 8 large-aeroplane standards: **multi-engined, > 5,700 kg**.
- Major damage or defect → C of A **deemed suspended** → only a **special flight permit**.

## 17. Operational Procedures

### Annex 6 (p.449)
- **Part I** international commercial air transport (aeroplanes); **Part II** international general aviation (from **Sept 1969**); **Part III** international helicopter operations (from **Nov 1986**).

### General requirements (p.449–451)
- One **quality system** and one **quality manager**, with feedback to the **accountable manager**.
- No person in a part of the aeroplane not designed for people, except with the commander's temporary permission for safety or to reach accessible cargo/stores.
- **Flight deck admission**: only assigned flight crew, an operating crew member, an authority representative on official duty, or someone permitted by the Operations Manual; **final decision: the commander**. Authority inspectors may board and enter the flight deck, but the commander may refuse if safety would be endangered.
- No stowaways or hidden cargo; no portable electronic devices that may affect aircraft systems.

### Documents carried (CAR Section 2 Series X Part VII; Rule 7) (p.450)
- C of R, C of A, **Airworthiness Review Certificate**, noise certificate, **AOC**, crew licences, radio licence, **journey log book**, **Operations Manual**, **MEL**, **Flight Manual**, Cabin Crew Manual, cockpit checklists, **search procedure checklist with least-risk bomb location**, maintenance release/CRS, **LOPA**, emergency equipment layout, route guides, current charts (incl. diversions), weight schedule, **load and trim sheet**, passenger list, cargo manifest, **dangerous goods list (shown to the PIC)**, **insurance** (passengers, baggage, crew, third party, hull — hull optional for training aircraft), **safety briefing card at every seat** (pictorial).
- Physical copies unless approved on an **EFB**; DGCA certificates in original or DGCA-attested copies.
- Flight information **preserved on the ground** for each flight until duplicated where it is stored, or else carried in a **fireproof container**.

### Operator certification (p.451)
- **AOC**: authorises specified commercial air transport; issued by the **State of the Operator** (principal place of business, or permanent residence) after the operator shows adequate organisation, training, flight operations, ground handling and maintenance. Remains valid while the original standards are kept under **continued surveillance**. Other States recognise AOCs meeting Annex 6.

### Operational requirements (p.451–454)
- Use of **autoland, HUD, EVS, SVS, CVS** for operational credit approved by the **State of the Operator**. **EFB**: electronic system for storing, updating, displaying and processing flight-crew information.
- Operator exercises **operational control** by an approved method. Use **ATS whenever available**. Use the State's published **instrument departure and approach procedures**; the commander may accept ATC deviations if obstacle clearance is assured, with the final approach flown visually or per the procedure.
- **Persons with reduced mobility** may not be refused (with aids, escorts, guide dogs) if they tell the airline at booking/check-in. Procedures for **inadmissible passengers, deportees, persons in custody**; the commander must be told.
- Baggage securely stowed; passengers seated to **help, not hinder, evacuation**; exits and escape paths clear before taxi, take-off, landing; commander ensures everything secured.
- **No smoking**: when unsafe, on the ground (unless allowed), outside designated areas, **in aisles and toilets**, in cargo areas without flame-resistant containers/covers, **where oxygen is being supplied**.
- **Take-off**: commander satisfied weather and runway allow a safe take-off, and **RVR/visibility ≥ the applicable minimum**.
- **EDTO** (ETOPS): two or more turbine engines where diversion time to an en-route alternate (ISA, still air, **one-engine-inoperative** cruise for twins; **all-engines** cruise for 3–4 engines) exceeds the **threshold time** set by DGCA; needs specific approval.
- **RVSM** (FL 290–410, 1,000 ft): equipment to show the level, **hold it automatically**, **alert at deviations of no more than ±300 ft**, and **report pressure altitude automatically**; DGCA authorisation; demonstrated height-keeping. RVSM approval is valid **globally**.

### All-weather operations (p.454–456)
- **LVP**: aerodrome procedures for **Cat II/III approaches and LVTO**. **LVTO**: take-off with **RVR < 400 m**.
- Cat II/III only if the aeroplane is certificated for DH < 200 ft (or none), an approach/autoland success-failure recording system exists, the authority approves, **at least 2 pilots**, and **DH set by radio altimeter**.
- **LVTO below 400 m** needs DGCA approval; scheduled operators down to **125 m RVR** with a **90 m visual segment** at the start of the roll; **non-scheduled and GA: not below 500 m**. Take-off below **Cat I conditions (550 m RVR / 800 m visibility)** only with **LVP in force**.
- Low-visibility training per an FSD-approved syllabus; commander checks facilities, **LVPs in force** (from ATS) and crew qualification. **Full rated take-off thrust** for LVTO; **no supervised take-offs or landings** in Cat II/III or LVTO. Must-work items for LVTO: wipers, window heat, **anti-skid**, **thrust reversers**.
- **Aerodrome operating minima**: **Normal AOM** (scheduled and approved GA/non-scheduled) from AAI-published data; **Restricted AOM** = normal **DA/MDA + 100 ft** and **visibility/RVR + 400 m**.
- **Approach ban**: don't **start** an instrument approach if reported RVR/visibility is below minimum. If it drops below after starting, don't continue **below 1,000 ft above the aerodrome** or **into the final approach segment**. Once past that point, you may continue to DA/MDA, and land if the visual reference is established there and maintained. RVR may be derived from visibility.
- **Touchdown-zone RVR always controls**; mid-point and stop-end also control if reported and relevant (high-speed part of the landing to about 60 kt): mid-point minimum **125 m** (or the TDZ value if less; **50 m** with roll-out guidance), stop-end **50 m**.
- VFR in VMC; **no special VFR for commercial air transport aeroplanes**.

### Instruments and equipment (p.456–461)
- **Spare fuses**: **10%** of each rating or **3** of each rating, whichever greater.
- **Windshield wipers** at each pilot station: MCTOM **> 5,700 kg**.
- **Weather radar**: pressurised passenger aeroplanes where TS or other detectable hazards are expected at night or in IMC.
- **Flight recorders**: crash-protected FDR, CVR, AIR, DLR; lightweight ADRS, CARS, AIRS, DLRS. **> 15,000 kg** with both CVR and FDR: **two combination recorders**, one near the cockpit, one far aft. **FDR ≥ 25 hours**; **CVR/data link ≥ 2 hours**; **> 27,000 kg, first C of A from 1 Jan 2022: CVR 25 hours**. CVR records from before moving under own power until no longer able to; **no magnetic tape or wire**.
- Pressure altitude source with **25 ft** resolution. **Boom or throat microphones below the transition level/altitude**.
- **Forward-looking wind shear warning + GPWS with forward-looking terrain avoidance**: turbojets **> 5,700 kg** or **> 9 passengers**.
- **ACAS II**: turbine aeroplanes **> 5,700 kg** or **> 19 passengers**.
- **Reinforced flight deck door** (small arms, grenade shrapnel, forced intrusion; lockable from either pilot seat): passenger aeroplanes **> 45,500 kg** or **> 60 seats**.
- **First-aid kits**: **0–100 pax: 1**; **101–200: 2**; **201–300: 3**; **301+: 4**. **Universal precaution kit**: 1 where cabin crew are required (**2** if > **250** passengers). **Medical kit** (for doctors): > **100 passengers** on sectors > **2 hours**.
- **Oxygen**: cabin pressure **< 700 hPa (10,000 ft)**: unpressurised flights carry oxygen for **all crew and 10% of passengers** for time beyond **30 minutes** between **700 and 620 hPa (10,000–13,000 ft)**, and **everyone** below **620 hPa (13,000 ft)**. Pressurised: oxygen for everyone for any time below 700 hPa after decompression; above **25,000 ft (376 hPa)** (or unable to descend to 13,000 ft within **4 minutes**) at least a **10-minute** supply for all cabin occupants; flying above 25,000 ft needs a **pressurisation loss warning**.
- **Crew PBE** (pressurised, or > 5,700 kg, or > 19 seats): eyes, nose and mouth protection with **15 minutes** of gas for each flight crew member on duty (plus portable PBE if no cabin crew) and each required cabin crew member.

### Fuel policy (p.459–461)
- Based on aeroplane-specific consumption data (or manufacturer's), mass, NOTAMs, weather, ATS delays, MEL items.
- **Taxi fuel** (incl. APU); **trip fuel** (take-off or re-planning point to landing); **contingency fuel**: **5% of trip fuel**, but not less than **5 minutes holding at 1,500 ft** above destination; **destination alternate fuel** (missed approach, climb, cruise, descent, approach and landing at the alternate; with two alternates, the greater; with **no alternate, 15 minutes holding at 1,500 ft**; **isolated aerodrome**: piston **45 min + 15% of cruise time (incl. final reserve) or 2 hours**, whichever less; turbine **2 hours at normal cruise** incl. final reserve); **final reserve fuel**: **piston 45 minutes**; **turbine 30 minutes holding at 1,500 ft** above aerodrome; **additional fuel** (engine failure/depressurisation at the critical point, then 15 min hold and landing; EDTO critical fuel); **discretionary fuel** at the PIC's discretion.

### Emergency equipment (p.461–463)
- **Hand fire extinguishers** (no dangerous air contamination): at least one in the **flight deck** and in each passenger compartment not readily accessible to the flight crew.
- **Crash axe or crowbar** on the flight deck: **> 5,700 kg** or **> 9 seats**; another near the rear galley if **> 200 seats**.
- **Break-in points** marked **red or yellow** (outlined white if needed).
- Annual emergency and evacuation training with drills.
- **Megaphones**: **61–99 seats: 1**; **100+: 2**.
- **Emergency lighting** with independent power: passenger aeroplanes **> 9 seats**.
- **ELT**: every aeroplane carries an **automatic ELT**; **> 19 passengers**: at least **two ELTs**, one automatic.
- Passenger briefing: seat belts, exits, life jackets, oxygen, other equipment, briefing card.
- **Extended over-water**: life rafts for everyone, enough even if the largest raft is lost; each with a **survivor locator light**, survival equipment and at least **two survival ELTs**. Lists of emergency and survival equipment available to **RCCs** at all times.
- Communications: two-way for aerodrome control, receive met information, two-way with at least one aeronautical station, and **121.5 MHz**; RCP equipment and approval where prescribed. Navigation equipment for the operational flight plan and ATS (VFR by landmarks excepted); PBN equipment and DGCA approval where prescribed. **NAT HLA** (formerly MNPSA) **FL 285–420**.
- Over areas where SAR is especially difficult (designated by AAI): portable **VHF survival radio** usable by unskilled persons away from the aircraft, signalling and life-saving equipment. IFR needs an **audio selector panel** at each crew station.

### Flight and duty time limitations (CAR Section 7 Series J) (p.463–468)
- **Rule 42A** of the Aircraft Rules: DGCA sets limits; operators submit a scheme (part of the Operations Manual).
- **Scheduled commuter operators**: aircraft up to **40,000 kg**, between local destinations except **Category I cities** (Mumbai, Kolkata, Delhi, Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Thiruvananthapuram, Chennai).
- **Acclimatised**: to a **3-hour-wide** time zone around departure local time; if the next duty starts more than 3 h away in time, acclimatised to the departure zone for the first **48 hours**.
- **Duty period**: report to free of all duties. **FDP**: report until **engines off** after the last flight. **Flight time**: block to block. **Local night**: **8 hours between 2200 and 0800**. **Night duty**: any duty touching **0000–0600** (acclimatised zone). **WOCL**: **0200–0600**. **Positioning = deadheading**. **Standby**: available for duty without intervening rest. **Rest period**: continuous, free of all duties. **ULR**: any sector **> 14 h flight time**, FDP up to **22 h**.
- Operator publishes rosters for at least **7 days** with weekly rest shown; home base or temporary home base (**7–28 days**). Crew must not accept assignments beyond limits or fly when fatigued.
- **Two-pilot limits (24 h)**: flight time **8 h** with FDP **11:00 → 6 landings**, **11:30 → 5**, **12:00 → 4**, **12:30 → 3**; flight time **9 h**, FDP **13:00 → 2 landings**; flight time **10 h**, FDP **13:00 → 1 landing**. Encroaching on night: **8 h flight time, 10 h FDP, 2 landings**.
- **Augmented crew**: **3 crew**: 12 h flight time; FDP 16 h (bunk) / 15 h (seat) / 14 h; 2 landings. **4 crew**: 14 h; FDP 18 h (bunk) / 16 h (seat). **ULR (4 crew)**: 17 h; FDP 21 h.
- **Cumulative**: flight time **35 h / 7 days, 65 / 14, 100 / 28, 300 / 90, 1,000 / 365**; duty **60 / 7, 100 / 14, 190 / 28, 600 / 90, 1,800 / 365**.
- **Unforeseen circumstances**: flight time **+1 h**, FDP **+2 h**, **one extra landing** on a diversion; cumulative extensions max **4 h flight time / 8 h FDP in 28 days**; rest **+2 h** (FDP extension ≤ 1 h or flight time ≤ 30 min) or **+4 h** (beyond). Not combined with split duty. If no flying after reporting, rest at least **twice the time since reporting**, minimum **12 h**. PIC (with crew) consents to Head of Operations; quarterly report to DGCA.
- Duty and rest **records kept 18 months**.

## 18. Special Operational Procedures and Hazards

### MEL and flight manual (p.481)
- The **MEL** (in the Operations Manual, **approved by DGCA**) lets the PIC decide whether a flight may start or continue from an intermediate stop with something inoperative; may differ for night/IMC and day/VMC. The **MMEL** is drawn up by the **manufacturer** (approved by the authority). An operator's MEL may be **no less restrictive** than the MMEL.
- Operate per the **Flight Manual approved by the State of Design**, the C of A and its limitations, allowing for mass, procedures, pressure altitude, temperature, wind, **runway slope** and surface contamination.

### Icing (p.481–483)
- No take-off in known or suspected ground icing until inspected and, if necessary, **de-iced/anti-iced**; no flight into icing unless certificated and equipped; at **night** also a means to **illuminate or detect** ice (without glare).
- **De-icing**: removing frost, ice, snow or slush with **heated de-icing fluid (ADF)**. **Anti-icing**: applying **anti-icing fluid (AAF)** to a clean surface to protect it for a limited time (**holdover time**). Fluids are needed only until airborne.
- Roughness as thin as **0.40 mm (1/64 in)** causes severe **lift loss, more drag, worse manoeuvrability**, especially at take-off and initial climb; ice can jam controls, add weight, block **pitot/static** and AoA vanes, obscure windscreens. **No ice is insignificant.**
- **Cold-soaking**: cold fuel in wing tanks chills the skin below 0 °C so ice forms even with **OAT above freezing** (after long high cruise, quick descent into humid air, or cold fuel); often invisible **clear ice**, found by **tactile inspection** or ground ice detection systems; sheets breaking off can be ingested by **rear-mounted engines** or hit the tailplane.

### Bird strikes (p.483)
- Away from aerodromes the only defence is **avoiding bird concentrations** (migration, daily cycles), using monitoring, forecasts and warnings. **Shell crackers** are the most effective scaring method. **90% of bird strikes occur below 500 m.** Birds on the runway fly off about **2 seconds** before an aircraft at about 135 kt arrives.

### Noise abatement (p.484)
- **Departure**: not below **800 ft (240 m)** above the aerodrome; same procedure for a type at all aerodromes; **not used with wind shear warnings** or suspected wind shear/downbursts; never exceed the maximum body angle.
- **Approach**: reverse thrust above idle may be banned (e.g. at night); be in the **final landing configuration** from the **outer marker or 5 NM** from the threshold, whichever earlier.
- **Noise preferential routes** avoid noise-sensitive areas; no turns combined with noise-abatement power reductions.
- Noise abatement must not decide the runway when **crosswind (incl. gusts) > 15 kt**, **tailwind > 5 kt**, or the runway is **not clear and dry**. Lack of ILS/visual guidance doesn't bar a runway for noise abatement when landing in VMC.

### Fire and smoke (p.484–486)
- Fire needs **oxygen, heat and fuel** (fire triangle); remove one to extinguish it.
- **Class A** (wood, paper, cloth, plastic): cool with **water** (non-alcoholic liquids); **water/glycol** most effective; grey-brown smoke. **Class B** (fuel, oil, hydraulic fluid, tar): **foam or halon**, not water; thick **black** smoke, oily smell. **Class C** (electrical): non-conducting agent, **halon**; light grey/white bluish smoke, acrid. **Class D** (sodium, magnesium, lithium, potassium): **special dry powder**; **never halon**. **A and C** are the commonest on board.
- Hand extinguishers discharge for only **8–25 seconds**. Colours: **Halon/BCF green** (A, B, C); **CO₂ black** (B, C); **dry powder blue** (D); **dry chemical blue** (A, B, C); **water red** (A). Halon is **3 times** as effective as CO₂; after halon on a class A fire, cool the area with a non-alcoholic liquid; use **PBE** with halon in confined spaces. At least one **Halon 1211** extinguisher on the flight deck. Halon is being phased out (ozone); permitted only in cargo compartments until **28 Nov 2024**.

### Hot brakes (p.486)
- Heavy braking (rejected take-off, landing) → **loss of braking, fire, tyre deflation/burst** → runway or taxiway excursion, collisions, high workload. Crew may call **fire services** and decide on **evacuation** if fire is detected.

### Decompression (p.486–487)
- Causes: **structural failure** (window, door, bulkhead, explosion), **pressurisation system failure** (e.g. outflow valve), or a **deliberate** act (e.g. clearing smoke).
- **Explosive** decompression: **< 0.5 seconds**; faster than the lungs can vent → lung damage; dust, debris, **fog**, crew momentarily dazed. Slow decompression: same actions, more time.
- Danger: **hypoxia**; time of useful consciousness reduced by an explosive event. **Warning when cabin altitude exceeds 10,000 ft**; passenger masks drop between **13,200 and 14,000 ft** (never above **15,000 ft**). Structural failure may bring wind blast, debris, cold, people being sucked out (wear belts).
- Actions: **oxygen masks on immediately** (before a deliberate depressurisation), then **emergency descent** to about **10,000 ft**.

### Wind shear and microbursts (p.487–488)
- Report wind shear with wind and altitude above/below the layer (FMS), or as **airspeed loss or gain and altitude**, or its general effect.
- Significant non-convective wind shear: vector change **> 25 kt within 500 ft AGL**, **> 40 kt within 1,000 ft**, **> 50 kt within 1,500 ft**, or a pilot report of **IAS change ≥ 20 kt within 1,500 ft**.
- **Microburst**: about **1 NM** across at 2,000 ft, **2–2½ NM** at the surface; downdraughts up to **6,000 ft/min**; surface winds to **45 kt** (a **90 kt** shear); heavy rain in humid air, **virga** in dry air; life **≤ 15 minutes**, peak winds **2–4 minutes**; lines may last an hour; more than one is common. Take-off sequence: **headwind (performance up) → downdraught → tailwind (performance down)**; most dangerous **below 1,000 ft**.
- Defence: **avoid it**; if caught, **full power** and pitch up to the maximum usable AoA; warn others.

### Wake turbulence (p.488–493)
- Created **only when generating lift**: worst when the generator is **heavy, clean and slow**. Air spills from below around the tips: the **left** vortex turns **clockwise** seen from behind, the **right anticlockwise**; circulation **outward, upward and around** each tip. Vortices sink to about **500–1,000 ft below** the flight path, spread up to about 5 NM behind, and stay on the runway in **calm/light winds**; a light crosswind holds the **upwind** vortex on the runway; a strong crosswind can blow a parallel runway's vortices across. Worst wind: **light quartering tailwind**.
- Categories by MCTOM: **Super (J)**: **A380-800** (560,000 kg); **Heavy**: **≥ 136,000 kg**; **Medium**: **> 7,000 and < 136,000 kg**; **Light**: **< 7,000 kg**.
- **Time-based, arrivals**: A380 → Medium **3 min**, → Light **4 min**; Heavy → Medium **2 min**; Heavy or Medium → Light **3 min**.
- **Departures** (same runway, parallels < **760 m**, crossing paths at or < 1,000 ft below): A380 → non-A380 Heavy **2 min**, → Medium/Light **3 min**; Heavy → Medium/Light **2 min**; Medium → Light **2 min**. From an **intermediate part** of the runway: A380 → M/L **4 min**; Heavy → M/L **3 min**; Medium → Light **3 min**.
- **Displaced threshold** (opposite-direction or mixed operations): **2 min** (A380: **3 min**) for lighter categories.
- **Distance-based** (with surveillance; directly behind, same/parallel < 760 m runways, crossing behind at or < 1,000 ft below): behind **A380**: Heavy **6 NM**, Medium **7 NM**, Light **8 NM**; behind **Heavy**: Heavy **4 NM**, Medium **5 NM**, Light **6 NM**; behind **Medium**: Light **5 NM**; otherwise radar minimum. (The book's table prints Heavy→Heavy as 5 NM; ICAO PANS-ATM gives 4 NM.) Time-based spacing on final may replace distance in strong headwinds.
- Avoiding wake: stay **upwind** and **above** the larger aircraft's path; on take-off **lift off before** its rotation point and climb above its path; on landing **touch down beyond** its touchdown point; on an ILS stay **at or above the glide slope**.

### Other hazards (p.493–495)
- **Unlawful interference**: the commander (or operator) reports without delay to the local authority and the authority of the **State of the Operator**.
- **Forced landing**: immediate landing because flight can't continue (e.g. engine failure). **Precautionary landing**: premeditated because further flight is possible but **inadvisable** (weather, lost, fuel shortage, developing engine trouble). **Ditching**: forced or precautionary landing **on water**.
- **Fuel jettison** (to reach maximum landing mass): tell ATC; route clear of towns, preferably over water, away from TS; level **not below 6,000 ft (1,800 m)**; agree duration. Separate other traffic by **10 NM horizontally** (not behind), or vertically if behind within **15 minutes or 50 NM**: **1,000 ft above** or **3,000 ft below**. Agree a listening frequency if radio silence.
- **Dangerous goods**: forbidden unless exempted — items forbidden in the **Technical Instructions** and **infected live animals**.
- **Contaminated runways**: braking coefficient depends on surface friction. If the runway may be **wet** at ETA, **LDA ≥ 115%** of the required landing distance. **Dynamic hydroplaning**: standing water deeper than the tyre tread; tyres ride on the water, braking and steering almost lost; anti-skid reduces the effect.

### Exam traps
- MMEL: manufacturer; MEL: operator, approved by DGCA, ≥ as restrictive.
- **0.4 mm** of frost spoils lift; cold-soaked wings ice above 0 °C OAT.
- **90%** of bird strikes below **500 m**.
- Noise abatement not below **800 ft**; not with crosswind **> 15 kt**, tailwind **> 5 kt**, wet runway; landing configuration by **OM / 5 NM**.
- Fire classes A wood, B liquids, C electrical, D metals; halon never on D; water extinguisher red, CO₂ black, halon green.
- Explosive decompression **< 0.5 s**; masks drop **13,200–14,000 ft**; descend to **10,000 ft**.
- Wind shear criteria **25/500, 40/1,000, 50/1,500**; microburst **≤ 15 min**, peak **2–4 min**.
- Heavy **≥ 136 t**, Light **< 7 t**. Heavy→Light arrival **3 min**, departure **2 min**; distance **6 NM**.
- Fuel dump **≥ 6,000 ft**; others 10 NM, or 1,000 ft above / 3,000 ft below.
- Wet runway LDA **115%**.

## 19. Communications

### Abbreviations and Q-codes (p.521–523)
- Common abbreviations (ILS, QNH, RVR) are spoken as letters, not with the spelling alphabet.
- **QDM**: magnetic heading to steer to reach the station (no wind). **QDR**: your **magnetic bearing from** the station. **QTE**: your **true bearing from** the station. **QUJ**: true track to reach the station. **QDL**: intend to ask for a series of bearings. **QFE**: setting to read **height above the aerodrome/threshold**. **QNH**: setting to read **elevation** on the ground at the station. **QNE**: what the altimeter reads on landing with **1013.2** set. **QFU**: magnetic direction (or number) of the **runway in use** (2-figure number or 3-figure direction). **QGH**: may I land using (procedure/facility).

### Message categories, in priority order (p.523)
- (a) **Distress** (MAYDAY); (b) **urgency**, incl. **PAN PAN MEDICAL** and SECURITÉ; (c) **direction finding**; (d) **flight safety**; (e) **meteorological**; (f) **flight regularity**.

### Transmitting numbers (p.523–525)
- Flight levels, headings, wind, runway, QNH, frequencies: **each digit** (FL 180 = "WUN AIT ZE-RO"; heading 080 = "ZE-RO AIT ZE-RO"; Mach 0.84 = "DAY-SEE-MAL AIT FOW-er"; 128.3 = "WUN TOO AIT DAY-SEE-MAL TREE").
- Altitude, height, cloud height, visibility and RVR with whole hundreds/thousands: digits followed by **HUNDRED / TOUSAND**.
- **Time**: normally just the **minutes** (add the hour if confusion is possible); **time checks to the nearest half minute**; **UTC**; **2400** = end of day, **0000** = beginning.

### Standard words (p.525–528)
- **ACKNOWLEDGE** (let me know you got and understood it); **AFFIRM** (yes); **APPROVED**; **BREAK** (separates parts of a message); **BREAK BREAK** (separates messages to different aircraft when busy); **CANCEL** (annul the clearance); **CHECK** (examine a system — no answer expected); **CLEARED**; **CONFIRM** (verify); **CONTACT**; **CORRECT**; **CORRECTION**; **DISREGARD**; **HOLD SHORT**; **HOW DO YOU READ**; **I SAY AGAIN**; **MAINTAIN**; **MONITOR** (listen out on); **NEGATIVE** (no / not granted / not correct / not capable); **NEGATIVE I SAY AGAIN** (wrong read-back); **OVER / OUT** (not normally used on VHF); **READ BACK**; **RECLEARED**; **REPORT**; **REQUEST**; **ROGER** (received all of your last transmission — **never** as an answer to a read-back or a yes/no question); **SAY AGAIN**; **SPEAK SLOWER**; **STANDBY** (wait, I will call you — neither approval nor denial); **UNABLE**; **VERIFY**; **WILCO** (understood and will comply); **WORDS TWICE**. "**GO AHEAD**" has been deleted: answer with call signs instead.

### Station call signs (p.528)
- ACC **CONTROL** · approach **APPROACH** · approach/area radar **APPROACH RADAR / CONTROL RADAR** · tower **TOWER** · surface movement **GROUND** · radar **RADAR** · PAR **PRECISION** · DF station **HOMER** · FIS **INFORMATION** · clearance delivery **DELIVERY** · apron **APRON** · company **DISPATCH** · aeronautical station **RADIO** · flow **FLOW**.

### Read-back, frequency change, tests (p.528–529)
- Read back: **route clearances**; runway **enter, land, take off, hold short, cross, taxi and backtrack**; **runway in use, altimeter settings, SSR codes, level, heading and speed instructions, transition levels**. **QNH always.** Other items: acknowledge by call sign or abbreviated read-back.
- Change frequency when told; otherwise tell the station first; in controlled airspace get **permission** first.
- Test transmissions **≤ 10 seconds**. **Readability**: **1 unreadable · 2 readable now and then · 3 readable with difficulty · 4 readable · 5 perfectly readable**.

### Aircraft call signs (p.529)
- Full: (a) registration **VTEJP** (or Cessna VTEJP); (b) operator designator + **last four** of registration (**AIRINDIA TEJP**); (c) operator designator + flight number (**AIRINDIA 809**).
- Abbreviated (only after contact is established and if no confusion): (a) **first character + at least last two** (**VJP**); (b) operator + **last two** (AIRINDIA JP); (c) flight-number call signs **cannot be abbreviated**.

### Radar phraseology (p.529–530)
- Identification is not itself a service; you're told when identified and your position, and warned if identity is lost. Read back SSR settings. Told the type of radar service where not obvious.
- Vectors for separation (reasons may be given); "**resume own navigation**" when done; **orbits** for delay or spacing.
- **Traffic information**: **clock bearing** (or cardinal points if turning), **distance**, **direction of flight**, speed/type/level; relative movement terms (closing, converging, parallel, same/opposite direction, diverging, overtaking, crossing left to right / right to left). **Avoiding action** when collision risk is imminent.

### Weather (p.530–531)
- METAR (routine), SPECI (special), TREND (**next 2 hours**), TAF, ROFOR, SIGMET, AIRMET.
- Cloud: **FEW 1–2 oktas, SCT 3–4, BKN 5–7, OVC 8**.
- **CAVOK**: visibility **≥ 10 km**, no cloud below **5,000 ft** or the highest MSA (greater), **no CB**, no significant weather (precipitation, TS, shallow fog, low drifting snow).
- RVR said as "**RVR**", e.g. "RVR runway 27, 800"; several IRVR readings may be passed.

### India: channels and broadcasts (p.531–532)
- Air–ground by **HF, VHF, UHF** and **RCAG** (remote VHF/UHF); **ADS-CPDLC** at **Mumbai, Delhi, Kolkata, Chennai**.
- **ATIS** at almost all airports (continuous, repetitive); D-ATIS by pre-FANS data link.
- **VOLMET** on **HF** from **Mumbai and Kolkata**: aerodrome, wind, visibility, RVR, weather, cloud, temperature, QNH, trend. D-VOLMET by data link.

### Distress (p.532)
- Stay on the frequency where distress traffic began unless moving helps. **121.5 / 243 MHz** VHF/UHF distress; **2182 kHz** (MF) international distress; **500 kHz** maritime distress. SSR **7700 / 7600 / 7500**. **ELTs on 121.5, 243 and 406 MHz**.

### IFR communications and reports (p.532–535)
- IFR keeps two-way contact in **all classes** of airspace; outside controlled airspace a listening watch with the FIS unit.
- **HEAVY** after the call sign on first contact with each unit; **SUPER** for the A380.
- Climb/descent to heights or altitudes in whole numbers, first message includes **QFE/QNH**; flight levels digit by digit.
- **Position report**: identification, **position, time, level, next position and time, ensuing significant point**. Met information once per FIR at designated points: temperature, wind, turbulence, icing, humidity.
- **Special air-report (AIRREP SPECIAL)**: section 1 identification, position, time, level, next position, ETA; section 2 endurance; section 3 the phenomenon: **moderate/severe turbulence, moderate/severe icing, severe mountain wave, TS with/without hail, heavy dust/sand storm, volcanic ash, pre-eruption volcanic activity**. Braking action terms: **GOOD, GOOD TO MEDIUM, MEDIUM, MEDIUM TO POOR, POOR, LESS THAN POOR**.

### Radio waves (p.535–538)
- **λ = c / f** (c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s): longer wavelength, lower frequency. Polarisation = direction of the electric component. Polar diagram drawn to the **50%** signal-strength point. Modulation: **amplitude, frequency, pulse, phase**. Waves are refracted, reflected, diffracted and attenuated; bend towards the denser medium.
- Bands: **VLF 3–30 kHz** (myriametric) · **LF 30–300 kHz** · **MF 300 kHz–3 MHz** · **HF 3–30 MHz** (decametric) · **VHF 30–300 MHz** (metric) · **UHF 300 MHz–3 GHz** · **SHF 3–30 GHz** (centimetric) · **EHF 30–300 GHz**.
- Equipment: ADF **190–1750 kHz** (LF/MF); HF comms **2–25 MHz**; markers **75 MHz**; LOC **108.1–111.95**; VOR **108.0–117.95**; VHF comms **118–137 MHz**; glidepath **329.15–335 MHz** (UHF); DME **960–1213 MHz**; SSR **1030/1090 MHz**; GPS **1575.42 (L1)**, 1227.6 (L2); radio altimeter **4.2–4.4 GHz**; weather radar **9375 MHz** (SHF); MLS **5031–5091 MHz**.
- **Ground wave** (direct or ground-reflected) and **sky wave** (refracted by the ionosphere). VLF/LF long ground-wave range; MF/HF sky wave; **VHF and above line of sight**. Range ∝ √power (double range needs **4×** power). **Line of sight range (NM) = 1.25 (√h₁ + √h₂)**, heights in feet.
- **SATCOM** (voice + data) beyond VHF/radar; **SATVOICE** recognised by ICAO as a long-range system besides HF.
- **RCP** n = seconds for an instruction and acknowledgement round trip. **CPDLC**: two-way data link for **non-time-critical** strategic messages, supplementing voice; chosen beyond VHF range. **ADS-C/CPDLC** (FANS-1A) at Chennai, Mumbai, Delhi, Kolkata; **log on 10 minutes before** entering the FIR.

### Exam traps
- QDM = heading **to** the station; QDR = magnetic bearing **from** it; QTE = **true** bearing from it.
- Priority: **distress, urgency, DF, flight safety, met, regularity**.
- **ROGER** never answers a read-back or yes/no question; **GO AHEAD** deleted; OVER/OUT not used on VHF.
- Flight-number call signs **can't be abbreviated**.
- Readability **1–5** (3 = with difficulty). Test ≤ **10 s**.
- **121.5/243 MHz**, **2182 kHz**, **500 kHz**; ELT **406 MHz**.
- VHF comms **118–137 MHz**; SSR **1030 up, 1090 down**; LOS **1.25(√h₁+√h₂)**.
- HEAVY on first contact; SUPER for A380. CPDLC log-on **10 min** before the FIR.

## 20. Aircraft Accident and Incident

### Annex 13 (p.555)
- Investigations find causes to **prevent recurrence** (not to blame). States that may take part: **Occurrence, Registry, Operator, Design, Manufacture**. The **State of Occurrence** institutes the investigation. State of the Operator provisions apply when the aircraft is leased/chartered/interchanged and that State does the State of Registry's job.
- Any State providing information, facilities or experts on request may appoint an **accredited representative**. The **final report** goes to the States concerned and **ICAO** in an ICAO working language. For aircraft over **100,000 kg**, the States of Design and Manufacture name a representative on request.

### Definitions (p.555–558)
- **Accident** (manned): from boarding with intention of flight until all have disembarked (unmanned: ready to move for flight until it stops and propulsion shuts down), in which: (a) a person is **fatally or seriously injured** by being in the aircraft, by direct contact with any part (incl. detached parts) or by **jet blast** — not natural causes, self-inflicted, inflicted by others, or stowaways outside normal areas; (b) **damage or structural failure** affecting strength, performance or handling — excluding damage limited to a **single engine** (cowlings, accessories), propellers, wing tips, antennas, probes, vanes, tyres, brakes, wheels, fairings, panels, gear doors, windscreens, small dents/holes, minor rotor/gear damage, and **hail or bird strike** damage (incl. radome holes); (c) aircraft **missing** or inaccessible. **Fatal** = death within **30 days**. **Missing** = wreckage not found when the official search ends.
- **Serious incident**: high probability of an accident. Examples: near collisions needing avoidance; collisions not classed as accidents; CFIT only marginally avoided; take-offs or rejected take-offs and landings on closed/engaged/unassigned runways or taxiways; gross performance shortfalls at take-off/initial climb; **fire or smoke** anywhere (even if put out) or engine fire; **emergency use of oxygen by the flight crew**; structural failures or uncontained engine failures not classed as accidents; multiple system malfunctions; **flight crew incapacitation**; fuel emergencies; **runway incursions of severity A**; undershoots, overruns, runway excursions; control difficulties; failure of more than one redundant nav/guidance system; release of slung or external loads.
- **Serious injury**: hospital **> 48 hours** starting within **7 days**; **fracture** (except simple fingers, toes, nose); lacerations with severe bleeding or nerve/muscle/tendon damage; **internal organ** injury; **2nd or 3rd degree burns** or burns over **5%** of the body; verified exposure to infectious substances or harmful radiation.
- **Incident** (India): damage or injury short of an accident; forced landing; landing unairworthy; return to departure without completing the flight; precautionary landing; **position unknown** for any period; safety jeopardised in any way. **Significant incident**: an accident or serious incident could have happened had the risk not been managed within margins.
- **Air traffic incidents**: **AIRPROX** and serious difficulty from faulty procedures, non-compliance or ground facility failure.

### Reporting and investigation in India (p.558–560)
- In flight, report a major incident on the air–ground frequency (identification, type, the incident). After landing, **within 24 hours**, file the **Air Traffic Incident Report** to **DGCA** (Director Air Safety) with a copy to **AAI ED (ATM)**.
- **DG, AAIB** institutes and conducts investigations of accidents/incidents **in India regardless of registration**.
- **Notification**: the **PIC** (or owner/operator/hirer if the PIC is killed or incapacitated) notifies **AAIB and DGCA** by the quickest means **within 24 hours**, and for occurrences in India the **District Magistrate** and the **nearest police station**. Also notified by the aerodrome operator, the ATS in-charge and DGCA.
- **Evidence**: the Investigator-in-Charge has unhampered access to and control of wreckage, recorders and ATS records. Nothing removed or disturbed except by AAIB authority, or to **rescue people/animals**, prevent **fire** or other destruction, clear obstruction to the public, navigation or transport, or move wreckage on **water** to safety; goods under AAIB supervision; baggage and mail after photographing/weighing under police, magistrate, postal or AAIB supervision.
- **CVR and image recordings** may not be used for disciplinary, civil, administrative or criminal proceedings.

### Exam traps
- Accident window: **boarding → disembarking**. Fatal = within **30 days**. Serious injury = hospital **> 48 h** within **7 days**; burns **> 5%**.
- Damage limited to one engine, a tyre, or from **bird strike/hail** is **not** an accident.
- Crew using **emergency oxygen**, any **fire**, crew **incapacitation** = serious incidents.
- Notify **AAIB + DGCA within 24 h**; DM and police in India. Investigation by **State of Occurrence** (India: **AAIB**).

## 21. Facilitation

### Definitions (p.565–566)
- **Cargo**: property other than mail, stores and accompanied or mishandled baggage. **Deportee**: lawfully or unlawfully in a State, then formally ordered to leave. **Direct transit area**: special area where passengers stay during transit without entering the State. **Disembarkation/embarkation** exclude through passengers and crew of the same through-flight. **International airport**: designated for international traffic with customs, immigration, health, quarantine. **Lading/unlading**: putting on/taking off cargo, mail, baggage, stores.

### Annex 9 (p.566–568)
- Facilitates landside clearance (customs, immigration, health, agriculture); reduce paperwork, standardise documents, avoid delays (costly to everyone). ICAO was the first international body with binding facilitation Standards.
- States clear aircraft without unnecessary delay (allowing for security and narcotics control).
- **No documents beyond Annex 9**; **no visa or fee** for aircraft documents; accept **electronic** or paper forms; if received electronically, **no paper copy**. **General Declaration** limited to the Annex 9 format.
- **Not normally required**: passenger manifest; written declaration of **stores remaining on board**; list of accompanied or mishandled **baggage**; mail declaration beyond UPU forms. **Cargo manifest** in paper: the Annex 9 form. No more than **3 copies** of any document. Documents not needed if nothing is (dis)embarked or (un)laden, with a note in the General Declaration.
- Crew: speed up inspection; facilitate **Crew Member Certificates (CMC)** (ID card, separate from the licence); crew may check and correct CMC data **free**; secure records and anti-fraud controls. **Visa waived** for crew with a CMC arriving **on duty** to join their next flight (recommended also when arriving otherwise). **Technical personnel** urgently needed to make a stranded aircraft airworthy get temporary entry without delay.
- **Biometric** e-passports (Doc 9303 MRTD); **Automated Border Control** using the ICAO **PKD** and INTERPOL **SLTD** database.

### India (p.568–572)
- First landing and final departure at an **international aerodrome** (or notified customs aerodrome). International flights may **not carry domestic traffic** (pick up in one Indian city, drop in another).
- Documents: **customs** — General Declaration + cargo manifest; **immigration** — General Declaration + passenger manifest; **health** — General Declaration. No departure without immigration and customs clearance on the General Declaration.
- **No customs duty** on a foreign-registered aircraft brought in for a flight to/across India, not to be registered here, leaving within **6 months**, with a written declaration on arrival.
- Customs: **green channel** (nothing dutiable) and **red channel**; crew declare currency, gold, electronics on arrival and departure; transit passengers stay in the customs area; export goods presented at least **4 working hours** before departure; export certificates for valuables.

### Exam traps
- Passenger manifest **not normally** required; stores and baggage lists **never**; max **3 copies**; no visa/fee for aircraft documents.
- Crew visa waived with a **CMC** when on duty.
- India: customs GD + cargo manifest; immigration GD + passenger manifest; health GD. Duty-free stay **6 months**. Export cargo **4 working hours** early.

## 22. Security

### Annex 17 (p.575–577)
- Born from the violent crimes of the late 1960s: **Extraordinary ICAO Assembly, June 1970**. Coordinates security programmes; **operators** have primary responsibility for protecting their passengers and assets, compatible with airport programmes.
- Before **1985** the focus was **hijacking**; later amendments added sabotage, baggage reconciliation, items left behind, couriers, cargo and mail, domestic applicability, threat sharing, quality control, access control, in-flight security officers, cockpit protection, code-sharing, human factors, response management.
- Each State sets up an organisation and rules that protect passengers, crew, ground staff and the public and can **respond rapidly** to increased threats.
- An aircraft subjected to **unlawful seizure** that lands is **retained** unless leaving is needed to protect lives. The landing State notifies **the State of Registry, State of the Operator, States with dead/injured/hostage/on-board citizens, and ICAO**. States assist a seized aircraft with **nav aids, ATS and permission to land**.
- **Potentially disruptive passengers** board **before** other passengers. Screened passengers who mix with unscreened persons after screening are **re-screened** (with cabin bags).
- Armed foreign officers on board: only with prior notification by the State of embarkation to the destination State and notification of the **PIC**.

### India (p.577–578)
- **BCAS** (Bureau of Civil Aviation Security), headed by a **Director General**, is the regulator; **Aircraft (Security) Rules 2011**. The **Commissioner of Security (Civil Aviation)**, BCAS, MoCA, is the appropriate authority for Annex 17 and runs the **National Civil Aviation Security Programme**.
- AAI's **Directorate of Security** for AAI airports (Airport Directors locally); private/JV airports (DIAL, MIAL, CIAL, HIAL, BIAL) have their own set-ups.
- Security accidents/incidents reported **immediately** to the Commissioner by operators, ASG, aerodrome operators, regulated agents and caterers. The Commissioner may order an inquiry by an officer **not below Assistant Commissioner of Security**.

### Exam traps
- Annex 17 = security; Extraordinary Assembly **1970**; hijacking focus until **1985**.
- Seized aircraft **retained** on landing unless lives are at stake; notify Registry, Operator, affected States and **ICAO**.
- Disruptive passengers board **first**; re-screen after mixing.
- India: **BCAS**; Commissioner of Security (CA); NCASP; Aircraft (Security) Rules **2011**.

## 23. Human Performance and Limitations

### Why it matters (p.583)
- Part 2 of the book meets the ICAO **human factors knowledge** requirement for flight crew (also useful to cabin crew, ATCOs and engineers).
- The human is the **most flexible, adaptable and valuable** part of the system, and also the **most vulnerable**.
- About **three out of four** accidents come from less-than-optimum human performance ("human error"). Human error is inherent to human cognition and generally inescapable; understanding limitations improves safety.

### The disciplines (p.583–585)
- Human Factors is **multidisciplinary** and **problem-oriented, not discipline-centred**.
- **Psychology**: mind and behaviour (perception, learning, information processing, errors) → displays, controls, training, selection.
- **Engineering**: properties of matter and energy → design of displays, controls, systems, simulators.
- **Physiology**: how the healthy body works → environmental systems, effects of heat, cold, hypoxia.
- **Medicine**: preventing and curing disease and injury → toxicology of smoke, impact protection.
- **Sociology**: human groups → crew composition, passenger behaviour in emergencies, crew selection.
- **Anthropometry**: body sizes and muscle strength (with biomechanics) → seat adjustment, reach, door sizes.
- **Chronobiology** (part of biology): body rhythms and sleep → night flying, time-zone changes. **Statistics** for analysing surveys.

### The professional pilot (p.585)
- Situation awareness, flexibility, task management, communication, threat and error management, responsibility, handling skills, theory, motivation, fitness, reliability, balanced personality, teamwork, stress management, quick reflexes, risk assessment, decision making, CRM.

### Accidents (p.585–586)
- Commercial accident rate (excluding sabotage/terrorism): about **1 per million airport movements**.
- Human factors cited as a major cause in **over 73%** of accidents, unchanged since the **1950s**.
- Most accidents are linked to **deficiencies in human performance**: poor lookout, SA, decision making, task organisation, communication, failing to recognise threats, errors.
- **CFIT** is the most common form of air accident.
- Flight-time split: take-off and initial climb about **2%**, climb–cruise–descent about **83%**, approach and landing about **15%** of flight time; yet **approach and landing** has the largest share of accidents.

### The body in flight (p.586)
- Physiologically significant changes: big changes in **barometric pressure**, **temperature**, and **high-speed movement in three dimensions**.
- The body adapts to oxygen shortage by faster breathing, blood chemistry changes and more red blood cells; a **complete absence of oxygen kills in about 5–8 minutes**.

### Exam traps
- Human factors in about **73%** of accidents; **three out of four** accidents from human performance.
- Accident rate **1 per million** movements; most common type **CFIT**.
- Most accidents: **approach and landing**, although it is only about 15% of flight time.
- No oxygen at all: death in **5–8 minutes**.

## 24. CRM, TEM and LOFT

### CRM (p.589–590)
- **CRM**: effective use of **all available resources** (people, procedures, equipment, oneself) to make operations safe and efficient, reducing error, avoiding stress, increasing efficiency.
- About **cognitive** (situation awareness, problem solving, decisions) and **interpersonal** (communication, teamwork) skills, not technical skill. Applies to **single-pilot** operations too.
- An application of Human Factors; aim of CRM training: **incident and accident prevention**. Human-performance training carries the **same weight** as systems and normal/abnormal/emergency procedures; given mainly through **CRM training and LOFT**. Not providing it = non-compliance with an **ICAO Standard**.
- The **1977 Tenerife** disaster turned CRM into a full programme. Now also for ATCOs and engineers.

### SHEL model (p.590–596)
- Developed by **Edwards, 1972**; described in ICAO **Doc 9859** (SMM) and Circular 216.
- **S**oftware: procedures, manuals, checklists, symbology, computer programs, maps, charts. **H**ardware: flight-deck design, instruments, controls. **E**nvironment: inside and outside the workplace. **L**iveware: the human, the **most critical, valuable and flexible** part. The second **L**: other people.
- **Liveware is the hub**; other components are matched to it. A **mismatch at an interface** is as important as the blocks themselves and is a source of error.
- Interfaces: **L–H** human–machine; **L–S** human–materials/systems; **L–L** human–colleagues; **L–E** human–environment.
- Factors in the individual: **physical**, **physiological**, **psychological**, **psychosocial**; physiological and psychosocial effects are most pronounced.
- L–H example: **GPWS** in the **1980s** cut hull-loss rates. L–S includes manuals, NOTAMs, SOPs, computers, automation. L–E: internal (heat, noise, vibration, ozone, radiation) and external (weather, terrain, traffic), infrastructure, maintenance.

### Making CRM work (p.597–598)
- The PIC keeps full authority but must accept that questions are not a threat. The old "gear up and shut up" role ended badly. **No one person has the full picture.**
- Foundations: errors are **normal** and must be managed; a **non-punitive** response to error; airlines that tolerate error equip crews better.
- Effective programmes: **operational relevance** (no classroom games or personality tests), **own experience** (own incidents), **threat assessment**, **error management** (effective and ineffective countermeasures).
- Aims: **error avoidance, threat management, error management, undesired aircraft state management**.

### CRM skills (p.598–600)
- Leadership/command, decision making, communication, situation awareness, team building, workload management, vigilance, automation management, human performance, briefings, setting bottom lines, contingency management, evaluation of plans, assertiveness.
- **Trans-cockpit authority gradient**: domineering captain + unassertive F/O = **steep**; two captains = **shallow**. Desired: captain's authority recognised with good relationships; balance of task- and relationship-oriented behaviour.

### TEM (p.600–602)
- CRM manages **resources**; TEM manages **threats**. TEM accepts that threats and errors **will** occur; it manages rather than eliminates them. Countermeasures **avoid, trap and mitigate** errors.
- **Threats** are external, outside the crew's influence, but must be managed: **environmental** (weather, terrain, airport, traffic/TCAS), **ATC** (errors, language, similar call signs), **aircraft** (malfunctions, automation), **crew support** (dispatch, ground, maintenance), **operational** (time pressure, diversions, missed approaches), **cabin** (attendant errors, passengers).
- **Error**: crew action or inaction leading to deviation from intentions or expectations. Five categories: **intentional non-compliance** (wilful), **procedural** (right intention, flawed execution, includes forgetting), **communication**, **proficiency** (lack of knowledge or stick-and-rudder skill), **operational decision** (more conservative option ignored, decision not verbalised, or time not used; e.g. flying into known wind shear).
- Unmanaged errors may lead to an **undesired aircraft state**: wrong configuration, unstable approach, vertical/lateral/speed deviation.

### LOFT (p.602–603)
- **Non-jeopardy**, facilitated, **full-mission** simulation representative of line operations; emphasis on communication, management, leadership. Scenarios often from accident and incident reports.
- **Not** a check of individuals; it validates training and procedures. Extra training afterwards is given at once, with no stigma.

### Exam traps
- SHEL: **Edwards 1972**; **Liveware at the centre**; errors arise at the **interfaces**.
- Tenerife **1977** gave birth to CRM programmes.
- Threats are **external**; errors are the crew's. Five error types; wilful = **intentional non-compliance**.
- Steep gradient = dominant captain; shallow = two captains.
- LOFT is **non-jeopardy**: no pass/fail. CRM and LOFT improve the quality of **crew** performance.
- Automation **relocates** the potential for error, generally **increases** workload and creates **new forms of error**. **GPWS** (1980s) cut hull losses.
- Checklist error = **L–S**; cramped cockpit = **L–H**; noise or jet lag = **L–E**.

## 25. Aviation Psychology

### Aim and human error (p.609)
- Applied psychology of behaviour, emotions and mental states in aviation; aim: to **understand and predict** behaviour, improving safety, efficiency and comfort.
- Human errors are **inevitable**; most pilot errors are failures of **interpersonal skills, communication, decision making and leadership**. Frequency can still be reduced and effects mitigated. "A superior pilot uses superior judgment to avoid situations that need superior skill."

### Workload (p.610)
- Workload = mental effort needed to process information. High workload → more errors, fatigue, poorer performance.
- Four task factors: **difficulty**, tasks **in parallel**, tasks **in series** (switching), **time available**. Indirect: duration, fatigue, arousal.
- Symptoms of rising workload: narrowed focus, **task shedding**, loss of SA, decision **short cuts**, fatigue and errors.
- Acceptable workload uses about **60%** of crew resources. Overload symptoms: sharp drop in performance, **funnelling** of attention, regression, mental **blocking**, mood swings, restlessness and trembling, panic. **Thorough flight planning** is the key to reducing cockpit workload.

### Information processing (p.610–612)
- Functional model: **detection → perception → decision making → action → feedback**. The **central decision maker** draws on short- and long-term memory; a problem at any stage affects the outcome. The first stage is **sensory stimulation**.
- Three memories: **sensory**, **short-term (working)**, **long-term**. Sensory stores are separate for each sense and **decay rapidly**. **Iconic** (visual) memory lasts **0.5–1 s**; **70–80%** of information arrives through vision.
- **Habituation**: senses adapt to a constant stimulus.
- **Perception** converts sensations into a meaningful structure. Expected information is perceived more easily; we perceive only what we can conceive, and only a fraction of what reaches the senses. **Funnelled perception**: the same scene seen differently depending on the observer's starting point.
- The human information processing system beats computers because of its **flexibility**.

### Attention and vigilance (p.613–614)
- Attention is **limited**; it moves quickly but handles **one item at a time**, so prioritise. The main danger is **poor management of attention**.
- **Selective attention**: continually sampling inputs for relevance. **Divided attention**: time-sharing several mental activities at almost the same time. The brain has **different reservoirs** of resources for gathering, processing and action; sharing resources between two tasks **reduces performance** on each.
- An attention mechanism is needed because **STM holds few items** and **channel capacity is limited**.
- **Cocktail party effect**: a meaningful word (your name) breaks through from an unattended conversation: picking up relevant information unintentionally.
- **Vigilance**: the capability to stay alert above a threshold for a period. **Hypo-vigilance**: from monotony, tiredness, need for sleep, lack of stimulation; signs are **decreased sensory perception and muscular heaviness**; risk of **micro-sleep**; manage with healthy food and **rest periods during the flight**. **Hyper-vigilance**: acute, agitated **panic** with indiscriminate attention to every cue; poor decisions. Optimum lies between.
- **Complacency**: self-satisfaction plus unawareness of danger; automation can make pilots complacent.

### Skills and reflexes (p.614–615)
- **Motor programmes** (skills) are learnt sub-routines held in **long-term memory**, run **without conscious thought** (e.g. a coordinated turn is **skill-based** behaviour).
- Early training: competent to fly but not confident. Skilled = practises regularly, manages self, keeps **resources in reserve**.
- **Anderson model**: **cognitive → associative → automatic (autonomous)**.
- Reflexes involve little central nervous system. Reaction time = delay between stimulus and muscle response. Reflexes: **unconditioned** (blinking), **conditioned** (learned), **trained** (improved by practice).
- **Startle**: blink and body jerk from sudden noise, touch, falling sensation or visual shock; skilled tasks recover within **5–10 seconds**. Fear potentiates startle and can trigger fight or flight.

### Reliability and memory (p.616–617)
- Simple repetitive tasks: error rate **1 in 100**, after practice **1 in 1,000**. Errors are **cumulative**. Human reliability = functioning as one is supposed to.
- **STM**: **10–20 seconds** without rehearsal; capacity **7 ± 2** unrelated items. Sounds are easier to rehearse than images. Improve with **chunking** and **association**.
- **LTM**: **unlimited** storage time, but **retrieval** problems. **Semantic** (general knowledge), **episodic** (events in your life), **procedural** (skills).
- Learning: insight, imitation, experience; **skill learning** needs motivation, attention, observation, practice, corrective feedback. Retention helped by **mnemonics** and memory training.
- **Motivation**: intrinsic (satisfaction, achievement) or extrinsic (rewards, punishment, goals).

### Response and cognition (p.618)
- **Auditory** stimuli attract attention more than visual but are more often answered **in error**. Age **20 → 60**: responses **slower but more accurate**.
- Under pressure an unexpected stimulus tends to produce an **error of commission**.
- **Hallucination**: false perception, a distortion of real sensory stimuli.

### Decisions, SA and communication (p.619–621)
- Decisions are shaped by experience, probability and whether information matches expectation. Discuss problems beforehand; an inexperienced pilot who sees danger should **question it at once**.
- **Risk** = probability × impact.
- Once a pilot has an apparently correct explanation, the chance of ignoring information that does not fit **increases** (confirmation bias). Thinking ahead about abnormal situations is **good** (consistent action).
- SA spoilers: **stress, boredom, fatigue, emotional disturbance, poor communication, interruptions**. Don't see what you want to see.
- Decision steps: recognise the problem → consider its nature → analyse → list solutions → select → execute → follow up and communicate → feedback.
- Barriers to teamwork: **aggressiveness, arrogance, anti-authority, impulsiveness, invulnerability, resignation**. Don't hear what you expect: **actively listen**.

### Automation (p.621–623)
- Automation makes the crew more **peripheral**; pilots now manage hardware and software interfaces. It creates **new forms of error** and **relocates** error rather than removing it, often putting the person in a **more demanding** role.
- Pilots, computers and machines are **complementary**, not alternatives. Automation has improved safety overall.
- Manual workload falls, but mental workload does not fall as much and may rise, especially at busy terminals; risk of being **out of the loop**. Highly reliable automation breeds **complacency**; expertise is lost and a failed machine may leave the pilot unable to cope.
- Mismatch categories studied include SA, automation **complacency**, automation **intimidation**, captain's authority, interface design, selection, training and procedures, the pilot's role.
- Human-centred automation: accountable, subordinate, predictable, adaptable, comprehensible, flexible, dependable, informative, error-resistant, error-tolerant.

### People and design (p.624–626)
- Personality axes: **stable–neurotic** and **introvert–extrovert**. Extroverts impulsive and sociable; introverts withdrawn and cautious. High neuroticism = **trait anxiety** (vs transient **state anxiety**).
- **Culture** shapes whether speaking up is polite or disruptive; matters most for communication, leadership, teamwork.
- **Murphy's law**: if equipment can be operated wrongly, sooner or later it will be. The most important requirement for displays and controls is **standardisation**.
- **Anthropometry**: static, dynamic and contour measurements. Design for the **central 90%** (ignore lowest and highest **5%**).
- **Design eye position** (eye datum, reference eye point): too high → poor instrument view; too low → poor forward and downward view. Seats adjustable; **5-point harness with negative-g strap**; use the same position in all phases.
- **Digital** displays best for **quantitative** information; **analogue** for **qualitative** (and orientation, e.g. compass card). **Standard T**: attitude indicator in the centre, ASI, altimeter and DI around it. The **three-pointer altimeter** is easily misread.
- Controls: standardisation, frequency of use, sequence of use, importance, visual/tactile **dissimilarity**, symbolism, simultaneous use. Warnings **attention-getting without startling**. Cockpit lighting avoids harsh shadows and glare.

### Stress (p.627–629)
- Response to a stressor differs between people. **Moderate stress may improve performance**; stress boosts **physical strength** rather than mental performance. **Arousal** = readiness to respond to a stressor, shaped by personality, training and experience.
- Stress arises e.g. with **inexperienced pilots when demands exceed their capabilities**, or when a pilot believes no solution exists; also hunger, thirst, pain, lack of sleep. Stressors are **cumulative**.
- Manage: **recognise, accept, develop a coping strategy**; **know your limits and fly well inside them**; good cockpit housekeeping; quality headsets; stay on the ground if not up to it.
- Life-event scores: spouse death **100**, divorce **73**, separation 65, family death 63, injury 53, job loss 47, retirement 45, pregnancy 40, child leaving 29, moving 20, debt 17, vacation 13, minor law violation 11. Totals: **< 60 free**, 60–80 normal, 80–100 high, **> 100 serious**.
- Signs: mental blocks, confusion, channelised attention, resignation, frustration, rage, **poorer motor coordination**, **high-pitched fast speech**. Visible: **perspiration, flushed skin, dilated pupils, fast breathing**.

### Fatigue and arousal (p.629–630)
- Tiredness: fixed by a good night's sleep. **Fatigue**: very deep tiredness from the **cumulative** effects of a stressful life or environment; insidious and often denied.
- Causes: long ill health, regular sleep loss, prolonged stress, **disturbed body cycle** (jet lag, shifts), relationship problems, stressful environment. Symptoms: memory lapses, mistakes in speech and action, mood swings, reduced awareness, poor coordination.
- Self-help: accept it, eat well, reduce stress, regular sleep, avoid alcohol and caffeine, see a doctor.
- **Arousal** (inverted U): low arousal (cruise) → attention wanders; optimum → central decision maker most efficient; **high-arousal overload → attention narrows**. Optimum is **lower for difficult cognitive tasks**, higher for endurance tasks. Too little = inert, too much = hyper.

### Circadian rhythm and jet lag (p.631–633)
- Biological clock in the **hypothalamus**; governs sleep, body temperature, appetite, hormones. Circadian lows degrade performance.
- Typical day: **lowest body temperature ≈ 04:30**, deepest sleep ≈ 02:00, melatonin stops ≈ 07:30 and starts ≈ 21:00, high alertness ≈ 10:00, best coordination ≈ 14:30, fastest reactions ≈ 15:30, greatest cardiovascular efficiency and muscle strength ≈ 17:00, highest blood pressure ≈ 18:30, **highest body temperature ≈ 19:00**.
- **Melatonin** (pineal gland, "light of night") regulates **sleep**; rises after dark, peaks **02:00–04:00**; the body's **Zeitgeber**.
- Free-running clock (no time cues) ≈ **25 h**; normally reset to ≈ 24 h.
- Stop-over **> 24 h**: move to the new time as soon as possible. Crossing **> 3–4 zones** with a layover **> 24 h** (book's advice): keep to the **departure** country's rhythm as long as possible and keep regular patterns. Short layover (e.g. 22 h): **home time** (IST) stays relevant.
- Re-synchronisation ≈ **1–1.5 h per day**. **Eastbound ≈ 1.5 days per zone**, **westbound ≈ 1 day per zone**: going **east is harder** (about 50% slower).
- **Sensorimotor** performance best in the **evening**; **intellectual** best in the **morning**. Sleep length depends mainly on **where in the circadian cycle** you go to sleep.

### Sleep (p.633–634)
- **NREM** restores the **body**; **REM** restores the **brain** (memory). NREM stage 1: transition (~10 min), easily woken. **Stage 2**: light, first true sleep, 10–25 min, **≈ 50%** of sleep. Stage 3: beginning of deep (delta) sleep. **Stage 4: deep sleep**, no eye or muscle movement; woken → groggy.
- **REM (paradoxical)**: first after **70–90 min**; eyes move side to side; brain waves like waking; **dreams**. REM share falls from **50%** (infants) to **20%** (adults). Each cycle has **more REM**; REM is the most important stage for regeneration.
- **Deep sleep**: physical recovery and **neuron energy reserves**. **Sleep inertia**: impaired performance after waking; up to **20 min** after a nap.
- Sleep loss: fatigue, poor concentration and attention, **sensory illusions, mood disorders**; worse with **altitude** and **workload**; the sufferer rarely notices the decline.

### Mental health (p.634–635)
- **Germanwings 9525**, 24 March 2015, A320, French Alps, 150 killed: deliberate crash by a co-pilot who hid his unfitness.
- Fear of losing the licence stops pilots seeking help; reduce **stigma**. Serious disorders (psychosis) are rare and hard to predict; prevention should target **depression, anxiety and substance misuse**.

### Exam traps
- STM **10–20 s**, **7 ± 2**; iconic memory **0.5–1 s**; LTM unlimited with retrieval problems.
- Anderson: **cognitive, associative, autonomous**. Motor programmes live in **LTM**.
- Divided attention = several mental activities at almost the same time. Cocktail party = picking up relevant information unintentionally.
- Hypo-vigilance: monotony, tiredness, sleep need, lack of stimulation; fix with rest periods. Hyper-vigilance = **panic**.
- High arousal **narrows attention**; optimum lower for cognitive tasks.
- Error rate **1/100 → 1/1,000**. Startle recovery **5–10 s**. Acceptable workload ≈ **60%**.
- Design for **90%** (5th–95th). Most important design requirement: **standardisation**.
- Stress-free score **< 60**. Stress boosts **physical strength**. Visible signs include **fast** breathing and dilated pupils.
- Lowest body temperature **04:30**; free-running clock **25 h**; resync **1–1.5 h/day**; **east is harder**.
- Stage 2 ≈ **50%** of sleep; stage 4 = deep sleep; nap inertia up to **20 min**.

## 26. Aviation Physiology and Human Factors

### Atmosphere and gas laws (p.647–650)
- Big physiological changes in flight: **barometric pressure**, **temperature**, **3-D high-speed motion**.
- Air: **nitrogen 78%**, **oxygen 21%** (20.95), argon 0.93%, CO₂ ≈ 0.03–0.04%; water vapour 1–4%. Proportions are **constant** in troposphere and stratosphere. Troposphere (≈ 13 km average) holds **¾ of the mass**.
- **Boyle**: at constant temperature, volume ∝ 1/pressure. Gas expands on climb → **trapped gas** (ears, sinuses, gut, teeth).
- **Henry**: gas dissolved ∝ partial pressure above the liquid → **decompression sickness** (soda bottle).
- **Charles**: at constant volume, pressure ∝ temperature (temperature changes in rapid decompression, oxygen cylinders).
- **Graham**: gases diffuse from high to low concentration → **gas exchange** (alveoli → blood → tissues).
- **Dalton**: total pressure = sum of partial pressures. O₂ stays **21%** at all heights, but its **partial pressure** falls → **hypoxia**.

### Pressure, oxygen and zones (p.650–652)
- ISA: 1013.25 hPa = 760 mmHg = 29.92 inHg = 14.7 psi; temperature falls **1.98 °C/1,000 ft** to **36,090 ft**, then **−56.5 °C**.
- Oxygen available vs sea level: **8,000 ft 76%** (574 mmHg) · 12,000 ft 65% · **18,000 ft ≈ 50%** (395) · 24,000 ft 41% · **36,000 ft 25%** (187). Pressure falls faster at low altitude.
- Cabin altitude normally **6,000–8,000 ft** (max 8,000).
- Supplementary oxygen from **10,000 ft** (needed above 10,000–12,000 ft). Air only to 10,000 ft; air/O₂ mix 10,000–33,700 ft; **100% O₂** 33,700–40,000 ft; **100% O₂ under pressure above 40,000 ft**.
- Compensation begins at **6,000–7,000 ft**. Critical threshold without oxygen ≈ **22,000 ft**; 100% oxygen raises the safe altitude to ≈ **38,000 ft**.
- **Physiological deficient zone 12,000–50,000 ft** (ear/sinus block, breathlessness, dizziness, headache). **Partial space-equivalent 50,000 ft – 120 NM**; **total space-equivalent beyond 120 NM**: 100% O₂ no protection, sealed cabin or pressure suit; **body fluids boil above 63,000 ft** (Armstrong line).

### Body systems (p.652–657)
- **CNS** = brain + spinal cord. **Peripheral NS** links CNS to organs and muscles. **Autonomic NS** (part of the peripheral) runs involuntary functions: heart, breathing, temperature, BP, gut, sweating, **fight or flight**.
- Air path: nose/mouth → pharynx → larynx → trachea → bronchi → bronchioles → **alveoli**. **External respiration**: lungs ↔ blood; **internal (tissue)**: blood ↔ tissues.
- Breathing rate **14–18/min**; regulated by **CO₂ level** in the blood.
- Heart: right side → lungs, **left ventricle → aorta → body**. Systole contracts, diastole relaxes. Cardiac output ≈ **5 L/min** (72 bpm × 70 mL). Pulse **60–80** at rest; adrenaline raises it.
- Blood: plasma + formed elements. **RBCs** (> 90% of formed elements, no nucleus) carry **haemoglobin** (iron); WBCs fight infection; **platelets** clot.
- Angina/heart attack: coronary artery blocked (plaque, clot). Risks: genes, male, age, diabetes, BP, smoking, fat, LDL, inactivity, stress, obesity, alcohol.

### Hypoxia (p.659–660)
- Mild: inattention, poor judgment, poor coordination; severe: coma, death within minutes.
- Types: **hypoxic** (low O₂ partial pressure, altitude); **anaemic** (blood can't carry it: bleeding, anaemia, **CO poisoning**); **stagnant / ischaemic** (poor flow: cold, low BP, **high G**; oxygen helps little); **histotoxic** (cells can't use it: cyanide, drugs, **alcohol**).
- Onset insidious, marked **above 10,000 ft**; **night vision impaired from 5,000 ft**. Symptoms: **euphoria**, impaired judgment, headache, tingling, tunnel vision, **cyanosis**, formication, unusual fatigue, unconsciousness.
- Action: **oxygen**, **descend below 10,000 ft** (or MSA if higher).
- Smokers feel hypoxia lower (**7,000 ft** vs 10,000 ft); a pack a day cuts O₂ capacity **5–8%**.

### CO and hyperventilation (p.661–662)
- **CO**: colourless, odourless; Hb takes it up **> 200×** more readily than O₂; piston exhaust up to **9% CO**. Symptoms: poor concentration, headache, dizziness, nausea, **cherry-red lips**. Action: **heater off, vents open**, oxygen, land ASAP, see a doctor.
- **Hyperventilation**: over-breathing, **CO₂ too low**. Causes: fear, anxiety, intense concentration, motion sickness, heat, vibration, G. Symptoms: dizziness, **tingling**, visual disturbance, hot/cold, spasms, unconsciousness. Treat: **100% O₂** (if hypoxia, better in 3–4 breaths); breathe slowly **10–12/min**; **below 10,000 ft assume hyperventilation**; paper bag; calm and give a task.

### Alcohol, BP, blood donation (p.663–665)
- Allow **24 h** bottle to throttle. At **6,000 ft one drink = two** at sea level. Eliminated at **0.015% BAC per hour** (≈ one drink/h); coffee doesn't help. No hangover or masked hangover. Unit: half pint beer, glass of wine, measure of spirits. Max: men **5/day, 21/week**; women **3/day, 14/week**. **40 mg/100 mL** → significantly more errors; you may feel you're doing better.
- BP **120/80** normal (systolic/diastolic); measured in the upper arm. **Hypertension = major cause of unfitness**. Baroreceptor reflex adjusts BP (standing up, fright, running).
- Blood donation: active pilots shouldn't; if done, wait **48 h**.

### TUC and decompression (p.665–668)
- **TUC/EPT**: time to function usefully after oxygen is lost. FL180 20–30 min · FL250 3–5 min · FL300 1–2 min · **FL350 30 s–1 min** · FL400 15–20 s · FL430 9–12 s · FL500 8–10 s. **Rapid decompression roughly halves TUC**. At 35,000 ft about 33–54 s; halved again at 40,000 ft.
- No impairment without O₂ up to **8,000 ft**. Pressure halves at **18,000 ft**; DCS possible **from 18,000 ft**; unpressurised above **25,000 ft** → the **bends**. Risk up with fast climb, age, obesity, exercise, cold.
- Rapid decompression: **mist**, outward blast, gas expansion, air rushing from lungs. Slow: leaks or pressurisation fault.
- **DCS** (Henry's law, nitrogen bubbles): **bends** (joints), **creeps** (skin), **chokes** (lungs), **staggers** (brain). Treat: warm, **100% O₂**, **descend**, land, medical aid. No flying **12 h** after a rapid decompression.
- **Diving** with compressed air (≥ 30 ft): don't fly within **24 h**. Air evacuation of a diver: **lowest possible altitude**.
- Humidity: 40–60% normal; < 20% dryness; cabins 3–8% (humidifiers raise to ≈ 22%). Avoid coffee/tea (diuretics).
- Temperature: 20 °C comfortable; 30 °C heart rate, BP, sweating up; below ≈ 15 °C loss of feeling, poor fine control.
- Vibration: **1–4 Hz** breathing, neck; 4–10 Hz chest/abdomen; 8–12 Hz backache; 10–12 Hz headache, eyestrain; **30–40 Hz** vision (eyeball resonance). Airsickness **0.1–2 Hz**.

### Incapacitation and fits (p.669–670)
- **Obvious**: total loss (cardiac arrest, haemorrhage, epilepsy). **Subtle**: partial, gradual (hypoglycaemia, low BP, fatigue, drink, headache); often **more dangerous** because colleagues miss it. Periodic medicals reduce risk; frequency rises with age.
- **Grand mal**: generalised, loss of consciousness, convulsions. **Petit mal**: no loss of consciousness. **Any fit is an absolute bar to a licence.**
- **Faint/syncope**: standing up suddenly when hot/dehydrated, shock, blood loss, no food. **Vasovagal** (fear, sight of blood). No bar to flying if the cause is clear.

### The eye (p.671–676)
- Fastest and most sensitive sense. **Cornea**: 70–80% of focusing. **Iris/pupil**: light. **Lens**: final focus. **Retina**: ≈ **100 M rods, 6 M cones**.
- **Cones** at the **fovea**: detail and colour, day only, no night vision ("central vision"). **Rods** in the periphery: black and white, low light, movement.
- **Binocular** vision gives near depth and covers each eye's **blind spot** (where the optic nerve enters).
- Dark adaptation: bright **10 s**; dark **cones ≈ 7 min, rods ≈ 30 min**; ≈ 20 min moderate under dim red light (red distorts chart colours). Lost in seconds by a bright light (**close one eye**). Impaired above **5,000 ft** cabin altitude, by CO/smoking, lack of **vitamin A**, bright sun. At night look **15–20° off-centre**.
- **Empty field myopia**: eye rests at **1–2 m**; refocus near and far periodically.
- Flash blindness in storms: cockpit lights up, look inside, sunglasses, blinds. Sunglasses absorb **≥ 85%** (transmit 10–15%), absorb UV/IR, neutral colours; **no photochromic** lenses.
- **Myopia** (short sight): eyeball long, **concave** lens. **Hypermetropia** (long sight): **convex**. **Presbyopia**: lens stiffens at **40–50**, convex/bifocals. **Astigmatism**: misshapen cornea. **Glaucoma**: raised pressure, narrowing field, insidious. Spare spectacles must be carried. Colour blindness = cone defect.
- **Sharpness of central vision** falls with angle from the fovea and with: visual-system defects, **age, hypoxia, smoking, alcohol**, light available, object size and contours, distance, **contrast**, relative motion, drugs (p.675).

### The ear (p.676–679)
- Hearing and balance. **Eustachian tube** equalises the middle ear; trouble mostly on **descent** → **otic barotrauma**. Valsalva, swallow, yawn; slow or stop the descent, climb if needed. Never fly unable to clear.
- Hearing range **16–20,000 Hz**. Pitch = frequency, loudness = amplitude. **> 80 dB** may impair, **> 90 dB** measurably impairs; prolonged > 90 dB → **noise-induced hearing loss** (cochlea). Conductive deafness: ossicles/eardrum. **Presbycusis**: age, high tones first.
- Orientation: **vision** (most reliable), **vestibular** (otoliths: **linear** acceleration; semicircular canals: **angular**), **somatosensory** "seat of the pants" (unreliable). No fluid flow at steady rates: only eyes and instruments show a **steady turn**.

### Illusions (p.679–685)
- **Somatogravic** (otoliths): acceleration → **nose-up** (head-up illusion, night take-off, go-around) → pilot pushes; deceleration → **nose-down** → pilot pulls (stall); climb to level abruptly → **inversion** (tumbling backward) → pilot lowers nose.
- **Leans**: slow roll-in not sensed, abrupt roll-out feels like opposite bank. **Coriolis**: head movement in a prolonged turn, **most overwhelming**. **Graveyard spin** and **graveyard spiral** (pull back, tighten the spiral). **Elevator**: updraft → feels like climbing → pushes nose down.
- **Autokinesis**: stared-at light at night appears to move. **False horizon**: sloping cloud, dark scene with lights and stars. **Flicker vertigo**: **4–20 Hz** (propeller in sun, rotor, beacon in cloud); change RPM, turn away, shade, close eyes. Vection: circular, linear, roll.
- Clear air makes objects look **closer**; snow blowing into a headwind while taxiing feels **faster**.
- Approach: **feels high → flies low/shallow**: **upslope**, **narrow** runway, featureless terrain, rain, haze, **black hole**. **Feels low → flies high**: **downslope**, **wide** runway, **bright lights**.
- Unnoticed speed loss: aim point stays put while the aircraft sinks short. Ground proximity cues: speed, size, runway width, texture.
- Defence: **formal training**; illusions are natural; cross-check with other sources; in IMC **believe the instruments**, in VMC look at the horizon.
- **Motion sickness**: visual-vestibular mismatch; head still, fresh air, smooth flying.
- **Gestalt laws of perceptual organization**: **proximity, continuity, similarity, symmetry, simplicity, closure**; never build the mental model from wishes (p.679).
- **Outside references can mislead**: just after take-off, over water, in hilly or gently sloping terrain, a bank of sloping cloud, ground sloping down on the approach (p.680).

### G (p.685–686)
- 1 g = 9.81 m/s². Load factor **1/cos bank**: 45° **1.41 g**, **60° 2 g**, 70° ≈ 3 g. Aerobatic light aircraft ≈ +6.
- +G: heavy, immobile, **tunnel vision → grey-out → black-out**. Help: tense legs and abdomen, ease the pull, reclined seat. Tolerance cut by alcohol, smoking, fatigue, heat, obesity, sickness.
- −G: blood to the head → **red-out**; return to normal attitude.

### Health and fitness (p.687–691)
- **BMI** = kg / m²; normal **men 20–25**, **women 19–24**. Exercise to **double resting heart rate for 20 min, 3×/week**; lose weight by eating less; no appetite suppressants. Obesity → heart attack, hypertension, hypoxia lower, poor G tolerance, DCS, diabetes…
- Never fly on an empty stomach. Minerals: calcium, phosphorus, **iron** (haemoglobin). **Hypoglycaemia** slows reactions and cognition.
- **Gastroenteritis**: unfit even with medicine. Food contamination: unhygienic preparation, undercooked or stale meat, unwashed salad/fruit, seafood, local ice-cream and mayonnaise. Symptoms: nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, diarrhoea, loss of appetite; trapped gas expands at altitude (p.688). **Sinus block**: worst on descent; slow or stop descent. **Barodontalgia**: no flying **48–72 h** after major dental work. Dehydration impairs cognition.
- **No medication** unless cleared by an **aviation medicine specialist**. Why: the illness itself may disqualify; flight conditions change the body's reaction; side effects; effects outlast the course. Potent **analgesics** degrade performance. **Common ailments** feel worse in flight: **any doubt, stay on the ground**; keep a high standard of personal hygiene (p.689–690). Caffeine max ≈ **250–300 mg/day** (2–3 cups). Anaesthetic: **local 12 h**, **general 48 h**.
- **Hypothermia**: core below **95 °F** (normal 98.6 °F); shivering, then apathy. Severe: **82 °F** or lower; needs emergency care.
- Toxic hazards: furnishings, solvents, fuels, de-icing fluid, extinguishants, battery fumes.

### Exam traps
- Gas laws: Boyle trapped gas, **Henry DCS**, Dalton hypoxia, **Graham gas exchange**, Charles temperature.
- O₂ % never changes; **partial pressure** does. 18,000 ft ½, 36,000 ft ¼; 8,000 ft 76%.
- Oxygen from **10,000 ft**; 100% at 33,700 ft; pressure breathing above 40,000 ft; boiling above **63,000 ft**.
- Hypoxia types: **CO = anaemic**, **G = stagnant**, **alcohol/cyanide = histotoxic**. Night vision suffers from 5,000 ft.
- Hyperventilation below 10,000 ft: assume it isn't hypoxia; slow breathing 10–12/min.
- Alcohol 24 h; 6,000 ft doubles the effect. Blood donation 48 h; diving 24 h; decompression 12 h; local 12 h, general 48 h; dental 48–72 h.
- TUC FL350 30 s–1 min, halved by rapid decompression.
- Bends joints, creeps skin, chokes lungs, staggers brain.
- Rods 100 M, cones 6 M; rods 30 min, cones 7 min. Myopia concave, hypermetropia/presbyopia convex.
- Otoliths linear, canals angular; steady turn undetected. Ear trouble on **descent**.
- Upslope/narrow/black hole → **low** approach; downslope/wide/bright → **high**.
- 60° bank 2 g; 45° 1.41 g. Any epileptic fit bars a licence. Subtle incapacitation more dangerous.
